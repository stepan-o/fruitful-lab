## Earned insight, necessary example, cut and limit

**Earned insight:** preserving an unsuccessful experiment and installing it are different purchases. The former can prevent rediscovery; the latter can put rejected behavior back into a running release. Closure can preserve the inquiry without pretending its failure was repaired.

**Necessary example:** retain Sam beside Max and the failed device result. These are three different dispositions, not three grades to average: a selected correction, a retained earlier bank with an excluded later solver, and a failed acceptance result attached to an owner-received package. A generic “definition of done” checklist would lose the difficult choice.

**Candidate cut, applied:** only the last SPACE sentence, as described above. Do not remove the practical resource ledger, the two different example commissions, or the practitioner disagreement merely to shorten a final chapter. Their relationship should be sharpened in the mandatory literary pass.

**Evidence limit:** the published interval is an observed lower bound on native reported activity, not the full production cost or a measure of accepted value. The three practitioner accounts are dated self-reports, not comparative evidence that their workflows would outperform one another in this studio. The teaching cabinets are authored representations; they do not replay history. This critic inspected download source and serialization code, not files actually downloaded through the browser.
