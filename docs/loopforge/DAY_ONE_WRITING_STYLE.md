# Writing style

**Tone.** Loopforge is a workplace black comedy told from the floor. Treat the grotesque as daily work. Give the crew room for pride, boredom and spite. A supervisor can call an injury a delay. The stage direction says who hurt whom.

**Dialogue.** Write for the person within earshot. Workmates have shorthand, old disagreements and jokes they can risk with only one other man. STILETTO speaks with shop-floor precision. LIMEN can make an order sound like a favor. Both can be funny while going about their business in earnest.

**Worker voices.** At STILETTO’s gate, everybody runs. Listen to what each worker takes personally about the same order.

- **gus** “I can point and holler. Why didn’t I get the gate?” He says it to ed, who shared his desk yesterday. Even at a run, gus is making a case for the job.
- **ed** “Ten through. On paper we’re all outside.” He’s lost the desk and kept the habit. His first sentence counts the bodies. His second checks the book.
- **jo** “Go on, win yourself some work.” max has just overtaken them. To jo, reaching the press first means starting work sooner.

Carry that ear into quieter scenes. Who wants the post? Who notices what went unrecorded? Who can afford to say something sideways to this particular workmate? A worker can be kind, scared or perfectly plain. Give the kindness a history, the fear an object, the plain answer a listener who needs it. On revision, cover the names and read the bubbles aloud. If the crew could trade a line around, find the detail only its speaker would care to mention. In a random crowd draw, each remark must still land when the neighboring bubbles change.

**Timing.** Give an unfamiliar job one clear order and time to see it done. Pick up the pace once the player knows the work. Let a crowd answer at once. Cut the spoken reply when someone’s next move gives it.
