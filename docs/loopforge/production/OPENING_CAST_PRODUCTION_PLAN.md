## Recognition and hierarchy

Workers belong to the blue industrial family and remain subordinate to the supervisors in scale and visual emphasis. Each worker needs a distinct combination of large silhouette, head and shoulder shape, proportions, paint placement and bearing. Test identification at a glance in the actual full-screen room view, with names and speech bubbles hidden. Test front, side and rear views, crowd overlap and the real room lighting. Close-up beauty and small distinguishing scratches do not satisfy this criterion.

bob should receive attention through composition, behavior and recognizable features while remaining an ordinary worker within that hierarchy. Keep the approved supervisor colors and CATHEXIS proportions; do not regenerate supervisors for this task.

## A performance is a sequence

Compose actions with preparation, execution, response and settling. For example: approach the entrance, notice LIMEN, hesitate, stop, look towards a colleague, respond, return attention to the checkpoint. This is a staging example, not a new approved script or a fixed personality rule. Give speech a listener and coordinate gaze, turn, gesture and reply. Vary the timing according to the situation rather than randomizing repetitive gestures.

Loops belong to sustained actions such as walking and operating machinery. Do not leave the cast cycling through scratch, clap or startled gestures. Provide entry and exit motion, planted feet, continuous root movement and interruption handling. Keep animation intent separate from clip selection so a scripted rehearsal and later BDI agents can use the same performance system. Preserve speaker/hearer identity and causal effects for speech bubbles and responses.

Retarget the accepted worker library where appropriate, then validate each new proportion. Earlier contact corrections are not automatically valid on a new body. Check elbow and wrist limits, hand/head and hand/prop contact, leg crossings, floor penetration and transitions. Use custom motion only for remaining gaps; generated clips still require inspection and local cleanup.

## Performance and completion

Use the existing character review and scene systems. Measure bob plus LIMEN first, then the ten-worker opening and the wider cast stress scene on this actual older computer. Keep fixed camera paths, resolution, settings and warm-up for comparable runs. Report frame-time distribution and stalls, CPU/GPU costs where available, animation update cost, draw calls, triangles, texture memory and loading. Reuse the project's acceptance criteria and report failures as failures. Other physical devices require their own runs.

Build separate levels of detail and texture sizes for close-ups and floor views, preserving identity before decorative detail.
