# Second studio interview — 3D Artist

## Q1 — What is the most useful result you personally contributed this cycle? Describe the player/team outcome, what you actually inspected, and the evidence that could contradict your assessment.

My most useful contribution was making the Sam support decision more specific. The saved geometry contained real surface triangles, so rejecting them merely because their dominant joint names differed was not a physical diagnosis. I compared those seven triangles with the actual post face and found that accepting the larger measurement rectangle’s maximum would leave the proposed post at least 20.4 mm ahead of the inspected surface beneath it. That gave the team a reason to change the measurement rather than repeat the same attempt. It did not produce a working mount or an improvement visible to the player. A complete surface envelope could show an omitted supporting triangle; an actual fitted action could resolve the remaining question. My report explicitly left those possibilities open.

## Q2 — Which decision of your own worked poorly? Give one concrete instance, its consequence, what you knew then, and what you would change. Separate your responsibility from dependencies outside your control.

I was too willing to make a local geometry answer the finish line. In the support review I examined the seven supplied triangles accurately, but that bounded set could not establish the complete attachment patch. My recommendation therefore handed the central feasibility question back to another task. I knew the selection omitted some regions and that only one pose was saved. I should have made the required support patch and shared acceptance evidence the organising question from the outset, including in the earlier holder proposals. I own that framing. I do not own the scheduling restriction on full motion, and I should not have bypassed it to manufacture an answer.

## Q3 — Describe one cross-role exchange that improved the game and one that created avoidable delay or rework. What made the difference? Name the work and evidence, not just the participants.

Animator’s saved actual-loader vertices and fixed footprint made the support exchange useful: we could compare the same geometry instead of trading assumptions about where a chest socket ought to be. The shared temporal frame packet has also improved this review. It exposes the arms-out departure across several positions while another worker travels with lowered arms, which argues for tracing a particular actor and cue before blaming every rig. I cannot claim either review has already improved shipped pixels. The avoidable rework was the earlier split between building a plausible holder and proving that the real hand and body could use it. Reproducible files helped custody, but did not close that interface. A single complete interaction should have been the shared deliverable.

## Q4 — What did actual integrated footage or play reveal that source checks or isolated craft work did not? If you have not inspected moving evidence, say so and distinguish still-frame judgment from motion judgment.

I inspected captured stills and sampled temporal sheets, not continuous moving playback. They revealed how much of the asset work disappears behind presentation: speech covers LIMEN’s skull and STILETTO’s head, the operator is cropped during a control hold, and the facade fills almost the whole departure image at one timestamp. None of those is repaired by adding polygons. The images also showed a success that source validation cannot communicate: Bob’s paired lenses, the blue workforce, the steel supervisor and STILETTO’s long black/red silhouette remain distinct in the actual rooms. Timing, fluidity, exact contact and frame-rate quality remain outside my inspection.

## Q5 — The first retrospective called for one owned outcome, decisive evidence and a change-of-approach decision after a failed assumption. What concrete behavior in this cycle followed or missed that lesson? What should good work in your domain mean now?

The surface review followed the first retrospective’s change-of-approach lesson when it returned CHANGE instead of removing the mixed-joint guard and pretending the result passed. It missed the stronger version of the lesson because the outcome remained a bounded diagnostic, not a demonstrated usable attachment. This cycle’s owner-approved Day1 slice gives us a better unit of judgment: the character approaches, acts, changes something and leaves in the actual scene. Good 3D work now means preserving identity and believable construction through that whole chain, with an explicit handoff to the action and renderer owners. A valid mesh or a correct triangle count is supporting evidence, not the result.

## Q6 — Choose one improvement you would keep for the next cycle. Who owns it, when should it happen, and what observable result would show it helped? Include any tradeoff or disagreement with the current production approach.

I would keep one shared contact-and-readability gate before any new attachment design. Animator should own the complete interaction outcome, with 3D supplying the body/prop interface and Engine supplying the actual runtime view; Producer schedules the bounded check. It should happen before variants or material polish. It helped if the first review can show the contact patch, approach, transfer, release and intended-camera image, and a failed assumption ends that method without a succession of merely local passes. The tradeoff is a little more coordination before construction. I prefer that to an inexpensive prop whose fit is expensive to discover later. This is a proposed next-cycle practice, not a restart of Sam precision work.

## Q7 — What can you establish about the rendered models/materials/props in the integrated slice, and what remains outside your inspection? What should have happened differently in the earlier Sam-support work?

I can establish visible construction and contrast in the inspected R10 images: the major cast signatures survive, the admission gate reads as a threshold, the conveyor has distinguishable housings and controls, and the brain and press platen are visible at particular action frames. I cannot establish every material response, hidden face, skin weight, tool clearance, detail tier or mobile/performance condition from that evidence. The owner approved the demonstrated slice, and I am not asking to reopen that approval. Earlier Sam work should have started with the real attachment surface and whole-hand envelope, plus the deformation relative to the intended parent through an actual action. The card fitting an empty box was never the same claim as a worker being able to use it.
