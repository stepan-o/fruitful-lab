Producer: the game was the missing test

A player can now enter the factory, work through the tutorial, bring the line into operation, produce three workpieces and watch everyone leave for the night. The owner watched the live slice and approved it. That is the useful result of this cycle. It belongs to the demonstrated build; later corrections still need their own checks.

My central failure was allowing production to organize around evidence of preparation instead of a dependable way to experience the game. I owned the delivery plan. I repeatedly accepted source packets, exact hashes, schedules and conditional requests while the studio could not reliably play the integrated result. Those artifacts often contained useful work. They did not close the uncertainty that mattered.

This repeated the first retrospective’s lesson. I had proposed one owned outcome, decisive evidence and a change of approach when an assumption failed. In this cycle I kept refining the machinery around the failed assumption. More precise accounting did not give the studio a browser. More detailed limits did not make an hourly service compatible with a short start window. Working native game access should have been an early delivery dependency, resolved before I multiplied downstream acceptance plans.

Two decisions make the failure concrete. A diagnostic did not start because available memory was about 120 MiB below a 2560 MiB threshold. That established a configured guard miss, not an out-of-memory failure or demonstrated danger. I had not established why that exact threshold was necessary for that operation. A later native validation was interrupted because its first child started 1.553 seconds after an internal cutoff, despite the wrapper starting before it. That was a scheduling decision, not a compiler failure. Recording those distinctions honestly was necessary. It did not recover the lost opportunity. The foreground replay request’s 78-second start allowance was similarly incompatible with the support arrangement I was using.

I also let task-token allocations become production decisions. The owner repeatedly supplied more headroom before removing sprint and role ceilings. I should have sized useful work and recovery from the actual accounting model, rather than translating an approaching tranche into a smaller deliverable. Preserving cumulative cached-input accounting remains right. Treating a chosen allowance as a reason to avoid necessary quality evidence was wrong. The statistics report processing, including reused context; they are not a measure of newly written work, and I cannot turn missing cost information into a monetary estimate.

The team nevertheless produced a coherent day. Engine maintained one shared action history across player controls and the automatic sequence. The downloaded ending records 22 completed actions, three outputs, twelve departed characters and 590 retained events. Ed holds Bob’s identification while Bob remains unaware. These are meaningful shared-world facts. Systems and Level also clarified the boundary between four current controls and future duties, and between completed workpieces and an economy that has not been implemented. Their later-day proposal became stronger when attending a session was compared with postponing it and a particular worker’s later initiative supplied the return. That was useful design progress, not a claim that those branches already ran.

The decisive change came when direct native browser operation worked. I could use visible controls, capture the result and return exact moments to the crafts. The first complete timeline exposed dialogue over heads, misleading phase labels, cropped instruction, arms-out movement and a slow staffed interval. Source checks had not settled whether a player could see or understand the actions. As Engine put it, “The exported state was progressing correctly while the interface told the wrong story.”

The hearing findings taught the same lesson from another direction. Sixteen turns lacked at least one addressed listener’s receipt. I briefly selected Writer’s first response as an audience change before the later dependency analysis arrived. Writer then showed that four nonreceipts were genuine: Jo acted on later cues he did receive, and Bob responded to his own work and the visible crew rather than an unheard count. I withdrew my unnecessary audience changes. The selected correction keeps every original word and addressee, stages twelve required exchanges physically and preserves four nonreceipts. I own that premature adoption. A recipient list is not enough; I needed to ask which later action depended on hearing it.

The fresh interviews support a change in production, but they do not give me nine interchangeable endorsements. Seven specialists prioritize an early integrated encounter check. Systems instead selects a matched case without the proposed addition, before the continuation is polished. Writer selects an explicit recipient and knowledge check before script handoff. Those are distinct tests of value, physical communication and presentation. They should meet in one owned encounter, rather than become three additional approval queues.

The interviews also put limits on the review I had arranged. All nine specialists disclose still or sampled-frame inspection rather than continuous playback. The images exposed real defects, but they do not establish impact weight, grip, walking rhythm or sustained performance. Critic acknowledges missing two adjacent drawing defects while closing five named storyboard findings. Visual acknowledges that its earlier anchor-first recommendation was too narrow for a scene whose picture itself changed size. The corrective lesson is to inspect the immediate surroundings and the complete exchange, not to answer every miss with a larger inventory.

There were useful disagreements. Critic and Level reject a separate approval ritual for every small change. Visual accepts losing some balloon-to-body connection in return for a stable picture and readable rail. 3D recommends preserving the approved meshes because the witnessed failures do not establish a need to rebuild them. I will retain those limits. Owner approval should protect the coherent cast and direction from an indefinite polish cycle while exact technical defects receive corrections.

My own recording also has limits. I paused during the staffed slowdown, compared callbacks and resumed with a capture gap. Several conditions changed during recovery. I cannot declare the host, recorder or game the cause, or call that recording an uninterrupted benchmark. The current native craft tests pass the selected hearing and ending regressions; a separate Focus lint failure led to a narrow correction. Neither result supplies corrected browser pixels or a performance pass.

Good work now means a contribution that changes what the player can perceive or do, with a clear account of what was checked. A clean type check, strong drawing, well-written exchange and useful critique are different results. My responsibility is to join them into the experience and make a continue, change or stop decision on the assumption that matters.

For the next encounter, Producer will operate the ordinary browser path early. Engine will supply the same build’s state and action record. Director, the affected craft and Critic will inspect the action, recipient and consequence before broad variants are produced. Writer’s listener check and Systems’ comparison without the proposed addition will enter where they are relevant. Heavy work will follow actual cleanup and dependencies, without arbitrary short admission windows. The final replay and quiet performance comparison remain separate obligations. A failed first encounter should change the method while it is still cheap to change—not produce a more elaborate description of the same blocker.

This account was completed after the nine fresh interviews and before reading Root’s publication account or receiving the separate agentic-systems assessment. Their conclusions may differ. The full questions, answers, counted overview and statistics remain alongside this reflection so that those differences can be examined.
