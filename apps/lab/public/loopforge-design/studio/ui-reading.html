UI DESIGNER handoff

21 November 2026 · Preliminary source study, not a completed interface audit

Use this with the role brief and first assignment. Study current behavior first, then compare prior design intent and outside references. A source description, screenshot or passing check is evidence for a specific claim, not a substitute for playing the relevant interaction.

Start with the current player

Loopforge entrance → current Days 1–2 player. Inspect the ordinary entry and the separate Day 2 entry, settings, room inspection, crew/control selection, feedback and return paths. Record the actual build and what was reachable. Keep the standalone Days 2–4 player as a related implementation reference, not an interchangeable claim about the integrated route.

At source baseline d883422e, GameClient connects the menu to IntroPlayer, ProducerConsole, LeadershipCall and ShiftFloor. The source retains an older first-turn review path. A short live inspection on 21 November reached the current menu and Day 2 leadership-waiting console, which still uses six camera frames, factory counters and physical receiver/selector/lever imagery. Full runtime, mobile and accessibility review remain the first designer's work. Do not classify every older console element as obsolete merely because its design document is dated October.

  • Current interface components: GameClient, ProducerConsole, LeadershipCall, ThemeSettings and the surrounding styles. Read the relevant files in bounded sections.
  • Current shift viewer and contracts: inspect commands, confirmed state, selection, speech and lifecycle before proposing their UI.
  • ENGINE brief and runtime handoff: implementation evidence and remaining findings, with their dates preserved.
  • KVP-0001 and engine boundary: presentation interprets permitted state and submits intentions; it does not own world truth.

The new UI should make the causal relationship between player action and confirmed result clearer. It must not make a rejected command appear successful, expose a hidden belief as fact or conceal the reason a currently supported command is unavailable. Exact contract changes are proposals for ENGINE and SYSTEMS, not existing capabilities.

Project vision and approved material

Read player desires, the intro, approved floor and rehearsal, pixel-art plan and calibration, supervisor × room, and the SYSTEMS brief. Use current approved artwork in context; distinguish it from material still awaiting owner review.

The approved factory is a shared spatial source. Close framing can improve recognition without changing geometry. A readable speech bubble can improve presentation without rewriting dialogue or stretching the edit. Where those goals conflict, show the conflict to the owner instead of quietly changing the approved sequence.

Marvel Rivals: study the design decisions

This is the owner's central external UI reference. Start with the existing Sanctuary Economics comparison, especially its distinction between a gallery, a searchable roster and a time-sensitive selection screen. It contains credited visual sources and an official Art Vision player. Treat that chapter's interpretations as arguments to evaluate, not proof of designer intent or measured usability.

Use the developers' Art Vision Vol. 01 and official video as primary material. The official page introduces the visual team's design process. This onboarding study verified that source and inspected the existing chapter's analysis; it does not claim a fresh frame-by-frame viewing of the video. The designer should inspect the relevant sequences and cite timestamps for motion claims.

A screenshot cannot demonstrate task speed or touch usability. Do not infer that a design is better merely because it resembles the reference. Preserve credited study images as references; use original or cleared assets for Loopforge's product.

Security events on the floor. The owner wants dialogue to carry most of the checkpoint, with occasional expandable events. Leave ordinary remarks with their speakers. For a significant incident, place one selectable marker at its physical subject. In the proposed missing-ID study, the exchange at the desk stays visible. LIMEN calls for both desks to hold after bob says he has no card. A separate “Missing ID · bob” marker at the active desk invites inspection. Opening it shows the person, the known issue and any currently available response. A short event can open in a compact sheet that keeps the desk visible; a more involved incident can occupy a dedicated view with a clear return to its source. The Producer spends an intervention only by choosing an available action. Opening or dismissing the event preserves the shift’s existing time behavior. If the case resolves while the player reads, reflect that outcome and retire the expired action. The floor shows bob’s pass and his passage when those events actually occur.

Frostpunk and the missing-ID event

The owner named the original Frostpunk as a reference for events. Its city view gives everyday speech and a matter for the Captain different amounts of attention. In the dialogue capture, three residents’ remarks share the city with the construction and shift indicators. A triggered event can instead appear as a timed city marker that the player opens. Expiry behavior varies by event. City dialogue capture · Event marker behavior documented by players

In the “Facing starvation” screen, a crowd faces the Captain. The short petition sits over their image. Selecting the more ambitious response reveals the commitment to feed everyone in three days and stockpile food. The incident and the cost of answering it are both legible before the player commits. The related “People are hungry” request gives the Captain a choice of two food pledges or leaving the issue unresolved. The pledge becomes work the city must deliver. First composition to test. On desktop and phone, compare the same two-ready-station wide shot with the station strip omitted. Keep the camera identity and an accessible way to inspect a station. Designing Games: verified applications

Tynan Sylvester, Designing Games (2013), chapter 9, Interface. Printed page numbers below refer to the owner's copy; PDF numbers are one-based viewer pages. The cited passages were read during onboarding preparation. The PDF remains local under the shared instructions.

PassageApplication to this commissionHow to judge it
