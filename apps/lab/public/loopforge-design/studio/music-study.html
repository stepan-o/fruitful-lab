Loopforge · owner-directed Codex studio

One factory, one evolving theme

MUSIC DIRECTOR handoff

20 November 2026 · Commissioning study for MUSIC DIRECTOR

Status: researched creative direction and source inspection, not a listening report or implemented music engine. The owner's requirements are fixed where identified below; the musical and technical choices are proposals for the director to test with the owner. No reference recording is copied or redistributed here.

The musical thesis

A factory keeps repeating, but the people inside it change what repetition means. Let Loopforge's theme do the same. A familiar descending fragment, a persistent pulse and a recognizable harmonic world survive each move from menu to room. What changes is weight, space, instrumentation and the way phrases answer one another. The machine can be exhilarating without declaring every productive act virtuous; a supervisor can be imposing without a villain chord deciding the player's judgment.

The cultural frame is the owner's remembered threshold of networked life: unfamiliar machines becoming social places, discovery mixed with friction. Treat this as a creative feeling, not a literal claim that technology began in the 1990s. DnB supplies the rolling current; dubstep supplies Conveyor's mass and suspension. Both belong to an original theme.

What the book changes

Tynan Sylvester, Designing Games (2013), chapter 1, “Engines of Experience,” section “Emotion Through Music,” printed pp. 24–25 (PDF pp. 32–33), describes music as an emotional layer and cautions that exaggerated nonmusical effects can become counterproductive. Applied here: make the continuous score carry atmosphere while leaving mechanical and speech cues legible. Do not reward every produced unit with a flourish. Test the same interaction with music alone, effects alone and the mix: the mood should change without changing the apparent game outcome. Page 25's following discussion of spectacle reinforces restraint; loudness is not a substitute for earned consequence.

The relevant pages were read in the owner's local reference PDF. Future decisions need their own verified citations; do not reuse these pages as blanket support for tempo, latency or licensing claims.

The opening and the theme's grammar

Owner requirement: a rapid minor-scale descent lands on an off-note. Compose a new short seed; test a chromatic landing against an unresolved scale tension. Specify the interval, duration and resolution after listening. Do not replay the entire opening every time a menu changes. A cold start can introduce it; later returns can use its last two notes, rhythmic contour or harmonic implication.

Proposed palette: weighty but controlled sub, tactile chopped breaks, soft electric-key voicings, suspended pads, sparse metallic pitched accents and occasional breath-like texture. Humanized articulation and well-placed silence matter more than a large instrument list. First audition should be instrumental so speech remains readable; any vocal texture needs separate clearance and intelligibility review.

For jazz richness, propose one shared tonal center with compatible modal colors, extended chord voicings that avoid the bass register, controlled voice leading, syncopated answers and uneven phrase emphasis. Reserve a small number of intentional tensions. Define which bass, harmony and motif variants can coexist; random chord selection over arbitrary generated stems is not improvisation.

The engine needs memory of recent phrases and a small arrangement arc: establish, vary, thin, answer, return. Limit simultaneous fills, leave room for speech and prevent consecutive identical selections where alternatives exist. Seeded selection makes an audition reproducible without making musical RNG part of authoritative game RNG. Repeatability is a debugging aid; listening decides whether variation feels alive.

One clock or a deliberate bridge

There is a real choice to audition. At 170 BPM, a half-time backbeat suggests 85 BPM; it does not become a 140 BPM dubstep groove. Candidate A keeps a shared 170/85 grid for easy interlocking. Candidate B gives Conveyor a 140/70 center and uses an authored breakdown/transition segment before the new downbeat. Candidate C uses a different shared tempo after listening. Do not stretch whole generated mixes invisibly and call them equivalent.

The first listening comparison should hold motif, duration and approximate level constant. Compare both genre identity and continuity. The director should recommend an approach, then let the owner choose. Keep source BPM, measured BPM, bar length, key and any processing separate in the manifest.

Adaptive composition before engine integration

Use two complementary operations: add/remove compatible layers inside a musical passage, and move between authored passages at designated entry points. Michael Sweet's Berklee study of interactive scoring, sections 1–2, describes horizontal resequencing and vertical remixing. Here the proposal is a small transition graph with compatible entry/exit markers, not a requirement to install middleware.

Proposed flow: manual fixtures now / game adapter later → validated MusicInputs → arrangement state and transition policy → shared audio transport and mixer → audible result. In parallel, the existing game continues its own fixed-step simulation. Sound off must change no gameplay result.

Current code: reuse selectively

Inspection baseline: master bc7d42b8, PR #179. These are source findings, not fresh audio auditions.

  • apps/lab/lib/loopforge/landing-audio.ts: one user-activated menu effect; its decay may survive client navigation; it observes sound preference and document visibility. Reuse the lifecycle lessons, not the one-shot model as a music transport.
  • apps/lab/components/loopforge/first-shift/audio.ts and recorded-audio.ts: older first-shift procedural machine sounds and a small bounded recorded-sample loader. Inspect before reuse; this does not show that the current 2D playthrough consumes that entire path.
  • apps/lab/lib/loopforge/shifts/viewer.ts: current viewer has a welcome-voice element. Preserve speech and its controls when adding a music bus.
  • docs/loopforge/game-design/sound-library.json: existing cropped effects, provenance and gaps; explicitly distinguishes atmosphere from future soundtrack. Its historical “wired” labels require checking against the current route.
  • The Sound library, ENGINE handoff and legacy studio publications are references, not instructions to restart the retired studio.

Runtime proposal and performance questions

Keep a single session music host across supported in-app surfaces. Inventory Next client navigation, rewritten static readers, the embedded player and standalone player first: a full-document navigation or separate iframe cannot inherit an AudioContext by assumption. Propose a persistent shell where appropriate; otherwise label the interruption and resume behavior honestly. Do not promise uninterrupted sound across browser reloads or unrelated sites.

Use pre-generated, locally served and validated assets. No model request should be required to enter a room, and no provider credential belongs in frontend code. Trigger the audio context through explicit user interaction, preserve mute preferences, and define hidden-tab, device interruption, load failure and multiple-instance behavior. Keep music, speech and effects separately mixable under the master control.

Chris Wilson's “A tale of two clocks” explains why JavaScript callback timing alone is unsuitable for precise audio scheduling. MDN's sequencer example provides the audio-context scheduling and buffer primitives. ENGINE should schedule audio against the audio clock with bounded lookahead; a frame-rate timer must not become the beat clock.

Preload the active arrangement and its imminent transition, not the whole catalog. Propose and measure caps for simultaneous layers, decoded buffers, transition overlap and initial download. Decoded float PCM costs roughly seconds × sample rate × channels × 4 bytes: four stereo 30-second layers at 48 kHz occupy about 46 MB before overhead. Compressed transfer size does not establish runtime memory. Test narrow/mobile contexts, long playback, rapid menu changes, mute/resume and sustained simulation load. Report laboratory conditions and listening evidence separately.

First useful experiment

After scope and budget approval, make a small coherent family: original theme seed, one main-menu passage, one Options break variation, one Conveyor passage and the required bridge. Prefer two meaningful alternatives to dozens of unconstrained generations. Include seam tests, repeated loops and a normal-speed continuous traversal. Full mix variants are acceptable if phase-compatible stems are unavailable; do not claim stem control that the exports cannot support.

Record entry/exit bars, loop region and tail, verified tempo/key, compatible layers, loudness/peak checks, file hashes, model/request IDs, editing history, permissions and owner review. Separate generated output, prepared loop, integrated playback and accepted music. The first director's immediate deliverable remains the Music engine proposal tab; this experiment belongs to the subsequent approved build.
