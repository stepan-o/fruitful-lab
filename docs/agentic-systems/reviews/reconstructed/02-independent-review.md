## What earns its place

**Earned insight:** a missed receipt is not intrinsically a defect. Its value depends on whether a later action legitimately requires that information. The wrong repair can improve a delivery count and reduce the production's value. The Writer/Director case earns this judgment with an admission, selected repairs and a separate later regression record.

**Indispensable example:** preserve the card across the custody and hearing studies. The same object lets the reader distinguish location, observation and testimony without learning another scenario. A false floor claim is especially useful because it makes successful receipt visibly different from truth. The two-worker factory also remains necessary: an extra worker exposes reservation independently from presence and custody.

**Candidate cut:** shorten the first three sentences of the late paragraph beginning “The economic decision in the studio's correction is unusually concrete.” The count and rejected radius repair have already been narrated. Retain the interpretation that blanket repair buys delivery at the expense of information boundaries and spatial logic, plus the explicit limit that the record does not establish hours saved. This is a local tightening, not a reason to reopen the chapter structure.

**Precision correction:** “Keeping that promise costs implementation and tests once” overstates the saving. The shared contract centralizes implementation and checks, but does not make maintenance a one-time expense. Prefer a formulation about centralizing that burden instead of asking each new selector to improvise it.

**Good content lost:** the old chapter 08's explicit commission—name the later decisions that require a line, its essential recipient, and intentional silences—was a useful operational checklist. The new second exercise mostly preserves it through the dependent action and deliberate nonreceipt. It could be restored as one short exercise instruction, but another explanatory paragraph is unnecessary. The old reminder that tests cannot certify graceful walking or enjoyment is safely removed here: chapter 1 owns that distinction and this chapter still qualifies its hearing evidence. No original graphic, real screenshot, primary comparison, offline artifact or distinct exercise was lost.
