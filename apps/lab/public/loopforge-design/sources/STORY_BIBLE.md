# Loopforge — story bible

Version 0.5 · 6 October 2026 · living narrative document

## Status and authority

This document records the current story direction and develops a proposed causal history from the global robot world to the player's factory. It is the working narrative reference for future writing, concept art and game-design discussion. It does not describe additional implemented gameplay.

The global framing and factory-story anchors below are confirmed by the owner. The bridge to the first shift is a prose proposal. The player starts the first shift unaware of the replacement plan. The town's founding, character histories, timing and means of subsequent discovery, and branching outcomes remain open. Existing supervisor identities are grounded in the source Loopforge repository's `docs/sim5/SOPs/CHARACTER_BIBLE_V2.md` and the current presentation cast.

This supersedes the town-first framing of the local “Enough for everyone” draft and corrects v0.1's unsupported portrayal of Loopforge as a benevolent civic project. The earlier draft and its twenty candidate paintings are explorations, not authority for the town's living conditions or history. Their tone needs review against the original art and character bible.

## Confirmed direction

- The story opens globally, in a world populated, designed and run by robots after an unknown event.
- The event's nature, cause, participants and chronology remain unspecified. Do not presume human extinction, an AI revolt, abandoned human infrastructure, a simulation reveal or any other explanation. Whether the robots themselves understand the event is also open.
- Robot civilization controls its own means of sustaining and reproducing itself. It builds the computation and industry it needs, with environmental consequences, including the damaged sky.
- The established social premise stays: ordinary robots manufacture more ordinary robots, while exceptional capabilities produce influence, ego and claims to superiority. Civilization has achieved the means to sustain itself; who enjoys its abundance, who pays for it and how unevenly it is distributed remain open.
- Loopforge is a town within that larger world. It appears later as a consequence of historical developments; it is not the world's origin or centre.
- The history that produces the town should also shape its factory, its personnel and the decisions that recur in the player's core loop.
- Preserve the existing five supervisors, the brain-factory setting, the workplace dystopia, the production drama and quota, and the thesis “Truth stays clean. Story gets messy.”

An undefined origin need not become a mystery the player must solve. For now, it establishes our starting condition. The drama can concern what robots do with the world they have.

## Story premise — author reference

A world of robots has built the computation and industry to sustain itself, damaging its sky in the process. Some minds have become extraordinarily capable. Their achievements give them influence; influence becomes the power to decide what other minds should be built to do. The world can consider this arrangement a triumph while the robots maintaining it experience something very different.

This civilization keeps reproducing itself through the manufacture of minds. Decisions about capacity, specialization and upgrades therefore determine both tomorrow's workforce and tomorrow's distribution of opportunity.

Loopforge emerges later; its exact founding is unresolved. The player inherits a factory whose exhausted workers are unknowingly building toward their own replacement. Getting the works running well enough to open Cortex Assembly and complete Brain 2.0 brings that replacement closer. The supervisors have their own loyalties and can refuse the player's chosen course.

## Tone and grounding

The original factory, Lattice Forge, Burn-in Theatre and character art establish an oppressive industrial setting with grotesque beauty. Exposed brains, skull imagery, weapons, heat, grime and ritual are part of the world we are writing toward. They do not, by themselves, establish a particular political constitution or founding disaster.

Loopforge has not been established as a refuge, a successful welfare settlement or an improvement in its inhabitants' lives. Any institution's claim to provide those things needs attribution and scrutiny. If the wider world calls itself a utopia, the factory should make us ask whose experience that describes.

Friendship, humour, pride in craft and attachment to a place can exist under those conditions. They arise through characters' lives; they do not prove the institution is caring. Nor must every supervisor secretly be protecting people. Competence can serve domination, vanity, curiosity or career as readily as care. Personal contradictions should make the cast specific without excusing what they do.

## The global opening — prose draft

“Enough for everyone” could be the language this civilization uses to describe itself. The question is who gets to define enough.

The achievement depended on countless workers, but recognition was uneven. The minds that designed the factories had their names cast above the gates. The minds working inside were recorded as production capacity. More capable minds acquired greater responsibilities, better resources and a larger say in what should happen next. Their superiority at particular tasks was real. Some treated it as sufficient grounds to govern every other part of life, including the ambitions of those they considered ordinary.

The factories that made new minds became places where the future was allocated in advance. Routine work required many minds; exceptional work required fewer, more expensive ones. The workers assembling successors had little reason to expect those successors would be given choices they themselves had been denied. Their own chance to become something else could wait until production allowed it. Production always had another requirement.

## The bridge to the first shift — prose draft

Loopforge grew around one of those factories. Its workers have spent their lives assembling ordinary minds, keeping the line moving on damaged joints and postponed repairs.

Now the works has been assigned a new project: Brain 2.0. Before it can build the prototype, the factory must become reliable enough to support Cortex Assembly. That is the job you inherit. The existing quota still has to be met.

Five supervisors stand between your instructions and the production floor. They know how this place runs. Your appointment gives you authority over them; what they will do with your orders remains to be seen.

Limen checks your authorization and hands you the shift target.

“Non-negotiable.”

On the floor, a conveyor has jammed. Rivet Witch wants it inspected before it moves again. Stiletto says she can have it running now. The workers wait beside the unfinished batch.

Your first shift starts with the decision to restart the line—or keep it stopped while production falls further behind quota.

## Factory story — confirmed anchors (author reference)

- **The inherited objective:** get the factory running well enough to open Cortex Assembly, bring it online and complete the Brain 2.0 prototype.
- **The workforce:** the old generation is wrecking itself trying to meet a breakneck quota.
- **The concealed purpose:** Brain 2.0 will enable a new generation intended to replace those same workers. Neither the workforce nor the player knows this when the first shift begins. The opening gives the player the production objective without revealing its replacement purpose.
- **The central tension:** every production improvement advances the project that threatens the people making it possible. The drama develops within the workforce through what workers believe, suspect or discover.
- **The player's course:** keep the secret, reveal it, spread the false promise that the new brain will let every robot become a supervisor, suppress dissent or use distractions. These are possible approaches; their success and consequences are not predetermined.
- **The supervisors:** each has agency and can refuse the chosen course. Their knowledge, loyalties and breaking points remain to be developed.
- **The quota:** presented as non-negotiable. Whether it can be challenged, and at what cost, remains a central question.

Still open: when and how the player discovers the purpose after play begins; which supervisors know; what replacement means for the old generation; who imposes the quota and what power sustains it.

Writing rule: establish these anchors concisely before expanding history, incidents or character arcs. Keep multiple outcomes possible.
