## 1. Media Direction Fit

Decision: `media-ideas-v1.md` supports the approved V6 thesis and critic constraints.

The media file is aligned with the final public thesis:

```txt
Marigold meaning changes by where the flower is placed.
```

The strongest concepts translate that idea visually through altar, path, grave, garland, doorway, garden, table, and wallpaper placements. The direction also preserves the Editor/Critic constraints from V6: no V5 thesis revival, no unsafe food/herbal/foraging visuals, no flattening Dia de Muertos into Halloween decor, no false plant equivalence between cempasuchil/Tagetes and calendula, and no overclaiming South Asian, Nepal/Tihar, China/Qingming, magical, medicinal, or edible-use material.

## 2. Block Or Revise Before Selection

No concept needs to be blocked outright before Susy reviews the package.

Several concepts should carry design constraints before publisher/designer selection:

- Cempasuchil path or altar visuals: proceed only with remembrance, welcome, family, scent, light, and path language. Do not use skull spectacle, costume styling, haunted-house cues, or generic Halloween orange decor.
- Safe Marigold Meaning Guide / Safe Inspiration Map: revise as a calm boundary graphic. Do not show food, tea, oil bottles, tinctures, supplements, medical icons, harvesting, foraging baskets, dosage spoons, or recipe imagery.
- Cempasuchil vs Calendula / Marigold Family Context: frame as meaning context, not botanical identification. Do not imply readers can identify edible or safe plants from the graphic.
- Golden Wedding Threshold / Marigold Wedding Meaning: keep broad unless stronger country-specific sources are added. Do not universalize South Asian, Nepal/Tihar, or wedding meanings.
- Marigold Pattern Room / William Morris design lane: use Morris-inspired rhythm, green/gold vine movement, and ordered botanical pattern language without copying museum objects or protected wallpaper designs.
- Quote graphics: label or hand off as Bloom Whispers original lines, not historical, scientific, cultural, or folklore quotes.

## 5. Visual Guardrails

Culture:

- Treat Dia de Muertos and cempasuchil as remembrance, welcome, family, light, scent, paths, altars, graves, offerings, and the life/death cycle.
- Do not reduce cultural imagery to spooky decoration, costume mood, or generic seasonal orange.
- Keep South Asian wedding, Nepal/Tihar, and China/Qingming visuals broad unless additional country-specific sources are reviewed.

Safety:

- Do not show eating, brewing, tinctures, oils, supplements, recipes, dosage, harvesting, foraging, pregnancy guidance, medical use, or treatment claims.
- Use safety visuals only as calm boundary-setting, not fear styling and not how-to guidance.
- Magical/spiritual visuals must read as symbolism, ritual mood, or personal reflection, not factual metaphysical proof.

Plant identity:

- Keep cempasuchil / Mexican marigold / Tagetes separate from calendula / pot marigold.
- Do not make plant ID cards, edible plant guides, or dangerous simplifications.
- If "African marigold" appears, preserve the caveat that it is a horticultural common name and not an origin claim.

V6 thesis protection:

- Use the approved thesis in substance: `Marigold meaning changes by where the flower is placed.`
- Do not revive V5's confusing compressed thesis language in overlays, alt text, captions, metadata, or publisher notes.
- Let the visuals show placement first; source names, botanical Latin, Malinalco, Tonalxochitl, and institutional proof should not dominate first-screen media.
