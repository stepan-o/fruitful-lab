# Days 2–4 on the factory floor

**GAME SYSTEMS DESIGNER · Proposed build for owner approval · November 19, 2026**

The prototype uses sprite-based 2D and pixel-art UI. The player chooses who runs the factory this morning, watches what the crew does with those orders, and can intervene once. By the bell, the player should have seen who made the day’s cortexes, what held up the work, and at least one exchange they want to follow tomorrow. The [interview and research record](DAYS_2_4_SYSTEMS_STUDY.md) identifies the source of each established decision. The examples, test fixtures and recovery rules below are proposals for this first build.

## The opening within Act 1

| When | What the player can see and influence |
| --- | --- |
| Day 1 establishes the history | Use the approved shift as the starting state. Three cortexes reach the rack against STILETTO’s personal target of five. LIMEN yields to her demand. Workers witness the strike according to where they are. bob is injured, ed has his card and two clerks take up Security posts. The design tab will trace how the same behavior rules express the approved performance. |
| Day 2 makes the appointment count | Choose LIMEN to keep both supervisors at their posts or STILETTO to swap them. With LIMEN at Security, bob’s place in the queue can decide whether his missing ID is detected before clearance speeds up. A daily pass lets him work today. If LIMEN discovers the missing ID and a pass is never issued, he jails bob for the day. At Conveyor, STILETTO pushes a crew that arrived late. With STILETTO at Security, the crew gets through faster and then works to LIMEN’s audible all-clears. Compare the wait, bodily effort, willingness, stress, injury exposure and actual rack at the bell. |
| Day 3 lets yesterday change an answer | After a second concession, LIMEN considers STILETTO against protocol and she considers him weak. In the same posts, his first answer is now a refusal. She presses again. If he gives in, the crew can see it and he loses CONFIDENCE and AUTHORITY. On another run the player may have headed off the exchange while leaving STILETTO with lost production time to recover. |
| Day 4 can turn an argument into a staffing problem | With the relevant history, LIMEN now holds the queue and STILETTO calls for her crew. The workers decide whom to follow. If the player has saved the intervention, they can publicly back either supervisor. On another route, an accident under an aggressive Conveyor policy gives LIMEN cause to challenge STILETTO and propose taking Conveyor. Appointment, staffing and witnesses govern which dispute is possible and when. Careful play can avert the clash. |
| Beyond the study | Burn-in Theatre and CATHEXIS add a supervisor, a room and new moves. Week one ends with quota settlement, an output split and the first new workers. The page will place these consequences on the Act 1 timeline. Weekly quantities and unlock conditions are open. |

## Player controls

| Control and authority | When and what the player chooses | Immediate floor feedback and lasting consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Appoint the daily adviser | At the morning briefing, accept one available supervisor’s complete room proposal. Show who will run each room and why they want the assignment. This uses the appointment. The shift intervention remains available. | The supervisors take the accepted posts and choose policies from their own desires. The assignment lasts the shift. What happens in either room becomes evidence for the next morning’s bids. |
| Issue bob a daily pass | At the first discovered missing-ID incident, while LIMEN’s check is open, spend the Security intervention to grant bob passage for this shift. | The pass admits bob today and expires at shift end. ed keeps the original. If a later check finds bob still lacks his ID, the issue recurs. If the case closes without a pass, including when the intervention has been spent elsewhere, LIMEN jails bob for the rest of the day. His labor is unavailable until his release at shift end. A rushed clearance that skips discovery does not open the case. |
| Accelerate Security | While LIMEN is running individual checks, direct him to speed up. Spend the Security intervention. | His calls and the queue’s movement quicken. A timely order can prevent STILETTO’s repeat outburst. LIMEN loses LOYALTY and CONFIDENCE, and his AUTHORITY with workers falls. The crew that arrives earlier still needs assignments. |
| Change Conveyor pace | While STILETTO is running Conveyor, direct her to slow it down or push it faster. Spend the Conveyor intervention. | The order changes the line’s operating pace. Workers have to perform the cycles. Their movement, speech, handoffs and completed cortexes show the effect. Exposure to injury changes with the work, and STILETTO reacts to the player pushing or frustrating her quota drive. Stat magnitudes and speeds need playtesting. |
| Publicly back a supervisor | While conflicting orders are live in the room, spend the intervention to endorse LIMEN or STILETTO. Both the target and the room are explicit. | A public, attributed message reaches those in earshot. A worker may move, stay or follow someone else after seeing them go. Supervisors can see who heeded their calls. The floor carries the immediate staffing and production consequences. Each witness takes their own account into the night. |

Watching, following a speaker, or hearing a report costs nothing, and time can pass without a choice. A report offers an intervention only while its subject is still actionable. Under the proposed recovery rule, the simulation checks the current shift, mandate and room condition again when the player commits. An expired opportunity leaves the intervention available and shows what changed. An admitted intervention consumes it once even if the worker refuses or the machinery later stalls. The daily appointment and intervention become available again next morning. The world, custody, observed history and output carry forward.

## Decisions the demonstration will calculate

| Actor and evidence | Competing desires and eligible intentions | Completion, interruption and visible result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LIMEN knows his checks, the demands he heard, his own concessions and any accident he saw or learned about. | Enforce protocol, preserve security and initially exercise caution. Check the called worker, grant clearance, answer a demand, refuse release or pursue a change of assignment when the history supports it. | A check holds until identity is verified, policy changes, or its subject leaves. A demand can interrupt it. The stronger learned desire to protect protocol can sustain a refusal after a second demand. Show the call, the gate action and the resulting queue. |
| STILETTO sees her rack, elapsed time, available operators and locally observed or reported delays. | Fill the stations, meet quota, press a peer or push the pace. A slow shift can strengthen her wish to run aggressively. An understaffed line plus prior experience of LIMEN can prompt a demand. | Keep assigning or running until the task completes or a material condition changes. On a trip to Security the rest of the factory carries on. What the crew does on arrival decides whether her demand actually delivered usable labor. |
| A worker hears locally addressed orders and public backing, sees nearby peers and remembers personally witnessed consequences. | Obey, protect oneself, keep a preferred job or follow a trusted example. Weigh only actions available from the worker’s position. A worker can wait, approach, walk to a station, work or withhold labor. | A worker keeps walking or working until arrival, completion, a conflicting message, a physical obstruction or the bell gives cause to reconsider. A worker waiting for clearance can step out after seeing another go. Show the first step, who follows, which station fills and who is still missing. |
| All workers start with “I have my ID.” On Day 2 bob instead believes “I don’t have my ID.” ed believes both “I have my ID” and “I have bob’s ID.” ed holds the original. | bob’s desire to “find my ID” influences the question he chooses when he can chat with another worker. A daily pass leaves that desire active. ed has firsthand knowledge. Other workers know only what they witnessed or heard. | STILETTO interrupts worker chats during rushed Conveyor operation. Track who heard the question and how much of the reply was delivered. bob can reconsider his search at the next opportunity. The original changes hands only through a completed transfer. Other stations keep working. |

Each significant decision will retain the physical event, its source, who could perceive it, the actor’s interpretation, competing desires, chosen intention, action and outcome. A new instruction or sight can change action immediately. Durable beliefs consolidate overnight. Speech bubbles, positions, equipment and rack contents are the player’s evidence. A separate design inspection can expose the private motive behind one selected action. The writing will use authored lines attached to actual decisions.

## Design-page delivery and proof

1. Add **Days 2–4 on the factory floor** immediately after **Supervisor × room** with a stable direct link. Use the approved floor and cast, a progression view and the control descriptions above. The principal view follows Security and Conveyor concurrently.
2. Build a small deterministic TypeScript demonstration of the queue, station handoffs and cortex rack. Offer the same morning under both adviser bids and a replayable conflict reached on the repeated-pressure path. Preserve the run’s witnessed history across the overnight transition. Let the reviewer revisit the conflict from the same starting state with public backing for either side or with the intervention spent. Keep replay and private inspection visibly separate from in-shift player powers.
3. Show the accident route and the subsequent swap proposal as a worked design example. The playable slice covers pressure at Security, changing Conveyor pace and reacting to conflicting orders. It can illustrate risky exposure without fixing an accident to a date. Exact injury odds, endorsement weights and weekly settlement figures stay open for tuning.
4. Check three readings of the same factory. A spectator following workers should see why the press waited and hear how the two policies sound. A player trying controls should be able to spend the intervention early and see the later clash run unaided. A player optimizing output should be able to compare actual completed work at the bell, locate the lost time and try a different decision next run.
5. Verify the causal differences in the demonstration. Change bob’s call order relative to accelerated clearance and check whether the ID case opens. From the detected case compare a daily pass with detention, then advance past expiry or release to the next real check. Interrupt a chat before its reply is heard and inspect what bob actually learned. Compare otherwise identical workers with different firsthand histories and inspect a changed eligible decision. Confirm that an unheard endorsement leaves the distant worker’s decision untouched until new evidence reaches them. Test a staffed line, an uncovered station, a single worker completing successive stages and intervention expiry. Verify keyboard access, phone and desktop layouts, tab order and direct linking in the PR preview.
