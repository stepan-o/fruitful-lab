Loopforge game design

Working draft · 7 October 2026

Trace the factory the player builds, the authority they establish and the society they inherit. This author reference connects the long arcs to player experience and the engine boundary, before balance and implementation.

Agreed direction records the owner's stated design. Proposed trajectories, gates and recoveries are material to review, not approved balance or implemented gameplay. Old source rules are evidence, not constraints on the new game.

World and story grounding

A robot world

The world is populated, designed and run by robots after an unspecified event. Its cause remains outside the present account. Building sufficient compute has damaged the sky; unequal capability has become unequal authority.

A factory inside that world

Loopforge is a town produced by that wider history, not the centre or origin of the universe. Its oppressive industrial beauty, quotas and exhausted workers ground the drama. Its founding remains open; it is not an established refuge or benevolent reform project.

Manufacturing the hierarchy

Ordinary robots assemble more ordinary robots. Exceptional minds have real capabilities, their own egos and claims to superiority. Decisions about which minds to build also distribute opportunity and power.

The concealed project

Brain 2.0 enables a generation intended to replace the workers building it. Neither the player nor those workers knows this at the first shift. The timing of discovery remains open; it need not coincide exactly with the ability to inspect thoughts.

Agreed direction · proposed mastery gate

Security + Lattice Forge

Arrival
Limen and Stiletto
Visible operation
Begin with Conveyor and Security available and every assignment empty. Read the factory totals and the quota due at the end of the week. Choose Limen or Stiletto as the first adviser; their briefing proposes the initial arrangement.
Learning
Whose judgment you choose changes how the factory operates. Limen favours safety and compliance at a cost to output; Stiletto favours production at a cost to equipment and accident exposure. Each day’s output can strengthen the factory or fulfil the weekly obligation.
Decision
Choose the adviser, then accept or override the proposed assignments. At the daily allocation, permanently commit completed robots to the factory or the weekly quota. Balance can emerge from changing advisers and decisions as conditions change.
Friction
Retaining robots leaves more of the quota for later; committing them to the quota gives up their future contribution inside the factory. Caution limits growth. Pushing output can leave the line less capable on following days, even when no accident occurs.
Proposed gate
Candidate evidence: meet a weekly quota while sustaining a viable workforce and production line. Mastery, not a fixed number of days or a required accident, opens the next stage.
Hidden development
Robots produced while the Theatre is closed have no indoctrination history. Retained workers accumulate their own experiences; that absence does not automatically make them disobedient. Workers and supervisors remember whose methods you backed. Wear also accumulates beyond today’s output.
Carried forward
Cathexis inherits the workforce you retained, its experiences, the condition of the line and the authority established between Limen and Stiletto.

Burn-in Theatre

Arrival
Cathexis
Visible operation
Open the Burn-in Theatre and bring Cathexis into a factory already shaped by Limen, Stiletto and daily production allocations. Indoctrination introduces a new way to influence worker behaviour while the weekly quota continues.
Learning
Better coordination can strengthen Cathexis’s influence over workers. Loyalty to you, confidence in herself and respect from her audience can move in different directions. Her programme interacts with the practices already established by Limen and Stiletto.
Decision
Decide how much authority to give Cathexis and what programme to support. Choosing her as adviser gives her priorities control of the day’s proposal. Combine her role with the operating approach established by Limen and Stiletto, then continue the irreversible daily allocation.
Friction
Her influence may sustain an output push or organize resistance to its costs. Restricting her can sacrifice cooperation; empowering her creates expectations and leverage. Forcing her onto the Conveyor can damage her standing with workers without restoring loyalty. Neither praise nor repeated reassignment resolves incompatible commitments.
Proposed gate
Candidate evidence: sustain the weekly obligation while managing the effect of the programme and the authority you have given Cathexis.
Hidden development
Different production cohorts carry different conditioning and experience histories. Cathexis’s confidence, worker respect and loyalty can diverge. A supervisor may learn or infer which workers missed indoctrination and decide whether to mention it in a briefing. Equipment damage continues to accumulate.
Carried forward
Witch encounters both the physical damage from earlier operation and a social arrangement that may support or resist the downtime her work needs.

Cognitive Substrate Brewery

Arrival
Rivet Witch
Visible operation
Rivet Witch arrives with the Cognitive Substrate Brewery. Conveyor scraps can now become substrate. Her arrival also introduces repair as a player mechanic: engineering can restore equipment damaged during the earlier stages.
Learning
Only engineers can repair. Witch’s time is shared between technical work in existing rooms and the Brewery. Repair costs include time and lost production; money alone cannot provide the missing expertise.

Carried forward
Weaving inherits a substrate supply, the remaining faults and the relationships formed around deciding what to fix first.

Thrum

Cortex Assembly + Brain 2.0

Arrival
News of incoming supervisors
Visible operation
Fund and open Cortex Assembly, finish Brain 2.0 and prepare the first floor to operate with less direct intervention. News spreads that two incoming supervisors may replace two of the current team.
Learning
High visible output does not tell you who kept the factory working. A supervisor’s assessment may now also be a defence of their own position.

Hidden development
Fear of being replaced changes what supervisors are willing to admit. One may hide a mistake, another blame a colleague, another protect an ally. Earlier promises and overrides shape who still trusts you.
Carried forward
Act 2 begins with the workers, damage, relationships and promises you accumulated. Access to workers’ inner lives starts revealing what your earlier decisions have done.
Act 2 · the inherited society

The society becomes legible

A second factory floor opens. The first becomes mostly auto-managed, with continuing production, workforce and social feedback. Two new supervisors arrive, probably drawn from a larger pool, altering both mechanics and possible story arcs.

What carries forward

  • Individual workers with accumulated wear, memories, beliefs and relationships.
  • A functioning or compromised operating regime, including commitments to Cathexis.
  • Promises, rumours and competing accounts of who deserves credit or replacement.
  • Material capacity, retained expertise and the costs paid to complete Brain 2.0.
  • Accident history, supervisor stress and self-doubt, the director’s responses, disputed responsibility and different audiences’ knowledge.
  • Actual conditioning histories, including early retained workers made before the Theatre opened and any later programme participation.

What becomes possible

  • Previously hidden worker state and access to what robots think become available.
  • New interventions make social dynamics central to management and the run's outcome.
  • Routine first-floor control recedes; failures, deliveries and social feedback can still cross floors.
  • The replacement decision becomes a real policy, not a predetermined betrayal.
Thought access reveals a robot's interpretation, not omniscient truth. Its exact limits, the new supervisors and the final outcome structure remain open.

Illustrative paths through the shared progression, not locked classes or predetermined endings.

Stiletto plus Cathexis sustaining the pushHigher output and commitment can sustain expansion while wear accumulates. Witch’s necessary stoppages can challenge both production claims and promises made to workers.

Proposed trajectory

Procedural order

Begin with Limen’s safety priority, then constrain Cathexis to the official programme. Later engineering must work within an authority structure built before repair was possible.

  1. Security + Lattice Forge

    Choose Limen from an unassigned opening. Accept or revise his initial arrangement, then divide scarce daily production between permanent retention and the weekly quota. Prevention preserves equipment but can delay growth.

  2. Burn-in Theatre

    Use Cathexis to reinforce the programme. Earlier workers retain their experiences and missing conditioning until actual Theatre participation changes them. Commit to limiting her independence; any compliance circuit remains a separately designed side route.

  3. Cognitive Substrate Brewery

    Witch introduces engineering. A better-preserved line can leave more of her time for the Brewery, but Limen’s procedures and the established programme may obstruct the changes she considers necessary.

  4. Weaving Gallery + Brain 1.5

    Retain smart workers where precision raises value. Thrum's preparation must coexist with the operating schedule rather than continually being overridden. Permit a bounded Thrum intervention when stress demands it, accepting that Limen may object to the loss of discipline.

  5. Cortex Assembly + Brain 2.0

    Complete Cortex with a dependable but potentially brittle first floor. Replacement rumours invite formal accusations, selected records and appeals to higher authority.

Pitfall
The factory obeys but struggles with an exceptional condition. Removing discretion can make the director the bottleneck.
Recovery
From Part 03, allocate Witch’s time to a technical correction, make an explicit exception or expand someone’s mandate. Record the lost production and changed authority; no history is reset.
Act 2 inheritance
Act 2 receives a capable first floor whose obedience and resilience may be very different. A compliant Cathexis cannot lead a prohibited revolution, but worker reactions and other actors remain consequential.
Design test
Can this route reach Brain 2.0 without an unavoidable quota failure? Does better judgment create a viable evolution rather than a compulsory switch to Stiletto? Can sustained safety avoid the accident altogether without blocking progress?
Proposed trajectory

Production before everything

The player backs Stiletto's discretion and empowers Cathexis to keep the workforce committed to an aggressive production programme.

  1. Security + Lattice Forge

    Choose Stiletto before initial assignments. Higher output gives more robots to divide between the factory and the weekly quota; each retained robot is permanently unavailable for delivery. Wear and risk accumulate without an early repair option.

  2. Burn-in Theatre

    Give Cathexis authority to sustain the production programme. A larger early workforce brings its own unindoctrinated history. Her message can secure cooperation while creating expectations about what the sacrifices will achieve.

  3. Cognitive Substrate Brewery

    Witch arrives to a damaged operation. Letting her repair competes with the Brewery and the output both Stiletto and Cathexis have promised. Deferring that work can preserve today’s production while narrowing later options.

  4. Weaving Gallery + Brain 1.5

    Sell valuable smart output to accelerate expansion. Retaining too little expertise makes the advanced chain fragile; Thrum's recovery may become essential. Defending his relief programme against Stiletto becomes part of recovery, not an automatic productivity upgrade.

  5. Cortex Assembly + Brain 2.0

    Reach Cortex quickly enough to expose the cost of the promises. As replacement rumours circulate, each supervisor tries to separate their own results from the damage.

Pitfall
A major repair and a credibility crisis arrive together. The workforce has heard promises the director cannot cheaply fulfil.
Recovery
Accept engineering downtime once Witch is available, slow expansion or concede authority to restore cooperation. Future daily production can be allocated differently; already retained workers cannot be cashed out or reassigned to the quota.
Act 2 inheritance
Act 2 opens with a larger or wealthier operation, accumulated wear, and a powerful account of who paid for success. Cathexis may remain an ally or demand a change in policy.
Design test
Can recovery happen before a terminal collapse? Are the early gains substantial enough to justify the risk, without generating an accidental infinite scrap economy?
Proposed trajectory

Negotiated authority

Adapt between Limen’s safety and Stiletto’s output, then commit to Cathexis’s independent authority under concrete terms. The resulting coalition inherits the physical and social costs of the early choices.

  1. Security + Lattice Forge

    Choose either initial adviser, then use later factual results to decide when to change advisers or override assignments. Balance is an evolving approach, not a free priority setting. Weekly quota commitments and factory allocations cannot be reversed.

  2. Burn-in Theatre

    Back Cathexis with specific commitments to workers. Her audience includes early cohorts with experiences from before the Theatre. Her growing influence can make those experiences politically important; support or restrictions must survive actual production pressure.

  3. Cognitive Substrate Brewery

    When Witch arrives, her inspections can give worker complaints material support or challenge them. Granting her engineering time honours some commitments while sacrificing Brewery output and testing the others.

  4. Weaving Gallery + Brain 1.5

    Retain enough smart workers to stabilize advanced production. Accept Thrum's preparation when necessary; the cost may be slower accumulation of expansion money. Protecting genuine relief may require renegotiating the coalition’s expectations about output and obedience.

  5. Cortex Assembly + Brain 2.0

    Finance Cortex through a sustainable chain. Address the replacement rumour before incompatible assurances become the basis of several alliances.

Pitfall
A quota emergency requires breaking a promise or granting a concession that reduces the director's unilateral power.
Recovery
Renegotiate openly and pay a concrete price. The coalition may survive with stronger worker or supervisor authority and less freedom for the player.
Act 2 inheritance
Act 2 receives organised support and an expectation of consultation. Those relationships are productive assets and constraints, especially if the replacement purpose becomes known.
Design test
Are agreements enforceable enough to matter? Does this path carry real economic and political costs rather than becoming the universally correct dialogue route?
Proposed side route exploration

An alternative to Cathexis

The player deliberately builds the capacity to transfer conditioning away from Cathexis. This explores the requested specific side route; the worker-cadre solution itself is not yet accepted.

  1. Security + Lattice Forge

    Choose the first adviser from an unassigned opening. Establish viable weekly production and commit daily output to the factory or quota with the later institutional change in mind.

  2. Burn-in Theatre

    Manage Cathexis through a declared temporary arrangement, accepting its cost. Endless assignment shuffling is not a substitute for the promised alternative.

  3. Cognitive Substrate Brewery

    Witch introduces repair. Allocate her scarce time between inherited damage and the Brewery; an incident can divert work intended to support the later handover. Assigning blame changes who will cooperate.

  4. Weaving Gallery + Brain 1.5

    After Brain 1.5, retain and train a skilled cadre for the Theatre. Training competes directly with advanced throughput and revenue. Allocation to Thrum’s relief competes with both training and production.

  5. Cortex Assembly + Brain 2.0

    Complete an explicit transfer of responsibility, then finance Cortex with reduced dependence on a single supervisor. Cathexis can resent or negotiate the loss of her role.

Pitfall
The transition weakens production before the new arrangement can support itself. The new cadre may also develop its own agenda or status interests.
Recovery
Delay Cortex, keep a limited negotiated role for Cathexis, or accept obligations to the new group. None of these should reset the cost of the handover.
Act 2 inheritance
Act 2 receives more distributed authority and a new constituency, not an institution free of politics.
Design test
Is this route attainable before the commitment's consequences overwhelm the player? Does it solve a specific problem at a real cost without becoming the best of every branch?

The major Act 1 forks

Agreed central fork · proposed branch details

The Cathexis commitment

Commit one way and manage its pitfall. Assignment oscillation and reassurance alone must ultimately fail as indefinite strategies. The precise branches below remain design proposals.

Build around her authority

Gain
Powerful conditioning, coordination and genuine worker commitment.
Lasting liability
Cathexis and her audience acquire expectations and independent leverage. Loyalty can remain strong, but support has conditions.
Downstream response
Honour negotiated commitments, accept concessions, or deliberately channel a movement. High confidence alone must not guarantee betrayal.

Constrain her authority

Gain
Limit independent political action and retain more control of the approved message.
Lasting liability
Humiliation may reduce worker respect while increasing resentment. A compliance circuit may restrict adaptability or provoke a worker response; exact costs are open.
Downstream response
Support the remaining organisation through other methods. If a circuit guarantees Cathexis cannot initiate revolution, the game must honour that guarantee.

Build a specific alternative

Gain
A route away from dependence on either continuing empowerment or permanent suppression.
Lasting liability
Candidate: train a worker cadre to run the Theatre, using scarce smart workers, time and a costly handover.
Downstream response
Manage the new group's authority and interests. This route, its requirements and even its inclusion are not yet agreed.

Why switching back and forth fails

  • Beliefs outlast assignments.The audience remembers the message after Cathexis leaves the room.
  • Humiliation is not a reset.Damaging respect can reduce influence while making her less loyal.
  • Contradictory promises accumulate.Reassurance loses force when repeated actions contradict it.
  • Conversation still matters.It can negotiate a concession or repair a specific breach. It cannot erase an incompatible operating policy.
Agreed major Act 1 arc · proposed responses

What the accident teaches the factory

A major Act 1 arc. Most operating paths are likely to suffer at least one accident; sustained safety protocols can avoid it. The accident is produced by the factory, not scheduled because the story needs a crisis.

Prevent the accident

Gain
Preserve workers and machinery through sustained safety practices.
Lasting liability
Slower output and disagreement over stop authority compete with the weekly quota and growth.
Downstream response
Demonstrate mastery through safe throughput. This is a valid trajectory without a compulsory disaster.

Acknowledge and address the damage

Gain
Give affected people a credible account and address the actual hazard.
Lasting liability
Immediate distress, downtime, demands for accountability and a supervisor who may doubt their own judgment.
Downstream response
In Parts 01–02, reduce further exposure and work within surviving capacity. From Part 03, commit actual engineering time where needed. A public account or reassurance never restores equipment.

Restrict or redirect the account

Gain
Limit immediate circulation or use the incident to reshape authority.
Lasting liability
Witnesses retain their experiences. Selective blame and concealment can change relationships and future reporting.
Downstream response
Manage the actual damage and the people who know. Exposure depends on information routes, not a guaranteed reveal timer.

Prioritize safety and compliance

Lasting liability
Lower output leaves fewer robots to divide between growth and the weekly quota, and less earning potential.

Prioritize production

Gain
More output can meet the weekly quota or expand the workforce sooner.
Lasting liability
Wear and accident exposure accumulate. Retaining a larger workforce does not restore damaged machinery.
Downstream response
Reduce further exposure or accept constrained output before Part 03. Once Witch arrives, decide what engineering work displaces Brewery production.

Gain

Lasting liability

Who gets to remain

Two newcomers are said to be replacing the worst performers. The player determines whether that becomes true. Accident responsibility, concealed incidents and preventive work can all distort who appears to be performing well.

Carry out replacement

Gain
Restructure authority around incoming capability and the player's assessment.
Lasting liability
Lose established expertise and relationships; confirm the threat others feared.
Downstream response
Manage the resulting vacancies, loyalties and claims about fairness.

The daily adviser decision

Day one begins with the handover, a weekly quota and no assignments. Choose the adviser first; they choose the priority and propose the initial arrangement. Later days begin with yesterday’s results. Their room receives automatic event authority after assignments take effect.

  1. 01Read the facts
  2. 02Choose the adviser
  3. 03Hear their assessment
  4. 04Accept or override
  5. 05Run under their judgment

Adviser’s assigned room

Their response resolves automatically. You see what they did and what happened.

Other rooms and factory-wide decisions

Their response is proposed. You accept or override; informed supervisors react.

The planning loop and its limits
Read the factsDay one shows funds, worker count, known condition and the weekly quota, with every assignment empty. Later days show yesterday’s direct results, permanent allocations and remaining quota.
Choose the adviserThis is the primary daily choice. Compare known expertise, habitual concerns, recent observable conduct and prior advice. Choosing them for advice does not automatically move their room assignment.
Hear their assessmentThe adviser assesses current conditions on day one or yesterday’s results thereafter, adds attributed context, chooses a priority and proposes placements or operating responses. The player cannot submit a priority.
Accept or overrideAuthorize or override the proposed assignments. There is no roster to carry over on day one. Later, keeping a previous placement against the adviser’s recommendation is an override with remembered consequences.
Run under their judgmentThe adviser proposes responses throughout the day. Events in their assigned room resolve automatically under their judgment; response decisions elsewhere present their default for the player to accept or override. Results and relationship memories feed tomorrow’s report.
One primary choice each dayThe normal planning sequence includes one adviser selection before the shift. Inspecting a portrait or known record is free. Proposed commitment control: Choose and hear briefing commits the daily selection; reopening the briefing does not select another adviser or reroll it.
The adviser owns the priorityThe player chooses a person, not a target for that person to optimize. Their knowledge, expertise, stress, relationships and motives determine what they consider urgent. A different priority means choosing a different adviser on a subsequent day; there is no same-day shopping through full proposals.
The factual report stays factualDirectly revealed totals and recorded events are mechanically authoritative. Their causes, severity forecasts, hidden history and personal interpretations are separate. An adviser may challenge an explanation or add a claim, but cannot rewrite yesterday’s displayed delivery count.
Assessment and additional contextEarly advisers explain the factory sincerely and help the player learn. Expertise can still be incomplete. Later omissions, exaggeration or deliberate falsehood emerge from recorded beliefs, pressures and motives; deception does not switch on at an arbitrary day.
Compact proposalsShow one preferred arrangement and highlight a few material changes against the current roster. Keep unchanged positions collapsed. Explain the intended benefit and the cost the adviser admits; deeper records remain available without requiring exhaustive room-by-room comparison.
Overrides have a social costThe player retains assignment control, but overruling the adviser is a remembered challenge to their judgment or authority. Reaction depends on the importance of the change, confidence, loyalty, stress and past treatment. Consequences must remain proportionate enough for disagreement to be a viable decision.
One conversation with a purposeQuestions, reassurance, blame or promises respond to the chosen adviser’s assessment and proposed actions. They can affect relationships without letting the player replace the adviser’s priority. Exact follow-up limits remain open; ordinary event recommendations are part of the daily role, not extra adviser selections.
Orders still require executionAn accepted recommendation or player override becomes a recorded order, not a guaranteed forecast or guaranteed obedience. The supervisor doing the work can interpret, cooperate with or resist it through the existing BDI rules.
Available capabilityAdvisers can only propose actions permitted at the current stage. Limen, Stiletto and Cathexis cannot repair; engineering arrives with Witch. Advice cannot recall committed quota units or turn retained workers back into deliverable stock.
How the player learns whom to choose
LimenProcedure and controlled access. Useful when order or safe coordination is in question; his response can cost speed and discretion.
StilettoOutput and delivery. Useful when flow and deadlines dominate the visible problem; her approach can carry wear, exposure and overconfidence.
CathexisBelief, coordination and influence. Useful when the workforce’s collective response matters; success can increase her independent authority.
Rivet WitchFaults, repair and technical integrity. Useful when the chain keeps breaking; thorough work can consume today’s output and available time.
ThrumWorker strain, resonance and relief. Useful when the workforce needs recovery; his approach can reduce productive time and discipline.
Learn the choice before doubting the speakerBegin with Limen and Stiletto giving sincere, comprehensible assessments. Their different priorities still carry real costs. Establish what facts mean, what each notices and how their recommendation changes work.
Gain evidence through useAfter a shift, connect the chosen adviser’s priority, actions and one visible benefit or cost. Preserve a short record of their advice. Do not reduce reliability to a universal success score when changing conditions and player overrides matter.
Introduce uncertainty through the factoryAs rooms and social pressures develop, advisers help identify bottlenecks and risks the player cannot easily compare. Their changing inner state can make a previously useful tendency excessive, defensive or deceptive.
Reveal what was missingAt an appropriate later discovery or run review, show what an earlier adviser knew or believed, what they said, what the player authorized and how it contributed to the outcome. Revelation timing remains open, but the learning payoff is required. It must use preserved history rather than inventing a secret explanation afterward.

How this avoids replacing one kind of fatigue with another
  • The daily choice must be understandable and enjoyable to play. A technically sophisticated trace does not justify vague actions, confusing token movement or a briefing that feels like homework.
  • The player should be able to say before starting the shift: I chose this adviser because of this known tendency, and I understand which room I delegated to them.
  • No priority selector or conversation workaround lets the player turn the chosen adviser into any other adviser. Placement overrides remain available and have social consequences.
  • Early advice must be sincere, useful and readable. Later deception must have a recorded motive or mistaken belief; a novel line of generated prose cannot invent a betrayal.
  • Choosing an adviser must change priorities or responses, not only dialogue. It need not cause unnecessary reassignment when the current arrangement is sensible.
  • Override costs must be meaningful and proportionate. The player can knowingly reject bad advice; neither universal obedience nor constant opposition should dominate every situation.
  • Do not display every possible allocation, every private motive or a graph of rumour sources. Clear facts, a legible adviser choice and a few consequential actions carry the first playable loop.
  • Show automatic decisions and their effects clearly. Missing a brief animation must not prevent learning what the delegated adviser did.
  • The later reveal must explain earlier events from stored evidence, including the player’s own edits and other supervisors’ actual execution. It must not blame advice for a different plan the player imposed.

Proposed loop design · timing targets to playtest
1 second

Read the living factory

Is this working as I intended?
Player activity
Follow a batch or supervisor, notice a change, or select something to inspect. Most seconds require no input. Consequential decisions pause, so a missed visual cue is not a reflex penalty.
World activity
Batches advance, output accumulates, equipment changes condition and supervisors act. Visible activity follows state and intentions; station animations do not introduce a station-staffing simulation.
Feedback
Orders acknowledge promptly as accepted, queued or rejected. Physical execution takes its actual time. Belt cadence, queue shape, posture, alarm, sound and a persistent event cue make the result readable without exposing hidden scores. Resolved events in the adviser’s room remain visible in the feed; decisions elsewhere present their proposed response when player input is required.
Payoff
The pleasure of a dense machine working, and recognition of a person’s characteristic behaviour. Visual variation helps identify meaningful changes.
Carried outward
A visible hesitation or growing queue becomes a question for the minute loop. Inspection retains the event and its source rather than demanding the player remember a fleeting animation.
Example
The line slows under Limen’s procedure or accelerates under Stiletto’s approach. Completed units reach the output count. A warning, if earned by the state, remains attached to the room for inspection.
Design trap
A constant spectacle with no readable state is noise. A click required every second turns direction into busywork. Neither should be the main source of difficulty.
1 minute

Test an operating decision

Which problem should I solve, and what will that cost?
Player activity
Observe the adviser’s automatic actions in their room. Elsewhere, inspect their recommendation for an operational problem and accept or override it, then watch enough work to judge the effect. A meaningful question roughly each minute is a pacing hypothesis, not a mandatory alert.
World activity
Production, staffing and wear constrain the intervention. A supervisor interprets the mandate through current beliefs and commitments. Stress or a report may change the response, while the factory still resolves the actual work.
Feedback
Show an intervention’s output effect alongside known operating consequences. In Parts 01–02, slowing or stopping can prevent further damage but does not restore equipment. From Part 03, engineering progress can appear.
Payoff
A hypothesis tested: “the problem was outtake, not intake.” A supervisor’s approach becomes useful, troubling or newly understandable.
Carried outward
The chosen remedy leaves output, wear, a witnessed precedent or a promise. Those alter the next operating problem and the next consultation.
Example
An intake queue grows. Faster intake would worsen it. The player authorizes a temporary staffing change at outtake, accepting a cost elsewhere. After the transfer and several completed batches, throughput recovers; the displaced work is still waiting.
Design trap
A universal best intervention solves the loop; unrelated random punishment makes it unreadable. Stable operation must be a valid reason to leave things alone. Early play does not require a repair action.
30 minutes

Establish a way of running the place

What kind of factory did my successful decisions create?
Player activity
Across several days, choose advisers whose judgment seems suited to the known situation. Learn what their priorities achieve, accept or override their actions, and carry the resulting production and relationships into later choices.
World activity
Workers and supervisors retain history. Daily permanent allocations build capacity or weekly quota progress; equipment condition and relationship consequences carry forward. Engineering joins those tradeoffs in Part 03.
Feedback
Connect the session’s end to its beginning: committed quota progress, retained workers, changed equipment condition and a supervisor whose method you backed. Later sessions may also show engineering work; the recap uses actual history.
Payoff
Competence plus authorship: “I can run this place, and this is how I chose to run it.” An earned new capability can open the next problem; a recovered setback can provide an equally complete session arc.
Carried outward
Save the exact factory, people, commitments and unresolved matters. A session boundary does not reset relationships, reroll incidents or manufacture an ending.
Example
Across three early shifts, Limen’s procedures preserve the line but limit output. Retaining some completed robots strengthens the factory while leaving more quota for later. The player decides whether to keep his approach or choose Stiletto next; prior commitments and wear remain.
Design trap
Thirty minutes of unrelated alerts has no arc. Thirty minutes without a visible payoff feels like preparation for a game that has not started. A session need not complete Act 1 or Brain 2.0.
One second, one minute and thirty minutes describe player attention and payoff. They are not simulation tick rates, forced event intervals or room-unlock timers. Session design stops before the full-run structure.

The shift inside the session

A shift connects local experiments to lasting consequences. It is the repeatable decision cycle inside a session, not another mandatory thirty-minute unit.

01

Paused plan

Day one has a handover, weekly quota and no assignments. Choose an adviser before any arrangement. On later days, review results and choose again; the existing roster is a proposal baseline. Hear the adviser’s priority, then accept or override placements.

02

Continuous operation

The chosen adviser recommends responses throughout continuous operation and automatically resolves events in their assigned room. Routine notices record those actions. Other supervisors retain their roles, interpretations and ability to respond.

03

Consequential interruption

For response decisions outside the adviser’s room, pause with their proposed resolution selected. Accept or override it. Every supervisor interprets the decision when they witness or learn of it; the response can affect respect, loyalty and confidence.

04

Settle and carry forward

Account for completed output and known losses. Permanently split today’s production between factory and quota; show remaining weekly obligation and days left. Review development commitments when relevant. Payment timing is separate and unresolved.

First pacing hypothesis

For the first pacing test, target roughly three complete shifts in a thirty-minute sitting, including learning, reading and paused decisions. This is a hypothesis to test, not a countdown. The player may finish fewer shifts; unlocks still require demonstrated mastery.

Alternate commitment, observation, tension and release. Several systems can run at once, but keep one foreground operational question legible while the others leave quieter evidence.

Pressure and its transmission

Stress, news and the cost of relief

Workers and supervisors carry stress of their own. Work pressure, accidents, decisions and information feed it. The accident and its aftermath form a major Act 1 arc: material consequences, private interpretations and public accounts can diverge and influence the next shift.

Agreed direction · proposed propagation and response rules
Major Act 1 arc

The accident and its aftermath

A major Act 1 arc. Most operating paths are likely to suffer at least one accident; sustained safety protocols can avoid it. The accident is produced by the factory, not scheduled because the story needs a crisis.

From operating risk to a lasting fork
Accumulated exposurePressure, room staffing, wear, supervisor decisions and safety practices establish risk. Damage persists before engineering arrives; later, deferred engineering work also matters. Severity includes bounded randomness within those conditions.
Material damageWorkers and equipment can suffer grave harm. Accidents can repeat if their causes remain or recovery creates new pressures; a second incident also tests earlier promises.
Personal consequencesThe responsible supervisor experiences stress and a change in confidence. Their account of their responsibility can differ from the physical record. Others form their own views from what reaches them.
The player respondsThe daily consultation can assign blame, relieve self-doubt, reassure with conditions or exploit the incident for another agenda. Orders, resources and later follow-through determine what those words mean.
The player disclosesChoose which supervisors and workers to inform, when, and how to frame the account. Witnesses already know something; repair needs, absences and personal contacts can carry information outside the official account.

Disclosure and the private response are separate choices. A director can publicly acknowledge an accident while privately scapegoating someone, or support the supervisor while keeping other rooms uninformed. Neither secrecy nor eventual exposure is guaranteed.

Damage reduces capacity → quota pressure increases → stressed supervisors interpret the next problem through the accident and the director’s response → their actions change safety and reporting → further incidents or recovery follow. Information can carry this pressure into rooms untouched by the original damage.

Safety paths must still reach Brain 2.0. No unlock should require a casualty. Reduced risk must matter across runs; random severity must not routinely overwhelm preparation. Most serious consequences redirect the run, while destruction or exhausted recovery can still end it.

Show warnings before exposure, a prompt physical result after the decision, and later behavioural echoes. The player may misread a motive, but should be able to learn which operating choices changed risk.

Information travels through the factory

Event or allegation

An accident occurs, or someone claims one did. A lie creates a statement in history, not an accident.

Initial audience

Witnesses, responders, relevant supervisors or a chosen recipient know something first.

Contact and reporting

Official reports, consultation, worker contact and deliberate announcements can carry different versions.

Interpretation

A recipient accepts, doubts or rejects the account through their evidence and beliefs.

Relay or refusal

They pass it on, alter it, keep it private or decline to repeat it. A rumour can stop here.

Consequences

Stress, blame, trust or behaviour change among affected people. Those changes can feed back into real production.

A real accident, partial news

Physical truth
An accident injures workers and interrupts the conveyor. Its physical consequences happen regardless of whether leadership announces it.
Who learns what
The local crew witnesses the accident. Supervisors learn through contacts, reports or visible effects. From Part 03, Witch may also learn through an engineering request.
What they believe
One room learns that equipment failed. Another hears that leadership knowingly forced a dangerous restart. Recipients may agree on the injury and disagree about responsibility.
Player response
Disclose confirmed facts and the available response, delay a wider statement while investigating, or restrict official reporting. Only promise a repair when engineering is available; restrictions do not control every channel.
Consequences
Witnesses need actual relief and safer conditions. Later recipients may fear for themselves or distrust the earlier silence. Prompt disclosure can also spread immediate distress; no communication option should be universally free.
Thrum’s role
Thrum can ease the affected workers’ stress once available. Doing so takes productive capacity and loosens discipline while the missing output still has to be addressed.

A false accident takes hold

Physical truth
No such accident occurred. A supervisor or worker claims there was a serious conveyor incident and that leadership is hiding it.
Who learns what
The claim reaches a group through a source they trust, perhaps against a history of real accidents, secrecy or unexplained absence.
What they believe
The listeners may believe both the disaster and the cover-up, or only suspect something is wrong. Repetition from the same source is not independent confirmation.
Player response
Investigate the account, provide credible evidence, address the underlying reason it sounds plausible, or try to silence its circulation. A denial from distrusted leadership may be insufficient.
Consequences
Fear can affect attendance, cooperation or concentration even while the original accident remains fictional. A later real incident caused by the resulting disruption would be a separate event.
Thrum’s role
Relief can help distressed workers regain capacity without proving the rumour false. Reassurance, evidence and stress recovery solve different parts of the problem.

An attempted rumour fails

Physical truth
Someone attempts to start a false accident story. There is no matching accident and no automatic factory-wide morale penalty.
Who learns what
The intended messenger refuses to relay it, the source has little credibility, or the listeners have direct contradictory evidence.
What they believe
The claim remains local, is rejected, or attracts no further attention. Not every unsuccessful rumour must expose its originator to the player.
Player response
If the attempt becomes known, ignore it, challenge it or investigate the motive. The response depends on what the player actually learns.
Consequences
It may leave no wider effect. A recipient may remember the attempt or revise their opinion of the source, but that consequence is conditional—not an obligatory punishment.
Thrum’s role
No audience became distressed, so no relief intervention is required for this claim. Thrum should not be consumed by a crisis the simulation never produced.
What creates stress and what remains separate
Work pressurePace, workload and unresolved hazards create continuing strain, even without an accident.
Supervisor conductThreats, humiliation, contradictory instructions and escalating demands can add stress. The effect depends on who experiences them and their history.
Direct experienceInjured workers and witnesses are affected immediately by a real accident. Restricting an announcement cannot erase what they experienced.
Information and anticipationNews of real or imagined danger, alleged concealment and replacement rumours can distress workers away from the event. Knowledge need not spread uniformly.
Supervisor stressSupervisors experience the incident and the director’s response themselves. Their strain affects how they interpret the next problem, not just what they report.
  • Physical wear, experienced stress, willingness to obey and trust in leadership are distinct. A quiet, compliant workforce can remain highly stressed.
  • The factory has a true event history and each worker has a history of exposure and belief. A report reaches an audience; it does not automatically update everyone.
  • Being told, believing, repeating and acting on a claim are separate steps. Origin, relationships, prior experience and available evidence affect each step.
  • News can be distorted in severity, cause, responsibility or claims of concealment. A rumour need not be wholly true or wholly false.
  • Stress can fall while a grievance, damaged body or memory remains. Recovery should not require forgetting what happened.
The player’s influence over information
DisclosureChoose the scope, timing and certainty of an official account. Separate confirmed facts from unresolved causes.
InvestigationSeek witnesses, operating records or a supervisor’s explanation. Witch can add engineering evidence from Part 03. Follow-up attention during the daily briefing has an opportunity cost.
Access and channelsInfluence official reporting, permissions and contact where the factory’s rules allow. The player does not have an omnipotent switch for who knows.
FramingAttribute responsibility, offer reassurance or let Cathexis interpret the event. Credibility and the recipient’s experience constrain the result.
Material responseReduce dangerous exposure or accept lower output. From Part 03, allocate engineering time to actual repair. Communication does not restore lost workers or damaged equipment.
ReliefOnce Thrum arrives, decide when and where to deploy his unusually strong ability to lower stress, accepting its production and discipline costs.
Agreed direction

Thrum can actually make them feel better

Thrum provides genuine, unusually strong stress relief. His near-unique value is that he can make the workers feel better, even when today’s production result looks poor.

The Brewery scene becomes a shared gathering; workers dance beside the conveyor; the Theatre becomes a relaxed musical session. These are positive experiences for the workers. The original rules likewise trade output and discipline for relief in several placements.

Relief consumes productive time and reduces discipline. Low stress does not automatically mean high compliance, and a fall in discipline need not mean the workers are incapable.

LimenMay see recovered but less compliant workers as a loss of order. His old response to Harmonic Dissolution includes lower loyalty.

Thrum’s established specialty is worker relief. It does not repair machinery, erase memories or settle blame. Whether and how he can help another supervisor’s stress is a separate open design question.

Before he arrives, the early game must remain viable through managing pressure and avoiding repeated shocks. Exact lesser recovery methods remain open; they should not make Thrum’s specialty redundant.

How interpretation becomes action

Beliefs, desires and intentions

Supervisors act on an incomplete interpretation of the factory, their colleagues and their boss. BDI connects that interpretation to a persistent course of action, then lets the result change what they believe next.

Agreed foundation · proposed behavioural design
Beliefs

What I think is happening

“The director says safety matters, but punishes every stoppage.” Store the claim, its source, certainty and contradictory evidence separately from world truth.

Desires

What I want to achieve or protect

Meet the quota, keep workers alive, prove competence, protect a colleague or avoid replacement. Several concerns can conflict.

Intentions

What I have committed to doing

After engineering unlocks: “Keep the line stopped until the repair is verified.” An intention has a plan, a reason to persist and conditions for finishing, abandoning or reconsidering it.

Stress, self-confidence, loyalty, personality, capability and remembered experiences shape interpretation and priorities. They are not three extra names for BDI. Confidence in a particular belief is also distinct from confidence in oneself.

1 · Observe

Receive only available signals and messages. Missing information stays missing.

2 · Interpret

Revise beliefs about the equipment, other people and the director’s expectations.

3 · Prioritize

Weigh competing concerns through personality, stress, history and existing promises.

4 · Commit

Select or retain a plan; record why alternatives were rejected.

5 · Attempt

Request a concrete action. Resolve it against actual equipment, resources and other actions.

6 · Remember

Record the result, revise self-confidence and stress, and decide what to tell whom. That message enters another agent’s observations.

A supervisor can sincerely obey a misunderstood order. The player then interprets the supervisor through incomplete reports. Both sides can be wrong without either deliberately lying.
Commitment, initiative and breakdown

Reconsider on relevant new evidence, a changed order, an impossible plan, accumulated strain or a broken promise. Do not choose a fresh personality every shift. Persistence can be useful commitment or harmful fixation, depending on what the supervisor believes.

Following ordersPursue the director’s perceived priority. A damaging result can come from a mistaken belief about what was required.
Well-intended initiativeChoose an unrequested plan believed to help. Competence, authority and knowledge determine whether it helps.
Private motiveProtect reputation, a colleague or a principle. That concern can coexist with loyalty and production goals.
SabotageIntend to obstruct or damage a particular goal. Require that intent and an available means; do not infer it from a bad outcome.
BreakdownAccumulated strain disrupts execution or deliberation: fixation, withdrawal, contradictory commands or failed follow-through. Specify the mechanism and recovery; do not roll a random villain personality.

Determinism and the model boundary

Given the same initial state, game rules, ordered inputs and recorded random draws, replay must produce the same actions and outcomes. Seeded accident variation is compatible with this; surprise to the player does not require an untraceable cause.

Keep incident randomness separate from narration and visual variation. A different caption, image choice or model-call timing must not reroll an accident. Safety affects both exposure and the possible consequences; exact distributions come later.

An LLM can propose a contextual interpretation, conversational act or plan at a meaningful decision point. Validate and save that proposal before it affects behaviour. Routine BDI deliberation, action resolution and propagation run without waiting on the model.

A fresh live model call is not guaranteed to reproduce the same output. Replay therefore consumes the recorded accepted model input, rather than calling the model again. If we require identical outcomes from seed and player choices alone, outcome-affecting model inputs must be fixed in advance or excluded.

At a defined decision boundary, accept a valid timely proposal or use a deterministic fallback. Record which input was used; a late answer must not rewrite an already resolved shift.

What the causal record must contain
  • Physical precursor and incident evidence
  • What this supervisor actually observed or was told
  • Beliefs and active concerns before the decision
  • Relevant stress, self-confidence and relationship memories
  • Intention adopted, retained or abandoned and its rule-based reason
  • Attempted action, actual result and any random draw
  • Messages sent, their audiences and subsequent belief changes

The explanation must come from the inputs and decision rules recorded when the action was selected. LLM prose can express that trace; it cannot invent a plausible motive afterward.

Visual evidence and player understanding

Resolve the event and its viewpoint before choosing an image. Match supervisor, room, action and supported visible state. A damaged conveyor plate must not imply casualties that never occurred; a thought or propaganda image must be presented as someone’s interpretation. Reuse neutral room and character art when no exact event plate fits.

Keep player evidence narrower than the author’s causal log. Warnings, stoppages, hesitation, complaints, changed reports and remembered promises make agency legible in Act 1. Brain 2.0 reveals an accumulated inner history, not retroactively invented motives.

Design checks before balancing
  • Can a safety path finish Act 1 with no accident and a different cost?
  • Can the same order produce a different plan for a documented difference in belief or history?
  • Can a supervisor make a harmful mistake while loyal, and refuse a harmful order without becoming a traitor?
  • Does changing the private response affect later conduct without rewriting the accident?
  • Can disclosure reach only some audiences, and can an attempted rumour fail?
  • Can repeated reassurance fail when the director’s actions contradict it?
  • Does a recorded run replay identically without fresh model calls?
  • Can every displayed event image and narrated motive be justified by the recorded event or viewpoint?

The author or developer can inspect the full trace. Act 1 players see only their available evidence and reports. Brain 2.0 may expose deeper mental state according to the agreed reveal; it does not grant the player an omniscient debug console by accident.

How an arc is assembled
  • Track unresolved commitments, hazards, grievances and knowledge gaps as continuing threads. Group events by these links, not merely by occurring on the same day.
  • Recognize a turning point when a decision changes an intention, relationship or operating arrangement. Show the earlier promise or pressure that makes the change meaningful.
  • Allow competence, restraint, cooperation and recovery to form satisfying episodes. Avoiding a disaster must not produce an empty report or provoke a forced new crisis.
  • Let the episode layer choose emphasis, pacing and viewpoints after events are recorded. It cannot add a betrayal, reroll damage or change motives to improve the ending.
  • Treat a revelation as new access to an existing fact or recorded private act. It may change a character’s judgment of history; it must not rewrite that history.
  • If a run is dull, revise the underlying pressures, available commitments or presentation during design. Do not secretly punish a successful player to manufacture drama.
The quality bar

A strong episode lets the viewer recognize each supervisor, follow what each stood to lose, identify a consequential decision and trace the aftermath. A polished recap without those connections fails the Producer Vision.

  • Select an action: can we show its actual decision inputs without inventing a motive?
  • Select a claim: can we trace its audience and source, including failed transmission?
  • Select a later refusal: can we point to the earlier order, injury, promise or interpretation that matters?
  • Select a recap beat or image: which recorded event or clearly marked viewpoint supports it?
  • Review a safe, competent run: does it show meaningful character choices and a coherent arc without forced sabotage?
  • Change only presentation: do the underlying event history and outcome remain identical?

Engine depth

How much causally relevant state exists: individual workers, wear, stress, beliefs, intentions, relationships and memory.

Player knowledge

Which facts, reports and interpretations the director can access. This depends on observation, disclosure and progression.

True system complexity, revealed information and the effort required to play are different things. Brain 2.0 can reveal more of an existing society without making every hidden variable a gauge. Keep communication as simple as the decision allows, and judge the result by the playing experience.
Simple communication for meaningful and enjoyable play

Players should understand why they are choosing, what they are authorizing and how to read the result. The simulation can contain much more than the interface reveals. Its depth earns its place by creating decisions and consequences that are enjoyable to play.

Make the central choice visibleDay one: handover and weekly quota → choose adviser → hear priority and first arrangement → accept or override → run → permanently allocate output. Later days substitute yesterday’s results for the opening handover.
Say what the action meansUse concrete labels such as Accept Stiletto’s arrangement or Stop the Brewery for Witch’s repair. Explain the known operational sacrifice and who is being overruled. Avoid vague dialogue stances whose mechanical commitment is impossible to infer.
Show authority before consequencesMark the adviser’s assigned room as automatically managed. Other-room response decisions clearly show their recommendation and the player’s override. A token move changes a named person’s work and may change delegated authority; make both meanings visible.
Reveal what helps the next decisionKeep immediate facts, stated reasons, known costs and relevant uncertainty close to the action. Let hidden beliefs, stress, relationships and accumulated history operate beneath it. Do not expose every variable or require players to reconstruct the whole system.
Let consequences teachShow a recognizable change in work, a specific reaction or a remembered incident. A brief later explanation connects it to the choice. Delayed revelations deepen the player’s understanding without pretending the missing information was available earlier.
Enjoyment is the acceptance criterionA readable interface and a consistent simulation are necessary but do not establish that the game is fun. The first end-to-end prototype must be played and judged by the owner; revise the loop around that experience before expanding it for a real audience.

In the first playtest, ask the owner whether choosing an adviser feels consequential, their plan is worth considering, overrides are tempting despite their cost, the factory is enjoyable to watch and the result creates interest in the next day. Rust-native means enforceable discipline

Explicit dataStable entity IDs, typed components and tagged commands/events. Model absence and failure explicitly. Store references by ID rather than passing mutable object graphs between systems.
Controlled ownershipSystems receive bounded read views and emit command buffers. An ordered commit phase owns writes. Declare read/write sets before considering parallel execution.
Deterministic transitionsFix system order, tie-breaking and random streams. Keep wall clocks, network calls and renderer state outside the kernel. Record external inputs before they affect a step.
Portable arithmeticDefine integer bounds, overflow, rounding, fixed-point or quantized values, canonical ordering and serialization. JavaScript numbers and Python integers do not automatically match Rust semantics.
Explicit failureValidation produces typed acceptance or rejection with a reason. Missing context, stale commands and failed model requests take named paths rather than silently mutating state.
Prove the migration seamRun headless fixtures and invariant checks from the start. A future Rust implementation must match the same command outcomes, events and canonical state hashes. Types alone do not guarantee equivalence.

What makes a replay deterministic

A replay is defined by initial state, rules version, random seed and ordered accepted external inputs—including model contributions and admission/fallback decisions. Replaying those records is deterministic. Calling a live model again is not promised to reproduce them. A stricter seed-and-player-inputs-only mode must freeze or exclude outcome-affecting model contributions.

Late responses cannot alter committed history. Requests carry their context revision and admissible boundary; the host records acceptance, rejection or fallback. Slow verbal delivery can arrive later as presentation only when it cannot change the choice or its meaning.

A concrete prototype without premature infrastructure

A practical first implementation can use TypeScript for the headless kernel, a separate model adapter service and the web client. Modules and explicit message boundaries are enough initially; this does not require a fleet of microservices. Rust, another viewer or another model service can later replace one side against the same conformance fixtures.

How we prove the boundaries
  • Replay one fixture headlessly and through both visual views; authoritative results match.
  • Disable or delay the model service; the factory continues with recorded fallback behaviour.
  • Run without a graphics context; production, knowledge and supervisor actions still resolve.
  • Recover a viewer from a fresh snapshot and ordered diffs without changing the run.
  • Verify that an Act 1 client never receives hidden worker statistics or privileged causal traces.
  • Measure visual performance independently from simulation correctness and model quality.

The small economy

  • Begin with one money-like resource and counts of basic and smart workers. Money funds development; daily operating purchases are not established.
  • The quota is due at the end of the week. Every day, allocate completed robots to the factory or the quota; the confirmed allocation is irreversible.
  • Payment timing, prices, excess deliveries and any later separate market remain open. Do not assume immediate payment, a weekly payday or daily wages from the quota deadline.
  • Smart output is more valuable and retained smart workers improve advanced throughput. Define its place in the contract before adding another allocation destination.
  • Engineering unlocks with Witch in Part 03. Repairs require engineering capability and time, with production forgone; a cash charge or purchased substitute engineer is not assumed.
  • Expose additional materials when their functions become relevant. The granularity of conveyor flow and buffers is still a systems choice; it does not require station staffing controls.

The workers beneath the count

  • Every worker has an individual state from Act 1. Counts are an aggregate view of those individuals, not a substitute for them.
  • Detailed state is visibly affected by production, supervisors and social experience before its statistics become accessible.
  • Brain 2.0 reveals what has already developed and unlocks new ways to manage it. Exact thought-access limits remain open.
  • Brain 1.5 introduces smart workers; Brain 2.0 enables the later generation. The versions and their discoveries must remain distinct.
  • Retained workers carry history into Act 2. What remains socially or mechanically relevant about dispatched workers is unresolved.

  • Provide a route to the first smart batch without requiring an existing smart workforce. Specialist-led pilot production is a candidate.

Where LLMs and BDI belong

LLM reasoning must earn its place through better play than an authored deterministic BDI baseline. Connecting observations and memories should improve a supervisor’s interpretation, proposed action or selective briefing, not merely decorate the same fixed outcome. The simulation retains authority over facts, capabilities and consequences.

BDI is a requested behavioural foundation: subjective beliefs and competing concerns lead to persistent intentions and concrete actions. The BDI section specifies the proposed accident example, knowledge boundaries, replay contract and role of recorded model inputs.

  • The simulation owns physical truth, resources, eligibility and execution. The model cannot invent deliveries, erase damage or grant an unavailable intervention.
  • A character receives only appropriate knowledge and attributed reports. Private beliefs, speech and physical actions remain distinct.
  • Promises and authorized actions must have durable representations so later behaviour can honour, reinterpret or break them coherently.
  • Use the model at meaningful encounters and reflections. Do not require a call for every worker on every tick; preserve a playable fallback and bounded cost.
  • Model changes need evaluations for continuity, knowledge boundaries, meaningful choices and faithful action. Performance targets and provider engineering belong in the later systems work.
  • Model text cannot silently create an accident or a factory-wide rumour. An admitted statement has a source and audience; the game determines who hears, believes and relays it. Generated thought must respect that history.
  • Deterministic replay consumes recorded accepted model inputs. Re-running a live model is not a determinism guarantee; the causal trace must predate any narrative explanation.
  • Compare authored reasoning, the same reasoning with LLM prose, and admitted model interpretation on the same starting situations. Require useful differences in advice or action, causal clarity, character continuity and knowledge discipline at acceptable cost and latency. Keep the simpler baseline when reasoning adds no player value.

Before implementing a shift

Decisions still worth making

The long arcs now connect to a proposed player experience. Paused planning, continuous shifts and decision pauses are agreed; balance, intervention details and the final interface still need design.

Open

Exact major forks

Safety, output and adaptive balance begin with the first adviser choice. Cathexis’s commitment and accident consequences add later forks. Their combinations form Act 1 trajectories; exact viable arrangements remain to test.

Open

The Cathexis side route

Define a concrete alternative with its own cost. The trained worker cadre is an exploration, not accepted canon. Decide the real costs and availability of humiliation, circuits and negotiated revolutionary action.

Open

Mastery gates

Define what capability each stage must demonstrate without forcing one supervisor arrangement. No turn counts or numeric thresholds are set here.

Open

Contract and money

Weekly deadlines and irreversible daily allocation are agreed. Decide when committed deliveries pay, prices, excess output and any separate later sales channel. Development uses money; daily upkeep purchases and a standard repair bill are not established.

Open

Brains and bodies

Decide whether output is accounted as completed robots or brains automatically installed in bodies. Do not inherit the old five-brains-to-one-worker exchange by accident.

Open

Advanced recipe and commissioning

Specify how substrate and weaving combine, how existing workers make the first smart batch, and whether later upgrades preserve worker identity. These are design requirements; player friction comes from staffing, throughput, cost and retention decisions.

Open

Visibility and thought access

Direct worker-state inspection arrives in Act 2. Earlier supervisors may discover or infer missing indoctrination from their own knowledge and report it during briefings. Define programme coverage, learning evidence and disclosure; retain uncertainty rather than forcing a reveal on a timer.

Open

Replacement knowledge

Keep the worker-replacement purpose of Brain 2.0 unknown to the player and workers at first-shift entry. Decide who else knows it and when the player can discover it. That discovery and the separate rumour that two new supervisors will replace two current supervisors follow distinct information paths. These are knowledge-design constraints, not obstacles the player is asked to solve.

Open

Origins of rumours and characters

Specify how reports enter and spread through the factory. The supervisors' prior histories should shape interpretation, but their detailed biographies and the town's founding are not yet fixed.

Open

Delegation and the second floor

Define first-floor handover requirements, which decisions remain connected to the director and the second floor’s functions. Cortex-stage prose describes the player’s task; exact delegation rules are still to design.

Open

Incoming supervisors

Two arrive in Act 2; selection from a larger randomized pool is tentative. Define complementary capabilities and differing story consequences before choosing a roster.

Open

Failure and recovery

Repair is unavailable and absent from player controls in Parts 01–02. Define viable reduced-capacity paths before Witch arrives, catastrophic failure conditions, and the engineering time and downtime required for later recovery.

Open

Scope of individual simulation

Preserve meaningful individual history without requiring every possible relationship or an LLM call for every robot. Exact granularity belongs in the systems phase.

Open

Final outcomes

Act 2 social dynamics determine the run's outcome. The ending structure and viable success conditions remain open; the trajectories are not an ending list.

Open

Stress and information propagation

Define exposure, report channels, credibility, rejection and lingering effects. Choose how much of a rumour’s history the player can infer in Act 1 and inspect in Act 2.

Open

Thrum’s relief and its costs

Define room-specific relief, the competing claims on productive time, and how discipline changes affect other supervisors. Preserve an almost unique positive specialty without making him a universal reset. His established role is worker relief; any effect on supervisor stress needs a separate decision.

Open

Supervisor commitments and reconsideration

Define competing concerns and persistent plans, then compare authored BDI with validated model interpretation. Add LLM reasoning where it improves playable advice and consequences enough to justify latency and cost.

Open

Episode scope and player access

Choose where a watchable episode begins and ends, which ongoing conflicts it follows, and when players may access subjective or complete traces. Avoid exposing Act 2 knowledge through an Act 1 recap.

Open

Shift interventions

Paused planning, continuous shifts and consequential decision pauses are agreed. Decide which situations interrupt, which orders are allowed during a shift, and how manual pause, speed controls and inspection work.

Open

Visual scene and device budget

Babylon.js with a controlled isometric stage is the proposed starting point. Validate one representative bay, art/geometry treatment and mobile performance before committing the full factory.

Open

Session pacing and live interventions

Test roughly three shifts per thirty-minute sitting, including paused reading. Specify the small set of live orders, their execution boundaries and which consequences require a decision pause; do not turn the attention horizons into timers.

Open

Adviser-led staffing

The daily adviser choice sets the assessment, priority, arrangement and response guidance. Their room’s events resolve automatically; the player can override placements and responses elsewhere, provoking individual relationship reactions. Design the selection guidance, event granularity, override costs, later revelation and bounded prose budget.

Continuity changes to preserve

  • The new progression starts with Limen and Stiletto. The older story-bible opening places Witch at the first jam and presents all five supervisors at entry; that scene needs a later writing revision. Witch's inspection dispute belongs after her arrival.
  • The original sim unlocks rooms by day and requires Theatre-washed brains for sale or conversion. The current design replaces those assumptions with mastery-based progression and basic output from the complete conveyor.
  • The original sim stores worker pools and room averages. Individual worker minds and Act 2 thought access are the intended extension, not existing implementation.
  • The old critical events generally use confidence, native room and an allow-or-suppress prompt. Independent refusal, worker-specific respect and political action are new design work.
  • The exact old percentages, conversions, capacities and event thresholds are not adopted. Current story and gameplay direction takes precedence over archived formulas.
  • The website's current teaching prototype is not the complete game described here. The six alternative prehistories and twenty bridge paintings remain explorations, not established canon.
  • The accident aftermath is now a major Act 1 fork alongside Cathexis. It is likely on many operating paths but must be avoidable through sustained safety; no mastery gate should demand a disaster. BDI will affect actions, extending beyond the old read-only cognitive diagnostic layer.
  • Day one has no inherited assignments. The first adviser proposes them. Quotas are weekly, daily output allocations are permanent, and engineering begins with Witch in Part 03. Earlier repair purchases and station-staffing examples are superseded.

Review the trajectories against these questions

  • Can each trajectory reach Brain 2.0 for an understandable reason, rather than because its numbers were favoured?
  • Does each major commitment have a real benefit, a distinct pitfall and at least one costly recovery?
  • Does each stage add a new decision while making previous choices matter more?
  • Can Act 2 explain an Act 1 behaviour through the same worker's existing history?
  • Would a different supervisor's beliefs or report change a meaningful decision?
  • Can we explain why an outcome happened without making every future outcome predictable?
  • Can a rumour genuinely fail, and can Thrum genuinely help, without either outcome being overridden by a prewritten crisis?
  • Does each proposed arc create a character collision whose competing commitments and causal history can be watched and inspected?

Direction and playback rules
Action before decorationSynchronize the strike with the visible contact. Mechanical settling can continue after an order is accepted; sound must never delay the command or invent a game outcome.
One foreground eventLet routine production sit underneath decisions and accidents. Coalesce repeated cues at faster playback; leave room for the factory to fall quiet.
State, not random noiseLoad, strain, jam and restart should sound related. Their timing comes from resolved world events and presentation state, independently of model calls.
Music has its own jobLater stems can carry working rhythm, mounting pressure, deliberation and aftermath. Do not turn every produced robot into a musical victory.
Bounded and optionalNo automatic preview playback. Gameplay sound is user-enabled, hidden-page work stops, and readable visual feedback carries every important state. Review-only recordings are never loaded by the playable shift.
