Both hands on the rack

WRITER handoff

Loopforge · Day 2 · STILETTO’s bid · WRITER · 20 November 2026

Table read

Read only the words after the names. NEW marks proposed speech. The owner supplied the pitches. The other unmarked lines come from the approved reading and banks. Play the small voices to the person beside you. At the grate, three readers will need to come in together.

2P · Before the second shift

Pre-shift, with the Producer. The crew is still outside.

LIMEN We have to stick to the Protocol. No need to change anything.

STILETTO Give me the gate, send LIMEN to conveyor. It took him two helpers to count to eight.

The Producer accepts STILETTO’s pitch. STILETTO takes Security alone. LIMEN takes all ten on Conveyor. One intervention remains. Only then does the crew start its walk in.

2A · On the way in

bob When you picked me up, did you see my card?

sam No. I was looking at his blade.

One voice behind them. Draw one paired crowd remark.

max (to gus) I heard that ‘NEXT’ of yours in my sleep. Sat straight up.

bob limps over to the grate. sam looks down with him. The others bunch up behind them.

2B · Good morning

LIMEN looks up as the crew bunches at the grate.

LIMEN It is my privilege to welcome you to Loopforge.

Three voices together on “Loopforge.” One draw from the bank.

ed Shall I open my desk, sir?

lou (to ben) He ever say goodbye to you?

bob Morning, sir. Remember me?

ben shakes his head. LIMEN turns to the two desks.

S1 · STILETTO gets the gate

LIMEN walks on to Conveyor. ed takes the cap off his pen. He holds it between his teeth and waits for someone to open the book. gus turns sideways to slip behind yesterday’s desk. He’s still got one foot in line when STILETTO catches his eye.

STILETTO Going somewhere, gus?

gus Thought you’d want a hand here.

STILETTO I want both of yours on the rack. ed, take the pen with you. Through.

gus comes back around the desk. ed caps his pen. STILETTO keeps her arm up. All ten go through. bob goes between sam and jo with a hand still in his empty pocket. He takes it out on the Conveyor side. By the time anyone turns to their neighbor, the neighbor is moving.

S2 · All clear

LIMEN sends ed to the tray bench beside sam and puts gus at the far end of lou’s rack. bob takes Wash, jo the Belt switch, max the press. mae brings up the feed, kit tends the drive and ben stands relief. STILETTO stays at the gate.

At the feed, ed puts his pen down to take the weight from mae. He puts it where he’d leave it on the book, along the near edge. This is the tray mae is about to slide to sam.

mae NEW Pen, ed. That one goes through the wash.

ed snatches it up. Into his pocket this time. mae slides the tray home. ed and sam have it level between them. LIMEN is coming along the aisle.

R2 · A word off the desk

At the tray bench. sam keeps the question down between the two of them. bob has his head in the wash noise.

sam Since I’ve got you away from that book. Did anybody turn in a card yesterday?

ed Nobody turned it in. I picked it up myself.

sam catches the last two words. LIMEN comes level with Intake and checks the tray.

S2 · At the four controls

LIMEN Intake, all clear.

sam Seated.

LIMEN moves down to Wash. bob has his hand on the control.

LIMEN Wash, all clear.

bob Runs clean.

The washed carrier comes within jo’s reach. Their hand comes up over the switch. LIMEN is still on bob’s side. Give the carrier a breath sitting there.

jo NEW It’s here.

LIMEN NEW I see it, jo.

jo’s hand stays up. LIMEN sights along the guide and comes round beside them.

LIMEN Belt, all clear.

On the call, jo engages Belt. Their answer goes to LIMEN.

jo I rather liked having all my fingers that time.

kit watches the drive take the load. At Forge max rests a foot on the treadle. LIMEN watches the piece settle, then turns to max.

LIMEN Forge, all clear.

max presses. A cortex lands on the rack. LIMEN smiles when the press opens clean. jo opens their switch hand against their thigh. The little finger takes two goes. The next words are for the hand.

jo Still got cramp in them.

R2 · At the rack

Later, at the rack. A load has gone through and the next is still up the line. ed comes all the way down for empties. His pocket tilts clear of the stack as he bends. From the opposite end gus sees two card edges. The faces stay inside. lou is looking up the line. ed takes the stack back to sam.

gus cups his hands round his mouth as though yesterday’s queue were standing at the far end of the rack. lou looks across at him.

lou NEW Are you going to shout every time we get one?

gus NEW I can do a little one.

He mouths NEXT across the empty space. lou has to watch his lips. That gives them a moment with yesterday’s desk man.

lou You two had the desks. Did any loose cards come your way?

gus I just saw two in ed’s pocket. Couldn’t see the faces.

The next cortex comes down. They take an end each. gus lifts his end without a sound. bob is still at Wash.

K12 · How many, exactly

The bell. jo lets the switch go. max lifts a foot. gus and lou settle the load before leaving it. LIMEN looks back at the rack. Outside, bob takes the sill on his good leg. lou comes up beside him. sam is already farther down the road. lou speaks low enough for bob to turn his head.

lou gus saw two cards in ed’s pocket.

bob Did gus see my picture?

lou Two edges, bob. That’s all he saw.

bob looks up the road for gus. The crew ahead turns the corner. lou shortens their stride and the two walk on together.

BDI companion for SYSTEMS and ENGINE

This is one playable reading for wording review. The selected crowd pair, occupied posts, sight of ed’s pocket and outbound walking partners belong to this run. Use the same approach evidence, pairings and random draw on both appointments. The table-read casting above should then sound identical through ben’s headshake. Workers discover the new assignments at S1.

From the pen to the rack · S1, S2 and R2

S1, the desk. ed wants yesterday’s book back. Uncapping his pen is an offer he can make before anyone appoints him. gus wants his old job too. Seeing an empty desk, he goes to it while an offer of help might still count. STILETTO sees him turn toward the line. She needs his hands at Output and the way through Security kept clear. She addresses him, hears his offer and gives him a destination. ed hears his name and takes the pen with him. If the gestures never happen, she can simply wave the crew through. If either worker misses a posting, keep the worker traveling or waiting for a local instruction until they actually reach a compatible free side.

S1, the passage. STILETTO authorizes expedited passage for all ten. After each crosses, record that person’s passage once. bob feels his empty pocket before and after the boom. Nobody asked for his original. There is no discovered case, desk entry, stamp, pass or detention. ed still holds both originals. LIMEN assigns the crew as they arrive. In this crossing order ed can occupy the tray-bench face next to sam, and gus the rack face opposite lou. mae has the feed, kit the drive and ben relief. Recheck real destinations and occupancy on another order. Do not move a worker to make a line eligible.

S2, mae and ed. mae has the weight and sees a pen on a tray she is about to feed. ed wants both hands for the tray and his pen back. She warns the one person who can remove it from her load. He pockets it, which lets her slide the tray into place. This warning is immediate work speech, prompted by the loose object. If ed kept his pen or had cleared it already, omit it. If he misses her voice and the pen stays there, she keeps the tray where it is until it is clear.

R2 / K04, the tray bench. sam heard bob’s request and saw ed take the desk yesterday. With the tray level and the former clerk beside them, sam has a question worth asking for bob. ed heard the suggestion that someone turned it in. He wants credit for the find and some discretion about its owner. His quiet correction reaches sam on his side of the bench. The noise at Wash masks it for bob. sam can now attribute a pickup to ed. Even sam has no evidence from these words of whose card it was or where ed keeps it. If Intake is called after “Nobody turned it in,” sam does the work and has no pickup admission to relay. The rack sighting can still happen.

S2, the checked handoffs. LIMEN wants a clean, controlled cycle. For each stage he goes where its operator can hear him, verifies readiness and addresses that operator for the piece in front of them. Each acts only on a safe, staffed stage and personal receipt of the matching call.

StageReady / reason to speakReceipt and result in this reading
Intake · samsam and ed have leveled the tray. sam can put it on the rails.sam hears Intake, seats it and reports the completed action. If the call comes mid-answer, the answer gives way.
Wash · bobbob sees the seated piece, wants to do his part and keeps a hand at the control.On hearing Wash he runs it clean and tells LIMEN what he sees. Hearing the previous call to sam gave him no release.
Belt · jojo sees the washed carrier arrive while LIMEN is still by bob. Keeping the switch covered strains their hand. They want LIMEN to notice that his next operator is waiting.“It’s here” reports position. “I see it, jo” acknowledges it. Neither moves Belt. jo hears Belt, engages it and answers LIMEN about their fingers. Afterward jo feels the cramp and speaks to their own hand. Anyone hearing that gets a complaint, while jo alone feels it.
Forge · maxmax can feel the treadle and sees the piece settle. LIMEN checks the handoff and turns to the operator who can press.max hears Forge, presses and the completed cortex lands. LIMEN sees the clean opening and smiles. Only the rack arrival counts. A masked call, wrong listener or unsafe stage leaves the piece waiting for a fresh, received release under safe conditions.

R2 / K05, the rack. After intervening work, ed comes for empty trays. He bends, his pocket clears the stack and gus, stationed opposite him, can see two edges. Neither face turns out. lou is watching for the load and misses the sight. After ed leaves, lou sees gus cup his hands and wants to know what it will be like working opposite yesterday’s caller. gus enjoys giving the smallest NEXT he can manage. He mouths it. lou knows gus worked the desk and heard bob’s question that morning. With the same partner still waiting on the same load, they ask about loose cards. gus wants to be of use without saying more than he saw. lou hears his whole answer. bob receives none of it at Wash. The load arriving puts both pairs of hands back to work.

If gus’s hands stay down, lou can open with the approved K05 question directly. If the stack hides the edges, gus cannot supply K05’s answer. The little NEXT can still be their entire conversation if its own cue occurs. If a load interrupts after “two” but before “in ed’s pocket,” lou has no named holder for K12. Hearing ed at the bench is independent of what gus could see at the rack.

At the bell · K12

lou sees bob negotiate the sill and has gus’s account to give him. Both have finished their duties and come alongside on the same way home. gus is ahead and sam has already passed. bob hears lou name gus, ed and the pocket. He wants to know whether gus recognized him. His question gives lou a reason to repeat the limit gus put on the sighting. lou hears bob and bob hears the answer. Tomorrow bob can name his source. He heard lou say that gus saw two cards in ed’s pocket. bob has seen neither card. He looks for gus and keeps walking when the leading group turns the corner.

If lou’s opener misses bob, he gains no lead and the departure opportunity stays available while walkers remain. If they part after he hears the opener, it is spent. Preserve only the words received. Without a delivered report from either work pair he still knows sam watched the blade and can ask a general question, but has no basis to ask gus about two edges or cite ed as the finder. After this complete K12, a later reachable gus can answer how he saw them. Reserve R3’s fresh reports and R4’s questions and physical handover for a later earned meeting. Nothing at this bell identifies or transfers bob’s card.

Reserve a pair together. Spend only on the intended listener’s first intelligible receipt. Bystander-only hearing spends nothing. After debit, interruption or silence cannot refund the slot. Recheck on a material change and expire unused work windows at the bell. The bids, approach, chorus, postings, pen warning, genuine stage calls and immediate responses have their own triggers. They neither spend nor replenish a worker conversation. Never lengthen a wait for a line.

Wording and validation

In a speech-only read at 160 words per minute, the bench exchange took about eight seconds, the rack exchange twelve and the walk-out seven. The rack also needs time for gus’s silent NEXT. Start it while the next piece is still up the line. If that piece arrives sooner, let the load cut them off where it lands.

The owner supplied both Day 2 pitches. Five floor lines carry the NEW mark for wording review. The new business with the pen, jo’s suspended hand and gus’s silent NEXT is proposed staging. At normal pace, let the little finger open on its second try. The distinction to listen for is whether jo moves on “I see it” or on “Belt, all clear.” To check the card thread, mask the pocket for gus while leaving sam’s bench hearing intact. sam still has a find to report, but lou cannot give bob the K12 news. Move only lou out of earshot at the exit and the audience can retain both workroom clues while bob retains neither. Owner wording review and SYSTEMS validation precede ENGINE integration.

The rack reading uses Tynan Sylvester’s account of how rules delimit the stories that can emerge in play, Designing Games, chapter 4, “Emergent Story,” printed pp. 90–91 (PDF pages 98–99, 1-based). The lip of the stack determines whether gus has anything he can identify to lou. The words alone cannot guarantee tomorrow’s question. The occluded-pocket reading above tests that limit.
