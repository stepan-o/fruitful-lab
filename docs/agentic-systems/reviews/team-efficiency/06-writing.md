## 1. The chapter needs an explicit team-design payoff for its search argument

**Location:** `apps/lab/lib/agentic-systems/revised-chapters/06.json:9`, especially the transition through the diversity studies to the factory-treatment paragraph at line 109.

The generator–evaluator–selection–archive argument is sound. FunSearch, AlphaEvolve, and the two human story-writing studies give it substance. But almost the entire argument also applies to one agent making repeated proposals. The opening promises a decision about a team; the body does not quite establish when paying for separate contributors is preferable to asking the same generator again. The three factory treatments are the natural place to finish that thought.

Add a brief, concrete piece of the author's reasoning: an example in which separate bounded commissions buy useful simultaneous exploration, what shared brief and acceptance record those contributors receive, and when a single owner takes over after selection. State the hoped-for gain and the review/integration cost that could defeat it. Present that as a proposed production design unless the case record actually demonstrates it. This would give the chapter a positive account of how a team can help without upgrading generation or diversity findings into evidence of team efficacy.

## 2. The narrative-obligations section spends too many paragraphs restating its central discovery

**Location:** `06.json:157–200`, from “Choosing an activity changes the scene’s history” through “That is the adoption cost of a richer candidate.”

The scene has a strong literary insight: an attractive ending can quietly commission a promise. But the same point arrives as spent-hour bookkeeping, another writer's limited permission, a three-item list of unsupported claims, the author's warm-ending temptation, and the final adoption-cost paragraph. The sentence-bench caption also establishes the appointment obligation before two following paragraphs explain it again. Repetition makes the section feel more like cumulative guidance than an unfolding essay, and its length pulls the chapter toward narrative-system implementation.

Keep the Kit/Lou moment, Short's concise structure, the author's personally revealing warm-ending paragraph, and the concrete Façade example. Compress the repeated prohibitions around them, letting the final move explain what this means for assigning and accepting contributions across an agent team. That would preserve the story while sharpening the book's subject.
