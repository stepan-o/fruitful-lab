Exact proposed provider brief · LF-MUS-PILOT-MENU-02

Original instrumental golden-era rolling drum and bass, 164 BPM, 4/4, approximately 128 fast bars. Play a dedicated Main menu cycle conveying curiosity with a pulse, the feeling of discovering an unfamiliar place online at night. Warm, close electric keys and tactile drums with clear ghost strokes, soft electric sub bass, generous space. Open with four quick notes A–G–F–E over low D, land on C-sharp and let it hang while the break continues. Move the inner voice of a D minor ninth toward a D minor sixth/ninth. Give the descent a distinct ascending melodic reply, with its own syncopated rhythm and a late final note. Carry it through B-flat major ninth and G minor ninth, then let a sparse A dominant chord point home. Write eight sixteen-bar sections. Establish the theme, introduce the reply, turn the harmony and displace the kick, give the kit an exposed solo, bring the keys back quietly in a new register, let the full break gather weight, clear the room for bass alone, then bring back the original falling figure. Let the final groove lead directly into the first downbeat. Keep the tempo steady through the beat shifts. No vocals, spoken words, samples of existing recordings, ending fanfare or long fade.

Single attempt. Propose a single 188-second instrumental in ElevenLabs Music v2.5, one variation, zero automatic retries. That allows a 187.317-second grid plus a small editing margin. Use the ElevenCreative music generator on the eligible account, select v2.5 and one variant, and inspect the actual credit quote before submission. The connected workflow also lists eleven_music_v2_5 with a text prompt, duration_seconds from 3 to 600, lyrics_type=instrumental and instrumental=true. Tool discovery confirms model settings only. Rights checks before production and release

Eleven Music Terms, updated 9 October 2026, are the source for these current constraints: Free lists 11 generation minutes, no downloads/API, and attribution; Starter lists API access. High-quality downloads start at Pro in that table. Verify actual account availability.

The terms prohibit artist/song/album/label names and distinctive lyrics in prompts. Reference names belong in research, not submitted prompts. Output permissions depend on plan and game classification. The Indie Game definition uses both game revenue below $500,000 and developer/publisher game revenue below $1 million, with further accounting conditions; verify qualification rather than assuming it. Studio Games require appropriate rights. Music-library redistribution is separately restricted. An upgrade can cover existing account outputs under §1.5; retain evidence rather than assuming either automatic clearance or a permanent Free-origin ban.

Recheck these terms, model-specific conditions and account eligibility before generation and release. Resolve uncertainty with provider support or appropriate legal advice. This is a production checklist, not a legal assurance.

A commercially usable whole product

The owner's requirement is that the complete Loopforge stack can be sold. MUSIC DIRECTOR owns audio-chain clearance and records any new music tool/runtime dependencies; PRODUCER reconciles the whole-product inventory with ENGINE and other crafts. “Free-to-sell” means supported commercial distribution, not necessarily free production, exclusivity or freedom from every continuing license obligation.

Maintain a release register with one entry per source/output/derivative and dependency: identifier, source URL and author/provider, acquisition date, exact license/terms version, evidence location, permitted platforms and distribution, modification/embedding rights, attribution, restrictions, expiration or revenue conditions, derivative parent IDs, reviewer and clearance status. Do not silently replace unknown with approved.

For human recordings and samples, check composition and recording rights separately where applicable. For code, inspect the actual dependency license and bundled example media; source-code permission does not clear sample audio. Do not use reference masters, ripped film dialogue, unlicensed breakbeats or dataset material with missing provenance. Do not repackage provider music as a public general-purpose sample library merely because it can be embedded in our game. Keep editable masters and internal production assets distinct from shipped game exports.

The proposed release gate is concrete: every shipped audio asset and proposed music dependency has a resolved commercial-use entry, required notices are included, restricted assets are excluded or replaced, and the sold build can run without a buyer having an ElevenLabs account or a live generation call. Track obligations that survive release. A Music Director review covers its audio scope, not an unsupported certification of every existing game asset.

Approve and record a finite batch

Before generation, present: target question, outputs and durations, alternatives, maximum attempts, selected model and plan, available quota, generation/export costs, subscription/renewal/overage conditions, proposed hard spending ceiling, rights basis and stop condition. Ask the owner only for unresolved scope and spending authority. Never purchase a premium plan, enable overage or expand a batch on an inferred budget.

Keep a ledger for planned and completed requests: UTC timestamp, request ID, prompt version, model, duration, variants, credit or dollar unit, estimated and actual charge, accepted/rejected result, retry reason, output hash, export path and rights-register ID. Preserve unknown costs as unknown; distinguish provider credits from USD. Reconcile the account balance before/after each approved batch. Stop at the authorized ceiling or unresolved billing discrepancy and deliver the saved work.
