Astafiev and the felt world

When the recruits fall into step

A sound reaches a trainload of new recruits before they can see who is making it. In the first chapter of Cursed and Killed, Astafiev lets us mishear it with them. There might be a dying creature out there. What might be a creature gives way to what might be a forest making noises in the frost. The sentences keep offering the ear something to make of the sound. The fog stays put. Then a boy up on a luggage rack moves his cap off his ear. For the first time somebody can actually do something about our desire to hear better. Book One, chapter one, Russian text

The sound resolves into men singing. A lesser passage could stop there with the mystery solved. Astafiev carries it out of the wagon. The recruits can pick out songs they learned in school. One song drowns out the others. Men who had been straggling start marching in time before they notice they have done it. By the time Lyoshka is in the barracks the song is still going on in him. A boy has gone from trying to hear something to obeying it. We have felt some of its pull ourselves. That gives the narrator standing to speak of submission when the word finally comes.

There are two ways to write the click behind max’s ear. If its job is to identify the mechanism, give the reader a clear shot of the joint. If its job is to introduce bob, let it catch his ear. He leans closer. The real LIMEN has time to speak and the kid is still looking at the imitation. The risk in staying with bob that long is precisely what makes his curiosity dear. We would quite like to know how max did it, too. The old studio opener made him ask directions. Everybody in the audience wanted that information. Here we get to want something because bob does. A sergeant with nothing left to correct

On his round of the barracks Yashkin hears someone praying and orders him to stop, на всякий случай — just in case. The little phrase comes from the narrator. Yashkin can go on giving the order in perfect earnest. A moment later the narration observes that everybody is doing nothing, so the sergeant soon runs out of corrections. Even a man this well suited to finding fault needs raw material. At the stove his back still shivers. One could laugh at his officiousness and quite sincerely wish someone would get him warm. Astafiev has spent enough time with the man to do both. Yashkin’s round in chapter one

Game writing has other means of putting that second voice beside the official one. During STILETTO’s threat, max turns his face towards the noise. LIMEN holds the photograph up beside the turned face. An earlier pass gave him “Eyes here, please.” That line straightens max up and makes the moment an ordinary reprimand. Keep the photograph there and the audience can see the lengths to which LIMEN will go to conduct a perfectly normal check. The man with the card need never suspect he’s funny.

There’s also a limit to borrowing Astafiev’s luxuriance. An abandoned Wash pass let a bead of black swell in the seam of an apparently clean brain. It made us watch the seam, anticipate another discharge and expect the discovery to matter to bob. The work that actually comes back to trouble his body is the loader stroke. His knee gives and he finishes it sitting down. The direction worth lingering over is the one that changes how this particular worker can get the job done.
