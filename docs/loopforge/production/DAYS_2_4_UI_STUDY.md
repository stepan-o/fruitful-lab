# Days 2–4 pixel interface study

Art Director · 19 November 2026 · Phone desk interaction and parallel artist production approved.

## The first phone proof

LIMEN has stopped bob at the desk. From a 320 CSS px phone view, the player should be able to see who is speaking, why the queue has stopped, and whether they can still issue a pass. If they keep watching, the shift continues. A player who looks at Conveyor instead should be able to discover the practical consequence through station coverage and an attributed account. A hidden inventory entry or a private decision trace cannot supply that information to the player.

## Approved phone interaction

When LIMEN discovers bob’s missing credential, a quiet signal appears at the ID desk. The floor keeps moving. Tapping the signal opens the Security panel and keeps bob, LIMEN and the queue in view. Opening, closing and reopening the panel while the case is active are free. Only an accepted “Issue daily pass” action spends the shift intervention.

| What happens in the shift | What the art must communicate |
| --- | --- |
| LIMEN rushes clearance before checking bob | The queue moves. There is no missing-ID signal or report for this check. |
| LIMEN discovers the discrepancy while the intervention is available | A desk signal with a distinct shape and a focusable 44 × 44 CSS px hit area. Opening it shows the reason for the hold, the pass action and a way back to the floor. |
| The player issues the pass in time | On ENGINE confirmation, show the pass with bob’s name and its shift validity. The gate admits him and his subsequent movement follows the simulation. The original remains with ed. Player-facing reports reveal only what their witnesses know. |
| The case closes without a pass | LIMEN detains bob for the rest of the shift. An open panel changes to the resolved account. A player who returns later can read that account. Retire the action and preserve any unused intervention. |
| The intervention was already spent elsewhere | The desk can still signal a report when the discrepancy is discovered. Make the report readable and identify the spent intervention. Detention and any station vacancy follow the actual shift. |
| The bell rings | Show expiry or release according to bob’s state. The next shift offers a fresh intervention. A later check can expose the unresolved credential again. |

## Build sequence and acceptance

1. **Prove Security at phone size.** Compose the same discovered case at 320 and 390 CSS px with the signal closed, the panel open, a pass issued and detention recorded. Check that the player can identify the speaker, the cause of the hold and the available action with sound off. Keep the desk and the queue legible when the panel opens. Also check the skipped-check and spent-intervention cases against the table.
2. **Complete the five surfaces.** Apply the approved B lighting and existing console materials to the morning bids, concurrent room work, reusable intervention panel, credential/custody props and shift close. Give ENGINE the alert and panel as separate parts. Keep gameplay text editable and package the reviewer’s actor inspector separately from the player-facing art.
3. **Package and publish for review.** Pair editable masters with native exports, a state/anchor manifest and matched desktop/phone proofs. Cross-reference real ENGINE samples where available and label any proposed binding. Add the indexed pack and evidence to the calibration gallery, update the authored ART DIRECTOR handoff and regenerate the reading copies. Deliver the scoped PR and verified preview for owner review.

Use matching starting states for pass/detention, completed/interrupted chat, all-clear/rushed handoffs, and public backing with a worker still waiting. Check sound-off reading on warm and cool floors at 1280 × 720, 390 × 844 and 320 px wide. Check the 768 px layout and focus order when integrated. Keep dates, names and output live. A reader should be able to identify who acted, what changed and which choice is still open from the player view. Label each comparison with revision, viewport, scale and provenance. Final visual acceptance is in the complete live 2D game.

Tynan Sylvester, *Designing Games*, chapter 9, “Interface,” section “Redundancy,” printed pp. 230–231 (PDF pages 238–239, counting the cover as 1). A small cue can be missed while the player watches another actor. Pair the pass silhouette with its shift-validity text, and detention at Security with an uncovered station when the simulation reports it. The existing 320 px comparison above tested the limitation of the prop alone. The proposed sound-off pairs will test the new treatment.
