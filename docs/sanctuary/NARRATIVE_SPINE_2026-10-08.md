## Argument and evidence boundaries

Chapter 2 keeps the illustrated chain and market map together: the same creative work can sustain several businesses because they supply different parts of the opportunity to play. Its short prose separates game access from computing and introduces combined versus separate providers.

Chapter 3, `three-ecosystems`, opens with Sony selling the PS5 below manufacturing cost and earning through the larger business around it. The financial selector, Microsoft acquisition portfolio and NVIDIA comparison stay together. Only total scale and consoles’ roughly one-fifth share remain in the opening prose; category detail belongs to the chart. Subscription and CD PROJEKT’s catalog agreement close the comparison and lead into the offer inside Candy Crush.

Chapter 4 follows King’s browser origins, ongoing creative work, purchases inside play, acquisitions and distribution costs. Valve follows in chapter 5. Subscriptions and freemium coexist; chapter order does not establish historical causation.

The companies share late-1990s game milestones, not identical founding dates or exclusive business models. Valve still makes games; Epic operates games as well as tools and distribution; Rockstar combines authored releases and continuing online production. Their next customers and commitments differ.

Diablo is the continuous historical thread: inherited role-playing ideas become immediate action; the purchased adventure supports repeated play; D2 expands the journey and later formalizes seasonal ladder competition; D3 exposes a conflict between obtaining equipment and enjoying the hunt; D4 combines a campaign with a continuing production calendar. Gauntlet then shows that selling continued life is older than these worlds. Replayability never serves as a synonym for recurring revenue.

The final acts follow what the player is trying to do, how rules structure that activity, what purchases change, and what evidence would distinguish a successful offer from displacement, pressure or friction. Public sources establish mechanisms and documented decisions. Illustrative instruments are labeled models, not estimates of private performance. Dated offers and screenshots do not promise present catalog availability.

New original history scenes and comparison controls share the established stone, brass, ember and muted teal palette. Motion is CSS based and pauses offscreen, on hidden pages, under the global motion preference and with reduced motion. Source images use responsive, locally hosted derivatives; rights records state the source, owner, analytical purpose and limitations without claiming blanket permission.

The production/engine chapter includes Loopforge using the project’s own assets. Original art and primary-source visual citations serve specific arguments rather than decorating chapter boundaries.

## The mobile bridge

The ecosystem overview introduces three acquired audiences with official brand art, keeping the makers visible. King explains how the next purchase can emerge inside a work people already enjoy, and why earning again requires preserving that relationship. Its platform bill makes Valve’s move into running a store a concrete next question. Mobile is a business model case, not a claim that casual games are simple or that Diablo copied King.
