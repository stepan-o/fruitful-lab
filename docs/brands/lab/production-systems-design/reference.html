  1. 01

    Opening / identity

    The experiment conveyor.

    The Turk sits behind the cabinet and stamps briskly. Paired A/B specimens ride jointed chessboard slats on a framed conveyor, with end drums, a returning lower run, fixed bearing blocks and bolted supports. A guarded chain links the head drum to the cabinet shaft. The paper register advances with every test, with subtle gray, red and green highlights. Clearly readable p-values and verdicts hold briefly before dissolving into smoke: noisy effects, losses, a rare large gain and occasional hindsight. There is no text beneath the opening illustration.

    Many experiments. A few useful signals.

The benchmark for every subsequent scene · v2.3

One light, real depth.

A broad upper-left key connects caster to receiving surface. Foot contacts stay tight; distant shadows soften. Pierced wheel shadows turn while their light-facing bevels remain steady.

Green leads.

Muted deep green in headings, links, navigation and selected controls. Warm charcoal carries prose; brass belongs to fittings.

Results read first.

Signed lift, then p-value, then verdict. Subtle color indicates direction; uncertainty and later judgment remain distinct.

Measured pace.

Slow, Medium, Fast. About 2.18 seconds per test by default; Fast delivers one every 0.8 seconds. The whole mechanism changes speed together; a pause remains a pause.

Honest metaphor.

The switch illustrates pace, not a statistical simulator. Fixed-traffic precision and multiple-testing policy are explained with sources.

Production browser evidence and checks. Figure 1 is complete and accepted. Figure 2 now has its own focused composition pass; Figures 3–8 still await refinement.

The judgment within.

Figure 2 · an original, intimate cutaway

Character and construction.

Racknitz informs the engraved likeness. HNF's reconstruction notes inform the working arrangement and flag errors in Racknitz's layout; the museum's head and exterior photographs clarify the turban, face and layered costume.

Reach before ornament.

Shoulders, elbow and wrist form a compact working gesture. Tapered sleeves follow the limbs; long folds describe cloth. The resting arm stays proportional to the torso and the upper board fits the seated figure.

One position, three views.

Both boards repeat e2–e4: grip, lift, advance, place and release. The operator pulls a lever; the connected Turk arm carries out the same move. Position indicators follow the occupied squares.

A different light.

A bright candle on a brass wall bracket warms the face and hands inside a green recess. Its enlarged shadow shifts opposite the flame, keeping the source, person and receiving wall aligned. The wood and brass belong to the accepted machine's material family.

Quiet motion.

One shared 12-second move connects both hands, both pawns, lever and output arm. The head and torso remain still. Nine meshing gears turn at different speeds; their fixed bevels face the candle. Candle and shadow share their own restrained cycle.

An editorial interpretation, not an exact historical plan. Proportions, linked move and responsive validation.

Casework finish · 4 October 2026

Continuous edges.

Base moldings wrap around the side on the conveyor's depth plane. Short turned feet ground the case.

A working door.

Two pin hinges, a visible door edge and a small hanging pull make the opening credible. The conveyor knee has its own mounting plate on the fixed central stile, clear of the door.

Quiet walnut.

Fibres follow each board; nested grain belongs to the raised panel. Thin inlay and bevels reward a closer look.

Open machinery.

The deeper frame preserves the whole eleven-wheel movement, paper register and chain connection.
