# First five days of Loopforge

17 October 2026 · Owner direction and proposed five-day implementation. Three isolated scene rehearsals are now implemented; a playable five-day run is not yet delivered.

The first five days establish a small factory society the player can recognize and influence. Security and Conveyor stay the only operating rooms; LIMEN and STILETTO stay the only supervisors. The player learns a few consequential actions while repeated encounters acquire history. A successful fifth day leaves a recognizable operating style, identifiable workers, a material commitment and an unresolved social question for Theatre and CATHEXIS to inherit.

The longer target is a real weeks 1–2 playtest. This five-day sub-arc sits inside the proposed 5–9-day opening phase. Theatre enters after these first five completed days, when the later readiness conditions are met. A day-five handoff can prepare her arrival; it does not require opening another room during this test. The end of week two closes the opening sub-arc within Act 1, not all of Act 1. Dates and gates beyond day five remain tuning proposals.

## What the player is trying to do

Get a functioning line through the first week, satisfy the inherited quota and decide what kind of factory to keep. The pleasure comes from making work flow, recognizing personalities, backing someone and seeing the world acknowledge the decision. A productive rhythm and a successful exception can be as enjoyable as a confrontation.

The factory begins prebuilt. The opening leadership call establishes the obligation without revealing the Brain 2.0 replacement purpose. No construction, placement, repair system, tool research, new supervisor or broad policy tree enters these five days. STILETTO's welder model is available for later staging; it does not establish an early repair mechanic.

## The player's small vocabulary

| Commitment | Available choice | Immediate world feedback | Persistent consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Back a supervisor | Choose LIMEN or STILETTO as adviser; approve their arrangement or use the existing room swap | Supervisors take their stations; the line and clearance procedure adopt their different methods | Who received authority, what they witnessed, and what the factory achieved under that arrangement |
| Operate the line | Use intake and drive controls; respond to a justified hold/wave or ease/push decision | Cradles enter, queues fill or drain, workers change pace, supervisors acknowledge or dispute the order | Output, wear, worker strain and a specific precedent |
| Commit the output | Retain some completed robots; dispatch the rest against the quota | The retained group joins the factory; the dispatched group leaves through its exit | Workforce composition, quota credit and funds, with the same IDs preserved |

There is no additional morality or personality choice. Choosing a cautious adviser need not mean always slowing the line; backing STILETTO need not mean accepting every risk. Success with one favourite is valid. Optional room reassignment preserves expression without requiring the player to staff individual workers.

The current authority rule remains: the adviser acts automatically in their assigned room; decisions in the other room can require the player. These automatic acts must be seen and attributed. Do not ask the player to approve every gesture, or imply they can veto an already completed act. The visible flow controls remain available during operation, so the player can affect the day between incident prompts.

## The loops and the five-day arc

The operating loop is **read flow → adjust or delegate → see work and people respond → revise the next adjustment**. Its feedback must show a useful difference before a score changes.

The leadership loop is **choose whose judgment to back → watch their method affect others → encounter a benefit or friction → keep, qualify or change that support**. Predicting a supervisor and enjoying their response is part of the reward.

The growth loop is **produce → choose who stays and who leaves → experience the changed workforce and obligation tomorrow → allocate again**. Retaining more bodies cannot produce unlimited throughput: stations, clearance and coordination constrain their useful contribution.

The five days form an arc across those repeated loops. They do not unlock five unrelated mini-games. Each day gives the familiar actions a new meaning through a consequence carried from earlier play.

## Ingredients that generate the show

| Ingredient | What appears on screen | What makes it more than decoration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A satisfying working rhythm | Workers approach, brace, transfer cradles and release them; gates, belt and sound have coherent timing | Material and station occupancy are real; the rhythm changes when the player changes flow |
| Two useful but incompatible methods | LIMEN insists on clearance and headroom; STILETTO gets stalled work moving and tolerates shortcuts | Each method has an immediate benefit and an actual cost; neither is merely the wrong answer |
| A few workers worth recognizing | One careful regular, one eager regular and, if retained, a newcomer recur in close encounters | Stable IDs and a small visible identifier connect appearance, witnessed events and later behavior; these are casting roles, not new classes |
| A visible audience | A nearby worker pauses, turns, resumes, follows or keeps their distance | Only present witnesses gain the memory; reactions change the next task or encounter |
| A precedent that travels | Another worker repeats a shortcut, clearance routine or helpful peer habit | A witnessed action and its result support the imitation; an exception does not instantly become a factory-wide rule |
| A small reward | A buffer clears, a difficult handoff succeeds, a worker claps, or STILETTO allows herself a satisfied pause | The celebration follows an earned result and remains brief enough to preserve its value |
| A cost that has not yet become a disaster | Recurring vibration, a hesitant approach, a queue, guarded posture or an increasingly impatient supervisor | Warning grows from the actual state; it can subside after intervention, and a risky choice can genuinely work |
| An unfinished question | A newcomer looks to a peer before the supervisor; two robots disagree about what “approved” means | It grows from this run's history and gives CATHEXIS an existing social arrangement to enter |

Keep the current 24-worker opening as the first scene-size test, but feature only two or three workers in the dramatic foreground. Others still work and occupy space. Use the existing worker model with restrained persistent markings or identifying details, not three newly generated bodies. A casting role must follow eligibility: do not invent a retained newcomer if the player dispatched everyone, or resurrect a lost worker for a scheduled scene.

Discipline and stress need different performances. A tense orderly crew follows the route rigidly; a relaxed disorderly crew drifts, chats or copies the wrong routine. An unconditioned newcomer is not automatically disobedient. The lack of Theatre history permits different social development; it is not a hidden punishment flag.

## Five days with conditional encounters

The order below defines dramatic emphasis and teaching scope. Encounters require valid world conditions. A quiet competent version is required for every day; it must offer a visible payoff rather than a manufactured accident.

| Day | Dramatic purpose | Player commitment and visible scene | What persists |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · The same plan, different promises | Establish their incompatibility | Both propose LIMEN at Security and STILETTO at Conveyor. LIMEN holds operators for roughly one diegetic hour, using some as clerks. STILETTO paces her empty line, walks to the shared entrance, shouts, hears his answer and returns. Workers gradually clear; the line starts and produces its first output. | Actual delayed worker-minutes, inspection staffing, first output, mutual irritation and the producer’s backing |
| 2 · Yesterday is an argument | Hear competing diagnoses | Both recap yesterday from their own perspective, react to the player’s treatment, name today’s priority and propose an assignment. Keep native stations or swap. Retained workers add a real cohort; dispatch improves quota progress. | Which diagnosis the player accepted, cumulative delay/strain, and emerging worker expectations |
| 3 · Collision | Make the accumulated disagreement explicit | Return to the shared doorway. LIMEN wants STILETTO off Conveyor after compensating for delays with excessive tempo; STILETTO wants Security to end the delays and catch the quota. Both may now propose the same swap for incompatible reasons. Use actual delay, pressure, wear and witnessed warnings as evidence. In a well-managed run, the clash concerns the next priority rather than an invented accident. | A public precedent, workers who witnessed it, and a relationship fracture or negotiated limit |
| 4 · Someone learned that | Turn yesterday's result into behavior | An eligible worker follows the demonstrated shortcut, waits for the demonstrated permission, or helps a colleague copy a successful method. The other supervisor encounters the resulting behavior. The player can reaffirm or qualify the precedent through the same operating/authority choices. | A small local habit and a relationship response; no instant universal discipline change |
| 5 · This is becoming my factory | Give a payoff and expose the next question | The player chooses whether to consolidate output into the quota or keep capacity for growth. Revisit a known worker and situation under the accumulated conditions. A fast run can show output and growing coordination trouble; a careful run can show trusted routines and remaining quota pressure; a balanced run can show a useful compromise with disagreement still alive. | The concrete material and social starting state for days 6–9 and CATHEXIS |

Day five does not guarantee injury, rebellion, failure or a supervisor betrayal. Nor does it assign an ending label such as good or bad management. The player should recognize their decisions in how the room now works. The following sub-arc can make Theatre attractive through coordination, shared rituals and social influence even if the opening factory is healthy.

## The constrained rivalry: days one to seven

Until CATHEXIS arrives, the player has two supervisors and two rooms. There is no third posting to separate them. The rooms share a doorway, so swapping their jobs changes how the conflict manifests; it does not remove their ability to obstruct or confront each other. Player responses include maintaining a stable arrangement, deliberate rebalancing, shaking things up, or committing to one side and tolerating the other’s resentment. None is a mandatory “correct” route.

Day one’s arrangement is the same in both proposals. Advice selects whose priorities and judgment are backed; assignment is a distinct, overridable decision. Later, LIMEN can demand Conveyor to curb reckless tempo while STILETTO demands Security to clear workers faster. Again they can propose the same swap while expecting very different outcomes. Agreement on a staffing diagram is not agreement on how the factory should run.

### Every morning is a diagnosis and a bid for authority

Each supervisor’s briefing must contain, in this order:

1. **Their reading of yesterday:** one concrete result they observed or received through a report, including what went well.
2. **Their treatment:** whether their advice was accepted, overridden, vindicated or blamed. Reference the actual decision; do not generate free-floating resentment.
3. **Their next priority:** the problem they consider most dangerous to weekly success.
4. **Their assignment proposal:** who should work each room, what it would change and which cost they discount.

LIMEN’s stable desire is a factory that remains intact and procedurally dependable. STILETTO’s is quota success and leadership approval. A missed production opportunity threatens STILETTO even before an accident threatens anyone else. Shortages and delays make her compensate with tempo; the extra tempo creates strain, wear and exposure that validate LIMEN’s objections. LIMEN’s procedural response can in turn consume more labour and time. This feedback loop is the opening’s dramatic motor.

Briefs derive from bounded, attributed evidence and persistent relationships. Use authored rhetorical templates selected by conditions for the prototype; a future language layer may phrase the same permitted claims but cannot invent facts, assign workers or settle outcomes. Each proposal includes machine-readable assignments, a reason, supporting event IDs and a discounted cost. The visible morning dialogue is the performance of that proposal.

### Predictable operating scripts

These are proposed causal profiles, not balanced numerical promises or delivered multi-day behavior. Compare runs with equal staffing and starting conditions. Randomness may change incident timing/severity inside risk bounds; it must not reverse the baseline cost of a method.

| Player pattern | What reliably happens | What workers begin to learn, shown in speech | Week-one leadership response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Keep native rooms, back LIMEN | Slow admission and clerical diversion constrain effective Conveyor labour; procedural consistency improves; cautious limits reduce added strain but can leave quota short | “Wait for the green.” / “He checked it yesterday too.” Order can coexist with frustration and dependence on permission | Recognize continuity and documented operation; challenge any delivery shortfall. Safety is not a substitute for the contract |
| Keep native rooms, back STILETTO | Admission friction remains until specifically changed; STILETTO compresses production into the remaining hours. Higher useful throughput while staffed, higher wear and strain; output can still disappoint if the bottleneck stays upstream | “Don’t mention the queue.” / “We made up the hour. Didn’t we?” Suppressed reporting can hide trouble from supervisors | Reward delivered quota, then respond to actual losses, downtime and cost. A surplus with an intact plant is legitimately successful |
| Swap: STILETTO Security, LIMEN Conveyor | Faster clearance and fewer clerks make more workers available; LIMEN caps line tempo. The bottleneck moves from admission to processing/buffers; extra bodies are not automatic output | “We’re through. Why are we waiting again?” / “One at a time. That’s the rule here.” Permission and pace now come from different methods | Assess real deliveries and operating condition. Surplus labour/queueing is a legible tradeoff, not a hidden punishment for swapping |
| Alternate or intervene selectively | A well-timed change can protect capacity and recover output. Frequent unexplained reversals leave conflicting precedents; fatigue and wear persist across swaps | “Which rule today?” / “Yesterday she said go.” Workers seek clarification, copy peers or wait for the favoured supervisor | Judge actual results and trend; repeated inconsistency affects explanations and confidence, not an automatic “balanced” bonus |
| Commit to one side and ignore the other | Stable authority can create coherent practice and strong loyalty to one supervisor, alongside the ignored supervisor’s mounting grievance. Costs of the chosen method remain | “Ask her. He doesn’t decide anymore.” / “I was following the rule.” Informal authority and selective reporting become visible | Praise what worked and demand correction of what did not. Leadership’s opinion is not the same thing as worker wellbeing |

Leadership receives a bounded weekly account: dispatched output against quota, available workforce and losses, downtime, operating condition and the producer’s attributed decisions. Its response combines these dimensions; do not collapse them into a good/evil score. A shortfall with recovery capacity differs from a shortfall with damaged equipment. A surplus with silenced workers can earn praise now while planting later vulnerability. Concrete quotas and response thresholds remain tuning work, to be set only after the production loop can be measured.

### Hidden development and visible evidence

Track only states needed to produce different next actions: strain, confidence that reporting helps, expectation of punishment, trust in a particular supervisor, dependence on permission and observed precedents. Keep personal and local knowledge separate; a worker cannot imitate a permission they neither witnessed nor heard about. Do not expose these as a psychological dashboard.

Each shift should produce at least one truthful signal from the affected population: a request, a hesitation, a successful routine, a complaint to a peer, a changed explanation, or silence contrasted with a worker who spoke yesterday. Supervisors can misread this: STILETTO may call quiet compliance “focus”; LIMEN may call repeated checking “discipline.” Their next-morning assessment expresses those beliefs. The player learns to read the difference between a supervisor’s interpretation and the world they saw.

The day-three clash is an escalation of days one and two, not a surprise catastrophe. At minimum, incompatible priorities produce a public demand to change or retain the arrangement. The severity, accusations and worker witnesses depend on the run. If the player has prevented strain or accidents, nobody fabricates them to justify the scene.

## One encounter in detail

**Candidate: the shortcut that worked.** A worker has a real clearance mismatch. They stop at Security and turn between the terminal and their destination. LIMEN's checking or STILETTO's permissive method determines the initial response; room assignment, adviser authority and any player order determine who can decide. A nearby worker watches. If both supervisors meet at the shared threshold, reserve that meeting and account for time away from their room duties.

If admitted, the worker crosses, resumes a waiting task and visibly restores useful flow. If held, the check completes, the worker crosses with verified clearance, and the delay is visible. Neither branch needs damage. A short line can punctuate the result: an attributed claim about what was allowed, not a narrated state change.

On a later day, a witness may attempt the same approach if the situation is similar and their desire makes it useful. A newcomer can follow only if they actually saw or were told about it. An argument becomes possible because the supervisors interpret a real precedent differently. If the record was verified instead, the callback can be a worker deliberately waiting for authorization while STILETTO grows impatient. The same scene family has different performances and consequences.

The exception must identify the worker, clearance requirement, issuer, scope and resolution. Today the code has a generic clearance incident and a text memory; it does not yet represent that chain of evidence or its later imitation.

## How the machinery produces a performance

A condition creates an opportunity; an agent perceives it; a motive selects a feasible intention; a reserved action plays; a recipient responds; a committed result becomes memory for its witnesses. The next encounter can use that memory. This gives repeatable causes with varied performances.

For the first five days, workers need only a small intention set: continue the task, obtain clearance or direction, follow a known example, pause because of strain, and comply with or avoid an intimidating approach. A bounded perception record holds current task, observed permissions, nearby hazards and relevant witnessed precedents. LIMEN weighs order and continuity; STILETTO weighs throughput and latitude to act. Neither automatically knows what happened in the other room.

Agent action choice and presentation scheduling have different jobs. The former validates conditions and changes the world. The latter makes an eligible encounter easy to see, manages who speaks and suppresses redundant flourishes. It cannot invent a fault or select an injury just because the day needs drama. An off-camera event still happens truthfully; a room cue or later in-world recollection can draw attention to its aftermath. Do not replay it as though it is happening again.

Every encounter needs: eligible actors, reason to occur, a physical place, approach positions, action phases, a recipient response, interruption rules, visible outcome and a later-memory hook. Reserve the participants and working space so a robot cannot operate a machine while simultaneously arguing at the gate. Failure to obtain space should defer or shorten the encounter, not teleport its actors.

## Camera and dialogue direction

Keep producer-console navigation into one room with its three CCTV angles. Add an optional close encounter composition that returns to the selected room view. Recalibrate the occupied Lobby and Security interiors against their concept art; preserve the approved factory topology, shared doorway and continuous exterior. The proposed compact interiors and service circulation are specified in the room asset brief. This revision must be implemented as actual floor/navigation geometry, not a camera zoom. Consequential decisions can pause through the existing decision rhythm; visual direction must not steal control through frequent forced cuts.

Start with one foreground exchange at a time and at most two short bubbles visible in the main composition. Speech belongs to its speaker; thought has distinct treatment and reveals only the permitted subjective glimpse. Retain speech as accessible native text with pause/review support. Camera changes must preserve the encounter's time and participants.

Routine work gets a quiet mechanical cadence. A stopped handoff changes that cadence; a gate, a cradle latch and LIMEN's emitter have identifiable local cues. The UI may retain essential quota/output readouts, but numbers do not stand in for worker action. Test the scene with incidental text hidden, then restore dialogue and check what meaning it adds.

The previous whole-day targets remain a test constraint: around one minute for routine days, two to three for heavier days, including choices and reading. Current catalog clips can last 8–20 seconds. Author short usable beats and entry/exit transitions; do not speed an entire gesture to fit a timing spreadsheet or loop a head scratch throughout a shift. Revisit the pacing explicitly if complete performances cannot breathe within it.

## Multi-day contracts that need deliberate changes

The inspected first-turn state has no day progression; its floor adapter hardcodes day 1. Its carrier loop is a short back-and-forth path, not reservation-based work. Clearance periodically removes a counter from the output buffer; it does not yet resolve individual gate encounters. Replace those placeholders before judging whether the world performs coherently. Security admission of workers and Conveyor processing of brain workpieces are distinct flows. Tie staffing and clearance to the actual workers; do not present the current periodic output-counter release as a finished inspection system. The final brain-to-robot activation needs an explicit physical treatment and appropriate machinery before the allocation scene can show a completed robot honestly.

Persistent state should include worker IDs and physical status, retained cohorts, equipment condition, quota ledger, relevant permissions and witnessed memories. Reset the day's shift clock, new output batch, temporary reservations and incident eligibility. Charging can restore the intended battery/fatigue resource; it must not silently repair structural wear or erase grudges. Distinguish those resources before tuning recovery.

The present settlement code multiplies the total committed count by the unit payment. Reusing it on persistent cumulative workers would pay old deliveries again. Settle the actual unallocated daily batch once, preserve cumulative weekly credit separately and reject duplicate day closure. Preserve dispatched identities in history without placing them back into tomorrow's crew.

The API currently accepts at most 256 commands. Five days of the existing 48 advance commands per day plus planning/controls can already exceed that bound. Version the multi-day contract and measure bounded replay cost; increase or partition the transcript deliberately for the playtest. Add checkpoints if measurements require them. Cloud accounts, a database migration and a general streaming engine are not prerequisites for proving these five days, but reload/resume must be tested on the chosen local/recorded-run path.

Numbers from the one-day fixture are not five-day balance. Test careful, sustained fast, adaptive, all-dispatch and growth-heavy runs. Caution must have a useful return and a viable quota route; early speed must sometimes pay off; growth must face real station/clearance capacity. No ordinary path should become inevitably unplayable merely because repair arrives later. Visible risk can lead to success or harm; surprise should arise from history, not an invisible mandatory disaster.

## Scripted scene studies before agent integration

Owner follow-up, 17 October: first build scripted encounters with the accepted models. Each study supplies deterministic storyboard poses and an animated sample. These are performance specifications: later agent systems must produce, interrupt and vary the same action vocabulary from perception, intentions and feedback. The studies carry an explicit rehearsal label. They change a real isolated encounter world through the kernel; they do not modify the live day run or its save.

Start with the day-one shared-doorway confrontation, then a Security clearance encounter and a Conveyor pressure encounter. Specify actor/recipient/witness, approach positions, camera marks, clip phases, prop ownership, dialogue, consequence and an alternate response. A captured screenshot must correspond to a reproducible moment in the playable sequence. Record missing contact clips instead of presenting generic carrying as finished machine work. Room asset generation follows the needs of these two scenes and their original concept art.

## Build order and review gates

1. **One complete work cycle in the real room.** One approved worker, a physical prop, a station and truthful start/stop behavior. Show actual feed/drive changes affecting it. Review contacts, readable purpose, rhythm and performance before filling the factory.
2. **One shared encounter.** Add a supervisor, worker response and one witness; prove the clearance scene with both resolutions, proper reservations and in-world dialogue. Review it from CCTV and close view without logs.
3. **A consequence tomorrow.** Preserve the same actors and witnessed decision into a second day; stage the changed behavior. Test settlement, charging/recovery and save/resume. This proves continuity before authoring all five days.
4. **Five-day performance palette.** Complete the conditional day emphases, quiet-success alternatives, concise briefings and assignment/allocation feedback. Benchmark the real 24-worker composition and tune camera, animation and effect budgets together.
5. **Owner playtest and alternate runs.** Run five days without debug explanation, then compare another approach. Expand to Theatre and week-two settlement only after there is a working, enjoyable short arc.

A useful playtest lets the owner identify a worker, anticipate something about each supervisor, point to a visible result of their own decision, recognize a callback and want to try another approach. Observe confusion, dull stretches, excessive interruption and failures to notice important action. Ask what they wanted to make happen rather than demand a correct interpretation of hidden psychology. Technical validation protects real causes and consequences; it does not establish that the show is fun.

## Decisions to resolve through the first scenes

The first scene should settle how long a complete physical work cycle needs, which prop/operation best represents it, and whether the existing CCTV framing makes its result readable. The two-day slice should settle how much routine supervisor authority the player enjoys delegating, which worker identifiers are memorable and which memory callback is worth keeping. Five-day runs should settle quota/retention balance, cumulative wear and whether the daily pace leaves enough room for character. Theatre admission and the week-two climax come after these foundations have evidence.
