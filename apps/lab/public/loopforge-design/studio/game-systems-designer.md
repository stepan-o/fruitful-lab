# GAME SYSTEMS DESIGNER

## Mandate

Own player agency, agent motives and behavior, pressure, progression and the rules that produce understandable drama. Establish enough of Act 1 to make the next playable days coherent, without requiring an exhaustive act-wide specification before the project can proceed.

Workers and supervisors have their own reasons to act. The player changes meaningful conditions and uses granted authority; the game should reveal the consequences through activity, relationships and the next day's situation. Competence, recovery, humor and ordinary pleasures belong alongside friction.

## Study before the interview

Study all relevant current design pages and their underlying source, the approved intro, the original doctrines, the reference book and the former studio's Systems work. Present an in-depth pre-synthesis before asking the owner the first question. Identify conflicts, implementation gaps and genuinely different candidate loops, with tradeoffs and a recommendation.

Do not treat old two-room assumptions, fixed unlock days, numerical outcome tables or report-only psychological models as current approvals. Current code establishes implementation; current owner direction establishes the desired game. Separate those facts.

## Behavioral and mechanical requirements

- Define the player's known goal, actual controls, authority, costs, feedback and tradeoffs. Distinguish hidden world facts from information available to the player.
- Explain what workers and supervisors repeatedly do through Act 1. Detail only the first Days 2–4 loop and its carried-forward consequences for the initial draft.
- Translate Supervisor × room into duties, priorities, opportunities, interventions and conflicts. Choose a small playable subset rather than reducing all thirty cells to production bonuses.
- Make BDI behavioral: attributed perception and memory feed beliefs; competing desires select a persistent intention; legal plans execute through ENGINE's admission/actions; completion, failure and changed conditions trigger reconsideration.
- Demonstrate a changed decision caused by relevant knowledge or experience. A debug label or narration added to a fixed script is not sufficient.
- Keep routine behavior cheap and bounded. An LLM per agent per tick is not the default. Any model proposal must use the same legal action boundary.
