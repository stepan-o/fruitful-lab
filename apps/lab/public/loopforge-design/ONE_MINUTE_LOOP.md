# Loopforge — one-minute loop inventory

8 October 2026. Working brief for the next design pass. The owner considers the one-second loop a good baseline and has asked to gather the next loop's flow, requirements and interfaces. The owner then clarified the pacing: one game day should take about one minute, two to three at most for a heavier day. This document consolidates that agreed requirement; proposed interface changes below are not yet implemented or accepted.

## What this loop does

**Review → choose today's adviser → approve their arrangement → run the shift and handle events → commit output and read the result → next day.**

The one-minute loop is the **whole game day**, including its ordinary reading, choices and transitions. A routine day targets about 60 seconds; a heavier day targets two to three minutes at most. Giving production a minute and adding several minutes of briefing is outside this requirement. These are experience pacing targets, not an instruction to force a timed answer or penalize a player for reading slowly.

A seven-day delivery cycle therefore has a nominal seven-minute routine-day baseline; if every day took three minutes it would be 21 minutes. That arithmetic explains why long mandatory briefings would undermine the weekly loop. Leadership calls, major developments and onboarding must fit into the longer-day allowance. Optional inspection and a player intentionally leaving the game paused do not establish the intended normal-path duration.

The smaller operational beat is **notice → adviser action or player response → visible consequence**. It sits inside the day; it does not get its own minute or demand a compulsory incident. Quiet successful production is valid.

## Whole-day flow and interface ownership

| Step | Player's job | Primary interface | What must be quick |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Read the situation | Understand the useful result from yesterday, the remaining weekly obligation and today's concern | Console summary / previous debrief; leadership call at week start | Show the change that matters. Do not repeat the full report at both ends of the day. Day one uses opening facts instead. |
| Choose the adviser | Compare current short pitches and choose whose priority to follow | Dedicated adviser roster | This is the primary daily decision. Recognizable tokens, meaningful pitches and inspectable tradeoffs; do not play five full briefings. |
| Hear and authorize | Read structured advice, inspect the proposed arrangement, accept or override | Adviser brief with a concise plan; detailed placement desk when needed | Proposed fast path: approve from the briefing after showing assignments and delegation. Keep deliberate edits available without a compulsory extra trip. No priority picker. |
| Operate | Watch the arrangement work; trust automatic decisions or respond where required | Producer console; room focus optional; incident dialog when consequential | Allow enough operation to feel the choice. Routine receipts stay out of modal flow; a safe day does not need an invented interruption. |
| Commit and carry | Permanently split output between factory and quota; understand the day's benefit, cost and outstanding issue | Dispatch, with concise result/debrief | Clear before/after totals and explicit irreversible commitment. Feed the result into tomorrow's context, rather than another mandatory exposition screen. |

Proposed first pacing budget, to test rather than treat as balance: situation 5s, adviser choice 10s, brief/plan 10s, operation 25s, allocation/result 10s. Total about 60s. A consequential decision, first-time explanation or weekly call can expand the day into the two-to-three-minute range. If normal play cannot meet that range, simplify the required route and reading before hiding the problem with a faster conveyor.

## Part 01 sets the daily interface requirements

Part 01 lasts only as long as its daily choices produce distinct learning and consequences. Design the number of useful cycles first; that sets what the daily interfaces must deliver before Cathexis and the Burn-in Theatre expand the puzzle.

**Proposed first test:** Starting hypothesis: three completed two-room days, with Cathexis and the Theatre available for the fourth day on a competent path. A two-to-three-minute opening plus two routine one-minute days puts the handoff around four to five minutes. This is a playtest target, not a fixed unlock date or an accepted final stage length.

- **First day · learn the commitment.** Choose LIMEN or STILETTO, understand the priority and delegated room, see work happen, and permanently split the output. The player should leave with one benefit and one known cost, not a lesson in every hidden variable.
- **Second day · yesterday becomes relevant.** Retained workforce, delivered quota and wear now affect the starting situation. Supervisor pitches and the selected brief acknowledge relevant prior choices. Staying with the same adviser is valid; a new adviser is not a compulsory tutorial action.
- **Third day · make an informed commitment.** The player backs, changes or selectively overrides the approach for a reason grounded in the previous results. Growth versus delivery and output versus exposure become a deliberate plan. The result should make the Theatre’s proposed coordination programme useful without making it a universal fix.
- **Handoff · a third way of running the workforce.** When readiness and commissioning requirements are met, Cathexis joins the next planning cycle and the Theatre becomes usable. Her pitch offers a new operating approach with a visible near-term benefit and sacrifice. Loyalty, confidence and worker influence develop underneath; no hidden conditioning history is exposed as a meter.

Replace the candidate “complete the weekly quota first” gate for this test. Full delivery may happen before day seven, but tying the unlock to it can delay players who retain output. Proposed readiness uses actual completed operation, a still-viable workforce/line, demonstrated irreversible output commitments and the capacity/investment required to run the Theatre. Exact thresholds and payment/funding rules remain open. Tune viable safety, output and adaptive paths to reach readiness around the third result; do not require choosing both advisers, overriding someone or suffering an accident.

The first weekly quota remains due on day seven. Under this proposal, Cathexis enters while the same obligation is still active. The player learns what her programme changes under an existing pressure; a completed week is not a prerequisite for meeting her.

Compare two, three and four completed two-room days in short owner playtests. If a second day cannot deliver new context, adding more days will not fix it. If three days leave players unable to explain authority or allocation, improve those interfaces before automatically extending the stage. Readiness failures need understandable recovery; do not auto-unlock merely to hit a stopwatch.

The funding path must permit this arrival: a nominal day-four handoff cannot depend on income that only becomes available at the end of the week. Payment timing and the cost of the Theatre remain open.

Three days is a design hypothesis, not an implemented schedule. The current one-day kernel cannot validate this arc. Do not gate “mastery” on an invisible judgment of player understanding; use explicit world requirements, while comprehension is evaluated in the playtest.

## Conditions established before operation

- The opening leadership call establishes the weekly quota and known factory facts. Acknowledge it before gameplay opens; later calls require actual completed-week records.
- The player chooses an adviser from current short pitches, inspecting their priority and tradeoff before appointment. Two candidates on day one; the roster accommodates at least five later.
- The chosen adviser gives a structured assessment, attributed context, priority and proposed arrangement. The player cannot give them a different optimization target.
- The player accepts or overrides placements, sees the adviser's delegated room, and starts the shift. No first-day assignments exist before this sequence.
- Output, equipment condition, available workforce, quota progress and the adviser/operator identities supply the visible operating context. No station-staffing puzzle or repair control is introduced in Parts 01–02.

## Operational events inside the day

| Beat | Player needs to understand or do | Interface | Required result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Read the operation | Is the arrangement working? Which room changed? | Producer console; selected camera on small screens | Room-specific activity and concise facts. Quiet operation is valid; routine output is not a popup. |
| Notice an issue | Where is it, what is known, and does it need my decision? | Camera cue + attributed notice; local amber impulse | Separate **decision waiting** from **supervisor acted**. Cue remains recoverable after the sound/light ends. Red requires a confirmed accident, not merely an alarming claim. |
| Inspect if useful | What happened here, who is operating, what supports the account? | Optional room focus; linked evidence/record | More room context without restarting, resolving or rerolling the event. Avoid requiring this detour to understand an ordinary decision. |
| Resolve under authority | What does my adviser want, what does it cost, and can I disagree? | Automatic-action receipt in their assigned room; incident response dialog elsewhere or for a factory-wide decision | Own-room events resolve automatically. Other response decisions pause and present the recommendation plus meaningful legal alternatives. |
| Read the consequence | Was the order accepted, what actually happened, and who reacted? | Return to the originating camera + concise outcome receipt; supervisor token/speech | Show execution and known immediate costs. Preserve pending consequences. An order, an attempted action and a successful outcome are different states. |
| Judge and carry forward | Did the response help? What remains unresolved? | Live operation; optional causal record; later daily debrief | Allow enough observable work for a consequence to become legible. Link the outcome to its decision without claiming a counterfactual was observed. |

**Proposed presentation emphasis:** the operating portion stays on the console. A player-controlled event adds one focused decision surface and returns to its room. Automatic actions should be visible on the console without forcing acknowledgement. Room focus and records deepen inspection; they should not be compulsory steps for every event.

## The decision surface's minimum content

1. **Situation:** room, current operator, source and concise observed facts. Attribute reports to their speaker; keep forecasts uncertain.
2. **Adviser's judgment:** their short, characterful recommendation and stated reason. The adviser and local operator can be different people.
3. **Commitment:** a concrete action, known sacrifice and meaningful uncertainty for each option. Name whose recommendation is overridden. No generic “agree/disagree” whose effect needs interpretation.
4. **Aftermath:** accepted/rejected order, actual execution, known physical result, an attributed reaction and anything still unresolved. More details remain inspectable.

Use the room scene, correct supervisor token and speech treatment to support those jobs. Match artwork to the known room, participants and state; use an explicit placeholder where matching art is missing. Do not substitute an impressive but contradictory scene. General guidance belongs in contextual help; the event supplies this choice's specifics.

## Authority, information and time

- The adviser proposes responses throughout the day and automatically resolves events in their effectively assigned room. Elsewhere, the player accepts or overrides. A draft placement or camera selection does not change authority. Mid-shift handover rules remain open.
- Show automatic acts as already executed, with actor and consequence. No disguised confirmation, undo or retroactive veto.
- Only supervisors who witness or learn of an outcome can react. Confidence, loyalty, respect and stress remain distinct private state; reactions and behaviour communicate permitted evidence without revealing hidden meters.
- Pausing to read is an interface affordance. Ordering production to stop or ease is an in-world action with opportunity costs. **UI pause must not itself count as lost production, safety relief or repair.**
- A consequential decision stays pending when dismissed for inspection. In the current viewer, room focus can continue the shift; records, intercom, planning and dialogs pause advance requests. Preserve a clear running/paused/pending status.
- Keep one foreground operational question legible. Group related notices; retain automatic receipts and unresolved issues so missing an animation is not a reflex penalty. Rules for simultaneous pending decisions still need design.

## Concrete first-day material already available

**Conveyor: a shudder in the line.** The load sensor rises while the belt still moves. LIMEN recommends easing the line; STILETTO recommends finishing the batch. Easing sacrifices production opportunity and reduces exposure without restoring existing damage. Pushing preserves opportunity, adds wear and can cause a worker loss. When the adviser runs the Conveyor, their response is automatic. Otherwise the player authorizes or overrides it.

**Security: a clearance mismatch.** Two workers' access records do not match their station. LIMEN recommends verification; STILETTO recommends waving them through. Verification holds production; waving them through leaves an exception. This is a working decision fixture, but the larger downstream consequence of that exception is not yet implemented. Do not present the two options as a fully balanced dilemma on that basis.

These give us matched tests of the same problem under different judgment and authority. Their timings and numerical costs are prototype fixtures, not final pacing or balance. No mandatory accident is needed to teach the loop.

## Interface inventory across the daily loop

| Interface | Job in the larger cycle | Frequency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leadership call | Establish quota, relevant directives and eventually last week's performance | Opening and weekly |
| Daily results / debrief | Direct facts and a short causal lesson; unresolved issue for the next choice | Between shifts; only end-of-day exists in the current slice |
| Adviser roster | Compare pitches, priorities and costs; make the primary daily choice | Once per day |
| Adviser brief / intercom | Structured interpretation and proposed priority; later bounded conversation | Daily, with event advice carried into incidents |
| Placement desk | Accept or revise assignments; make delegation visible | Planning; later handovers unresolved |
| Producer console / room focus | Operate, notice, inspect, receive consequences | Recurring |
| Incident response / outcome receipt | Resolve a meaningful decision and understand its aftermath | When warranted |
| Records | Recover missed facts and trace a consequence to its source/order | Optional |
| Dispatch | Permanently retain output or commit it to the weekly quota | End of shift |
| Development | Invest and unlock capabilities through demonstrated mastery | Progression milestones; not a compulsory minute-loop visit |

These are jobs, not a requirement to visit ten screens in a minute. The current focused screens already cover most of them. The proposed outcome receipt is an overlay/record presentation, not another compulsory workspace.

## Engine and presentation requirements

The engine owns the incident, perceived evidence, adviser policy, legal responses, authority at the decision boundary, accepted order, execution, physical changes, memories and information recipients. The player projection exposes only permitted facts and attributed accounts. The UI owns focus, camera choice, dialog visibility and illustration/effect playback.

For richer episodes, carry stable links from **problem → recommendation → order → execution → consequence → later recollection**. Keep a player's action distinct from the local supervisor's interpretation or refusal. Transport failure is not character refusal. Reopening must not regenerate a different recommendation; receipt replay must not execute the order or sound twice.

Current events have stable IDs and a prose cause; incidents carry a recommendation and choices. They do not yet provide the full linked episode/outcome contract above. Any schema extension should be explicit, versioned and tested against deterministic replay. Do not reconstruct private motives in the renderer.

No model call is necessary to make this loop complete. Structured authored advice remains the baseline. Later generated interpretation/prose must improve play in matched evaluations, use permitted evidence and recorded outputs, fall back cleanly, and stay outside production ticks. The UI must distinguish the adviser knowing, believing and merely claiming something without demanding a belief-network lesson from the player.

## Decisions to settle before new interface work

1. What is the smallest satisfying consequence beat: which physical change, supervisor reaction and unresolved issue deserve foreground space?
2. How should a completed automatic action remain discoverable without adding another alarm or confirmation?
3. When several problems compete, which can be grouped, queued or left as quiet notices? Which truly need a pause?
4. Which first-day event pair makes both trusting and overriding defensible, with a consequence the test can actually deliver?
5. How do we fit the whole daily cycle into about one minute, or two to three for heavier days, while leaving enough running time to feel the result? The older three-shifts-per-thirty-minutes proposal is discarded. Check this cadence against the roughly 10–20 minute full-floor unlock target; mastery still determines progression, not elapsed time.

Acceptance questions: Can the player say what happened, whose judgment they followed or challenged, what it cost and what remains open? Does the result make them want to continue? Can they answer without reading the full log or seeing hidden traits? Compare a safe run, an aggressive run and a deliberate override; successful quiet operation must remain satisfying.
