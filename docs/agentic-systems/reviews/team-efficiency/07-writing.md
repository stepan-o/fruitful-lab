## 1. The extended coverage and proxy sections still make game testing the principal subject

**Location:** `07.json:89–140` and `07.json:180–205`.

The chapter opens on the right question—whether a complete AI production system fulfills its promise—but its largest developed sequences concern visiting rooms, preserving a player's reservation, game navigation, helicopter simulation, scripted birds and human playtesting. This is an argument-weight problem, not a request to remove Loopforge or every game source. Retain a strong physical example, then develop one equally concrete production-team situation already named here: an asset changes after review, a delegated check runs against a different specimen, or a receiving agent cannot reproduce its predecessor's verdict. In the final proxy section, return to an actual creative-production decision and distinguish checking the agent judge's taste from checking factual delivery. That would also separate this chapter more clearly from Chapter 6's already-developed lesson about optimizing an incomplete objective.

## 2. The audit makes actual costs concrete but presents its demonstrated capacity only as a possibility

**Location:** `07.json:71–85`.

The 16-batch framing is careful and correct: three scoped passes, thirteen failed/incomplete terminal batches, some deliberate diagnosis, and no studio-wide failure rate. However, the passage spends its concrete detail on defects and broken test machinery, then changes to the conditional “A more faithful validator can be worth building.” The published audit says useful capability **was developed**: validation against the actually loaded model, source/world transforms, rig-specific contact checks and preserved regression evidence, all bounded to particular rigs, clips and contracts. The prose currently gives that finding less reality than the costs.

Use one such capability to explain what the team could subsequently inspect or hand over that its earlier check could not establish. Keep maintenance and interpretability costs alongside it; do not invent time saved, a baseline or universal reuse. This is an opportunity for the chapter's most distinctive argument: building the means to judge work is itself a production outcome, provided a later contributor can use it. The public audit exposes this positive finding directly; it does not expose the detailed error inventory at line 78, so either supply a public supporting record for that inventory or use the bounded observations the linked audit actually contains.
