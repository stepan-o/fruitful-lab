One gate, two work breaks, one walk home. A worker gets a chance to talk because another worker is beside them. What they choose to say depends on what they want and what they can personally account for. The next person may go home with a different idea of what happened.

Four chances in a shift

WhereWhen the opportunity opensWho can use it
Security crowd · oneThe second distinct worker clears Security for Conveyor. Count the second completed gate passage.Immediate neighbors still standing in the waiting crowd. Someone in an active check, in custody, gone through the gate or busy checking IDs cannot be a partner.
Conveyor · twoThe arriving crew occupy adjacent working positions. The first mutual pause can open a chat. A later work opportunity can open the second.The two workers actually stationed next to one another, within hearing, with time to address one another. They may talk while their hands work. Walk-out · oneThe bell has released the workers and two of them are actually walking out together.Neighbors on the same stretch of the exit route. A detained worker first needs release. Someone closing the register first needs to finish and catch up.

The pre-shift appointment, the already-talking walk-in, LIMEN’s greeting, his individual ID checks, essential work calls and the Producer’s intervention have their own causes. None spends a room or walk-out slot. A bell can end an unfinished Conveyor conversation. An answer outside needs a fresh opening beside a real partner and spends the walk-out slot. No unused slot rolls into another room or another day.

How two people get a conversation

  1. Shuffle the workers’ Security order once for the run’s shift. Resolve each check, clearance, delay, skipped check, detention and actual passage. Pass the resulting order to Conveyor placement. Use the accepted room assignment, available compatible posts and the sequence of arrivals. Record where each person actually stands, including extra hands at the tray bench or rack. Printed names in a table read describe one resulting layout.
  2. In Security, take immediate neighbors in the remaining queue. At Conveyor, take neighboring occupied work positions, including two assigned sides of a shared bench. On the walk out, take workers alongside or immediately ahead on the same stretch. Check distance, obstructions and whether either person is leaving. A worker passing through on an errand can witness an event there, but only someone positioned as a neighboring worker can claim a Conveyor conversation.
  3. For each possible pair, consider each person as initiator. A social desire is enough to ask for company. A specific question needs a reason to ask that listener. A willing listener can correct the speaker, disagree, gesture, decline or go on working. Random selection chooses among credible candidates. Keep source-bound roles intact. Use unplayed eligible exchanges first when the same two people repeatedly meet.
  4. Choose the pair, direction and topic for the whole exchange. Spend one room slot when the opener reaches the other worker. If movement prevents even the opener reaching them, release the reservation and try the next legal pair while the window is open. Once an opener lands, a cutoff still costs that slot. A resumed thought at the next meeting uses that meeting’s slot.
  5. Deliver each audible part in order. Apply what it means to the actual listener. A gesture needs a sightline. An independently overheard phrase may give a bystander a report attributed to its speaker. No room-wide belief update follows from drawing the conversation.

Security can use ordinary queue talk or a card exchange with an eligible neighbor. Once the second passage has happened, try the earliest viable pair. If the group clears in one rush and nobody is left to speak, record the missed opportunity. At Conveyor, do not spend both opportunities on the same pause. A new arrival, new handoff, stoppage or resumed cycle can provide the next opening. The same two people may speak again if they are still adjacent and have something new to say. On departure, do not hold the entire crew at the door for one conversation.

bob looks for someone every day

At shift start bob wants someone to talk to. While his original is missing, finding it adds urgency and helps him choose a listener. He tries to start a conversation when he has a free voice and a reachable neighbor. While he has yet to get a substantive reply, prefer his viable bid over equivalent chatter. An earlier walk-in conversation or being answered as a listener can satisfy his social aim. His unresolved search can still prompt another question. If someone returns his card he can still talk about the belt, the walk or the neighbor’s sore hand.

Keep attempts separate from replies. An unheard opener, silence or a work interruption before an answer keeps the goal alive. If bob still needs a reply at departure, give his viable walk-out pairing first consideration. If he is alone, in custody until others have gone or separated by machinery, he can miss. Record where he tried and what prevented it. Never move a worker, manufacture a witness or grant a fifth slot to make his daily total come out right.

What can be said about the card

Any worker can talk to bob, ed or gus about the card when they are real eligible neighbors. Workmates can ask bob about a pass they saw, ask ed because he kept the book, or ask gus because he took the other desk. Any of those three may also open the conversation. Someone seeing a clerk accept a desk gains a reason to consult him. It gives them no knowledge of a pickup. Two other workers can talk shop or exchange unrelated news. Previously heard card news stays with them until they have an eligible listener for that topic.

bob knows he lost the original. sam’s account can narrow his search. A pass lets him work today. ed knows he picked up bob’s original and still has his own. Whether he acknowledges the find, identifies the owner, shows the face or hands the card back depends on his listener and what interrupts him. gus starts with a desk preference. In one run he sees two card edges in ed’s pocket. In another he only knows which clerk kept the book. His answers differ.

For each delivered claim keep its subject, what was claimed, speaker, actual listener, immediate source, earlier source if relayed, how it was acquired and when. A cautious worker can remember a report while withholding belief in its contents. Keep contrary reports side by side until evidence settles them. A witness seeing bob receive his original and a neighbor hearing bob say it came back can know different things after the very same handover. Old beliefs held elsewhere can outlast the loss. A new day resets the slots and bob’s daily social aim. It preserves personal memories, promises, card custody and what another person still needs to be told.

One answer heard two ways

bob Anybody turn one in?

sam Somebody found one.

sam ed’s desk.

On the rushed line STILETTO can call “sam. Catch that lip.” If the last two words land, bob can seek ed on the way out. If they do not, he only knows a loose card turned up. With the same people walking beside him, he can choose ben, who stood behind him when he fell. His first question to ben is about what went past their feet.
