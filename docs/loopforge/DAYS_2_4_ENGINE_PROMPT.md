## Shared behavior for this commission

- On Day 2 the player accepts one supervisor’s entire bid. LIMEN keeps Security and STILETTO runs Conveyor. STILETTO proposes the swap, runs Security quickly and leaves LIMEN to require an all-clear at each Conveyor handoff. The chosen lead affects how workers move and speak and how quickly completed cortexes reach the rack. STILETTO’s rushed policy raises workload and injury exposure. LIMEN’s routine is safer and still raises stress.
- The player gets one intervention in one room per shift. Watching, reading a report or passing up a decision costs nothing. Once the intervention is spent the shift continues. Workers and supervisors resolve subsequent events from their own perceptions and desires.
- Workers start with “I have my ID.” After Day 1, bob starts Day 2 with “I don’t have my ID” and the desire “find my ID.” ed believes both “I have my ID” and “I have bob’s ID.” ed has physical custody of bob’s original. bob can ask coworkers about it when a chat opportunity arises. What a recipient hears can influence a later reply or action. STILETTO interrupts worker chats during rushed Conveyor operation.
- LIMEN can discover the missing credential if he checks bob before clearance is rushed. At the first such incident, a player with the intervention available can issue a **daily pass**. bob may proceed that day and still wants his original. The pass expires at shift end. The next actual ID check can expose the same problem. If LIMEN discovers the discrepancy and no pass is issued, LIMEN jails bob for the rest of that day. His station loses his labor. If the discrepancy goes undetected, this incident never opens.
- On the repeated-pressure route, STILETTO’s Day 2 demand can make LIMEN yield again. He concludes that she is against protocol and she concludes that he is weak. Next time he refuses, then yields under pressure, losing CONFIDENCE and AUTHORITY. A later refusal can hold the queue. STILETTO calls for the crew and LIMEN orders them to stay. Workers tip the conflict according to what they heard, saw and remember. A saved intervention publicly endorses either supervisor. Even two workers who hear the same endorsement can choose differently. Durable beliefs consolidate overnight.
