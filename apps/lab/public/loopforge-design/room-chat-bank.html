Room and walk-out chat bank

WRITER · Days 2–4 · Owner-approved SYSTEMS corrections · 20 November 2026

Each entry is one conversation between two people. Read only the named lines. Play an emoji as a silent reaction over its speaker. The opening note gives each person a reason to speak now. The note after the last line says what the listener can take away. A work call, an obstruction or the bell can cut the exchange at any word.

The first two banks belong to Security and Conveyor. The walk-out bank begins once the two workers have left their duties. The card bank can supply the topic in any of those four daily slots. At least one actual participant in a card conversation is bob, ed or gus. Names show a possible performance. Recast a neighbor only when that person has the evidence, desire, voice and physical position for that side. Keep personal history with its actual witness. For instance, anybody who heard ed can repeat what he said. Nobody can inherit sam’s memory of kneeling beside bob by drawing sam’s line.

Security crowd

Q01 · Which end of the queue

ben sees the queue turn a corner and wants their place back. lou remembers who arrived together.

ben Was I in front of you?

lou You were till you stopped to ask.

ben I’ll take that as a yes.

If heard. ben now expects lou to vouch for their place. Neither has clearance.

Q02 · The ink

kit sees ink on the side of mae’s hand and wants to know whether the desk is moving. mae recognizes a mark from home.

kit They stamped you already?

mae Yesterday. I’ve had a bath since.

kit 😬 Good ink.

If heard. kit learns that mae’s mark came from yesterday. It tells him nothing about today’s checks.

Q03 · Short leg

bob feels his knee stiffen when he stands still. He wants sam to move a little. sam has room on one side.

bob Can we stand crooked a minute?

sam How crooked?

bob Just enough I can get my knee straight.

If heard. sam learns which side bob needs today. Moving is a separate visible act.

Q04 · The name call

A short worker can hear gus but cannot see the desk. Their neighbor can see why the worker ahead moved.

ben Was that me?

jo That was a man getting his card back.

ben He sounded delighted.

jo He was standing on my boot.

If heard. The first speaker learns the movement was a returned card. A shout from the desk would supersede the explanation.

Q05 · One peculiarity

jo sees max watching LIMEN’s jaw and knows max likes doing his voice. max has noticed the pause before a name.

max He takes a little breath before your name.

jo Do mine.

max I can do the breath. I’d need six years to say your name like that.

If heard. jo learns what max listens for. Both need to have heard a real call.

Q06 · Where to stand

bob remembers being struck and wants a safe cue. kit wants to avoid volunteering anyone to move.

bob You tell me when he means me.

kit I’ll wait for my own name first.

If heard. bob learns kit waits for their own name. He still has to interpret LIMEN’s next order himself.

Q07 · Keeping count

A worker waiting at the bend asks for a firsthand head count. The neighbor actually saw two go through.

mae How many got in?

lou Two.

mae Sure?

lou I only count the ones I envy.

If heard. The questioner learns the neighbor counted two passages. It gives no reason to believe either has reached a station.

Q08 · Same rails

sam can feel jo holding the rail tight. jo remembers losing a place in a previous surge and wants to keep this one.

sam Going to take that rail in with you?

jo Last time I let go I went back three places.

sam Hang on to mine as well then.

If heard. sam learns jo is keeping a place, not about to step out.

Q09 · A pen in the air

ed has been seen poised with his pen while LIMEN reads. max wants to know what is being written. The neighbor has a clear side view.

max What’s he writing about us?

ben Nothing. Look at the shadow under his pen.

max Ah. That’s us being considered.

If heard. max learns the nib has not touched the page. No new fact about anyone’s card.

Q10 · The desk jobs

Both workers saw the helpers take their desks. mae handled trays yesterday. kit would like to know why she resents them.

kit You’d have taken a desk.

mae Course I would. I wouldn’t have made you listen to a speech about it.

If heard. kit learns mae objects to carrying their share. Hearing the criticism does not put either clerk in earshot.

Q11 · Quiet crowd

The worker ahead is straining to hear their name while the other wants company. The listener signals for silence.

lou Have they ever called the wrong—

ben 🤫

lou Right.

If heard. The initiator learns the neighbor is listening for the call. It can suppress another attempt with that listener.

Q12 · The squeak

gus has left his desk and is in the queue on the swap route. A neighbor hears the familiar squeak of his voice carry from the gate area. gus can identify his own boot.

max I swear that gate still sounds like you.

gus That’s my left foot. Listen.

max Oh. It’s coming to work with us.

If heard. The neighbor now attributes the squeak to gus’s boot. No distant worker learns its source.

Q13 · A place in line

Two neighbors stand shoulder to shoulder. One wants to start talking and offers a place. The other would rather wait for the call.

bob Want to go ahead of me?

ben And have him ask me whose turn it is? You go when he calls you.

If heard. The first speaker learns this neighbor is willing to wait. Only a later step changes the order.

Conveyor neighbors

V01 · One click

jo heard a clean clamp bite and wants another quick handoff. kit can see the jaw has not fully seated on the next tray.

jo Send me another one like that.

kit Let the clamp finish biting first.

If heard. jo learns kit waits for the bite to finish. If jo then waits, both can hear whether one click follows.

V02 · Where to put your thumb

A tray-bench hand spots the neighbor gripping a cold edge. The neighbor has a reason to change grip before the next tray.

mae Your thumb’s about to meet an old friend of mine.

sam Which one?

mae Little burr on the underside. There.

sam Ow. Yes. Knows me too.

If heard. The listener learns where the burr is. They must actually feel or see it before claiming firsthand knowledge.

V03 · Wet square

At neighboring Belt and Forge posts, max waited for jo and wants to know why. jo actually saw LIMEN turn their card into their hand.

max There you are. Was it the photograph again?

jo He said I had to wait.

max And you went?

jo He turned it over for me.

If heard. Of these two speakers, jo saw LIMEN return the card. max learned about it from jo. In 3D ben and kit also saw the handoff. bob heard the orders from the tail with his view of the hands blocked.

V04 · Two sounds

A neighbor has heard the Forge double knock. The press operator knows one sound comes from their foot and wants credit for the other.

kit Which knock do I listen for?

max Second one. First one’s my heel.

kit You could oil that.

max It’s my good heel.

If heard. The listener learns which sound the operator claims is their heel. A later cycle can confirm it.

V05 · Drip

The wash operator has stopped spraying. The adjacent belt hand holds back, believing the spray is still running.

jo You finished?

bob Water’s off. That’s the brain dripping.

jo Takes its time drying its hair.

If heard. jo learns the sound comes from the draining tray when bob points it out. The whole exchange spends one Conveyor slot. On STILETTO’s line jo can take the safely washed, ready piece at a staffed Belt. On LIMEN’s line jo must also hear his all-clear. An urgent safety warning remains available after both chat slots are spent.

V06 · An empty post

Two workers have just become neighbors by the tray supports. One is waiting for Wash. The newcomer actually saw bob beside kit in Security.

mae Have you brought me Wash?

ben I brought me. bob was still beside kit when I left.

mae 😑 Then this one can sit.

If heard. mae gains ben’s dated observation about bob. An empty post alone cannot supply the cause of the dispute.

V07 · Wrong end

ed has been put on trays near sam. He reaches for the end sam has already lifted. sam wants to finish without taking the full weight.

sam That end’s mine.

ed They look remarkably alike.

sam Mine’s the one in the air.

If heard. ed learns how sam shares a load and gets a chance to show he can lift something besides the register.

V08 · Rack count

gus is alongside lou at Output and has counted a piece still under the press. lou wants the tally to match what reached the rack.

gus That’s four.

lou Put your hand where four’s going to land.

gus Three.

If heard. gus learns lou counts arrivals. If the piece sticks, it still has not landed.

V09 · A breath before the call

The next operator hears the all-clear routine and is tempted to anticipate it. A neighbor has seen LIMEN wait for a carrier to settle.

ben He always says it here.

kit Watch the wheels. That one’s still turning.

ben That’s a long word for him today.

If heard. The first speaker learns what movement their neighbor watches. A nod between workers never authorizes a handoff.

V10 · The crooked grin

bob enjoys the work today and sees the next operator smile. He seeks company even if his card has already come back.

bob You like this bit too?

jo I like handing it to somebody else.

bob Oh. Different bit.

If heard. bob learns what makes the other worker smile. It creates an ordinary reason to find them tomorrow.

V11 · Counting the empty trays

A loader sees empty trays arrive nested and thinks one has gone missing. The neighboring hand can separate two stuck rims.

mae I sent you three.

ed You did. Two of them have become very fond of each other.

mae Give us that corner.

If heard. The loader learns where the supposed missing tray went. Belief changes only when the rims separate or the explanation lands.

V12 · One hand free

At neighboring drive and Belt positions, kit has seen jo rubbing a wrist after yesterday’s all-clears. While the control is safely stationary, kit has a free hand to demonstrate. This elective advice uses a Conveyor slot.

kit Put your elbow here while you wait.

jo How do I work the switch?

kit When he says so, you pick your elbow up again.

If heard. The listener learns a way to rest the wrist. Relief is only known after they try it.

V13 · A first turn

sam actually saw bob run the controls alone. Now next to bob, sam wants a turn at the part bob enjoys.

sam Which bit would you do twice?

bob The belt. You feel it catch through your hand.

sam Let me feel that next time it’s stopped.

If heard. bob learns sam would like a turn at the control. Neither can acquire a new assignment by promising it.

V14 · A quiet minute

A neighbor hears the noise drop after a press stroke and offers company. The other wants to hear the drive come back cleanly.

ben Nice when it shuts up, isn’t it?

kit Hang on. I like the noise it makes next.

ben I’ll shut up as well then.

If heard. The initiator learns the neighbor listens to the restart. Silence leaves the station attended.

V15 · One word at work

Two neighboring workers have a breath between handoffs. One wants company and asks whether talk is welcome.

bob Can you work and talk?

mae Depends. Can you listen and lift?

bob Give me this end.

If heard. The initiator learns when this particular partner prefers to talk. A new piece can cut the reply short.

Walking out

W01 · The hard part

Only if the press stood idle the whole shift and lou witnessed the wait. Outside, max stops at the sill to shake a sore foot. lou has seen them stand over an idle press.

max I could do that shift sitting down.

lou You did the hard part standing up.

If heard. max learns lou noticed the lost shift. lou can choose to wait or the crowd may separate them.

W02 · Tomorrow’s tray

mae and sam put a last tray down square as the bell rings. Walking out together, mae wants it left alone overnight.

mae That one stays the way I put it.

sam I’ll tell the brain.

If heard. sam learns mae is proud of the set and wants it undisturbed. They have left the controls.

W03 · Three fingers

jo tried kit’s elbow trick and can actually open the hand after work. Walking beside kit, jo wants to acknowledge the help.

jo I owe you for three.

kit Keep ’em.

If heard. kit learns the exercise helped jo today. The degree of relief comes from jo’s hand or report.

W04 · Off the clock

ed falls into step with gus after a shift at the desks. He wants to know if anyone heard his end-of-day count. gus was on the rail.

ed Did anybody hear me say eight?

gus All eight of them. That’s why they left.

If heard. ed learns gus heard him, even if LIMEN walked past. He has gained an audience for tomorrow.

W05 · Which way home

bob has caught a neighbor at the outer door, still keen to talk after an uneventful shift. The neighbor usually turns the other way.

bob You go my way?

ben As far as the corner.

bob Good. I’ve got a corner’s worth.

If heard. bob learns how far they walk together. Their common route is a specific place to try again.

W06 · Hearing the bell

A worker at the far end of the press had to watch their neighbor move to know it was time. The neighbor heard the bell.

max Did the bell even ring?

lou I heard it.

max I saw you put your coat on. That’s my bell down there.

If heard. max learns which cue lou actually followed. Only lou can tell a later listener what the bell sounded like.

W07 · Practice

gus wants an encore of yesterday’s imitation. max needs to be out of LIMEN’s hearing before obliging.

gus Do the welcome.

max You did hear him this morning?

gus 😇 Yes. I’d like to enjoy it once.

If heard. max learns gus wants to hear the imitation for fun. gus gets an encore only if max actually does it. If LIMEN is still close, max only shakes their head.

W08 · Taking a post home

Two workers leave after doing different jobs. One sees the other continuing the motion of the shift with an empty hand.

jo What are you carrying?

mae Nothing.

jo Tell your arms.

If heard. jo learns mae still feels the lift on the walk home. No claim about an injury follows from it.

W09 · Count by ear

A neighbor remembers hearing a rack clang at the bell and wonders if the last piece made it. lou actually saw it stop short.

ben We got that last one, then?

lou That was me knocking the empty rack.

ben Sounded quite productive.

If heard. The questioner gains lou’s eyewitness correction. An unheard or unwitnessed finish leaves the tally open.

W10 · The late release

kit leaves next to a worker who actually spent the shift detained. kit saw the release and wants to know what the worker heard from there.

kit Could you hear the line from in there?

bob Heard the press. Two knocks, then you lot had a nice long rest.

kit Rest? We were pulling the tray out backwards.

bob Oh. Sounded lovely from in there.

If heard. kit learns the knocks reached custody. bob learns from kit what the silence cost the people at the press. The release itself is separately visible.

W11 · Switch hands

A neighbor carried the heavy end of a tray. The other worker wants to settle the favor on their shared way out.

sam My end tomorrow.

mae Which end is yours tomorrow?

sam Whichever one you had today.

If heard. The listener gains a personal promise about tomorrow. A changed placement may prevent it.

W12 · Going through again

gus has been reassigned from Security to the rack and is walking out with a worker who saw him there. They want to know what he’d pick tomorrow.

jo Desk again if he asks?

gus I’ve had my day in industry.

jo That was half a day, gus.

If heard. The listener gains gus’s own account of his preference, distinct from seeing him head for a desk.

W13 · Keeping pace

Two workers are actually walking the same way. One wants company but is a little slower.

ben Are you in a hurry?

sam I was till I got out.

ben Good. Walk with me a bit.

If heard. The neighbor learns the first worker wants to walk together. Slowing down makes a later meeting more likely.

The missing card

K01 · Line of sight

Either room or exit. bob asks a neighbor who stood behind him at the strike. jo remembers sam kneeling but the finder was out of view.

bob When mine hit the floor, could you see past sam?

jo Past sam? I could barely see sam. All I got was the back of the coat.

If heard. bob gains “jo saw sam and missed the pickup.” This narrows his list of witnesses. He learns nothing about where the card ended up.

K02 · One more slip

Either room or exit. bob was issued a pass and still has the expired slip. ben has seen him unfold it and wants to know whether it will work.

bob Another one of these and I’ll need a pocket for them.

ben Can’t you use that one?

bob Read the date.

If heard. ben learns from bob that the pass was for yesterday. The original is still missing.

K03 · Just the useful bit

Either room or exit. bob has an opening next to someone who heard ed enter a found card. The listener wants to help and has work competing for their hands.

bob Anybody turn one in?

sam Somebody found one.

sam ed’s desk.

If heard. After “Somebody found one” bob believes somebody found an unspecified card. Only “ed’s desk” points him to ed. If the opener alone lands, the neighbor learns bob is searching and bob gets no lead.

K04 · The desk off duty

Either room or exit. A neighbor who knows of the search has ed beside them, free of duty. ed personally picked up the card and wants credit for finding it.

sam Since I’ve got you away from that book. Did anybody turn in a card yesterday?

ed Nobody turned it in.

ed I picked it up myself.

If heard. The questioner learns from ed that he found a card. Ownership and present custody stay unconfirmed to them.

K05 · Two edges

Either room or exit. A neighbor knows a card is missing and asks gus because they saw him choose Security. gus actually saw two cards in ed’s pocket.

lou You two had the desks. Did any loose cards come your way?

gus I just saw two in ed’s pocket. Couldn’t see the faces.

If heard. The listener can report that gus saw two cards. They cannot identify either photograph. If “in ed’s pocket” never lands, the holder remains unnamed.

K06 · The clerk who called

Either room or exit. A neighbor asks gus about the missing card on the strength of his desk reputation alone. He remembers his own duty.

mae When somebody drops a card, is that your desk or his?

gus I got the people. ed got the bits of paper.

mae I’ll ask him, then.

If heard. The listener learns that gus kept the queue and ed kept the register. Neither role proves who picked up the card.

K07 · An honest miss

Either room or exit. A neighbor knows gus was near the original pickup on Day One. gus remembers his own blocked view that day. Today’s tray hiding ed’s pocket cannot supply that memory.

kit Did you see who got bob’s card?

gus I had ten pairs of feet and a bad angle. You want a pair of feet described?

If heard. The neighbor gains “gus says he didn’t see hands on the card.” His failure to see a pickup is no proof it never happened.

K08 · The boot

Either room or exit. bob reaches a worker he remembers at the tail of the Day One queue. They actually saw gus lift a boot.

bob You were at the back. Did a card come past your feet?

ben I saw gus lift his boot. Couldn’t tell you what for.

If heard. bob gains a reason to approach gus about the boot. The witness never saw a card under it.

K09 · The boot explained

Either room or exit. bob heard the boot report and finds gus. gus remembers lifting his foot for a reason unrelated to the card.

bob What was under your boot when I fell?

gus My other lace. I was standing on it.

bob Oh. I thought you’d got something of mine.

If heard. bob gains gus’s account of the boot and can abandon this lead without deciding who has the card.

K10 · Does the picture match

Either room or exit. bob heard a source name ed’s desk. Off duty or between operations, ed wants to deal with the question where he can show the entry.

bob ed. That loose card at your desk. Has it got my ugly picture?

ed Catch me before I open up. I’ll show you what I found.

If heard. bob gains a future meeting before checks. ed has promised a showing. bob still lacks the original.

K11 · Passed down

Either room or exit. bob meets a neighbor who personally heard ed say he picked up a card. They want to repay bob’s earlier confidence.

sam ed picked up a card. Told me himself.

bob Did he say it was mine?

sam Didn’t get that far.

If heard. bob learns this through the neighbor and can name them when he asks ed. They do not know which card it was.

K12 · How many, exactly

Either room or exit. bob meets someone who actually heard gus describe the two edges in ed’s pocket. They distinguish seeing from being told.

lou gus saw two cards in ed’s pocket.

bob Did gus see my picture?

lou Two edges, bob. That’s all he saw.

If heard. bob gains a lead to gus with the messenger named. Only what gus said about the edges may travel.

K13 · Did he show you

Either room or exit. bob has the edges report from a neighbor and can now ask gus himself. gus cares about being accused of rummaging.

bob gus, did ed actually show them to you?

gus He bent down. I could see into his pocket. I didn’t go through it.

If heard. bob gains gus’s direct account of seeing into the pocket. This still supplies no name on a card.

K14 · Show me the other one

Either room or exit. bob has a sourced lead and reaches ed off the desks. ed prefers a quiet return. Two real cards are within reach.

bob sam says you picked up a card. Is it mine?

ed I’d have given it back if you’d asked.

bob I’m asking, ed.

ed lets bob read the photograph. bob takes his own card when ed lets go.

If heard. ed acknowledges bob’s request. He has yet to identify a card or say who picked it up. K04 supplies an explicit pickup admission. Seeing the face gives bob recognition. An interruption here leaves the card with ed. Only ed’s release and bob’s receipt of his original end the search.

K15 · A lead from the other pocket

Either room or exit. bob heard gus’s two-card account, reaches ed and asks to compare them. ed is willing to show both.

bob gus saw two cards in your pocket. Can I see the other one?

ed You might have told him why you wanted to know.

bob I wanted to ask you myself.

ed holds the two pictures up. Whether he lets go depends on what happens next.

If heard. bob identifies his photograph only on actually seeing it. If there is time to look but no completed transfer, he can later say ed showed it to him.

K16 · A pass is a piece of news

Either room or exit. A neighbor saw bob walk through after a failed check and mistook the pass for his original. bob wants that corrected.

mae Somebody found it, then?

bob They gave me one till the bell. That’s when I start being me without papers again.

If heard. The neighbor now believes bob says he got a pass and is still looking. Seeing his passage alone never identified the document.

K17 · It came back

Either room or exit. A neighbor previously knew bob was looking. The transfer has since completed and bob can show his own original.

kit Did you ever find the thing?

bob 😮‍💨 Look at the face. Same miserable picture. I could kiss it.

If heard. The neighbor updates an old belief when bob tells or shows them. Everyone else may still think he is searching.

K18 · Don’t put a name on it

Either room or exit. Someone brings ed a report that gus found bob’s card. ed wants to correct the attribution without displaying the original.

ben Heard gus found it.

ed gus was looking at the queue. I was the one looking at the floor.

ben So you picked it up?

ed Yes.

If heard. The neighbor hears ed claim firsthand responsibility. They cannot truthfully say gus saw the pickup. If ed never heard the accusation this correction has no trigger.

K19 · The two desk regulars

Either room or exit. ed and gus are neighbors away from the checks. gus heard somebody ask about a loose card. ed wants his find attributed properly.

gus When they ask me about that loose one, what do you want me to say?

ed That I found it.

gus And then?

ed Then you send them to me.

If heard. gus can now tell a later neighbor he heard ed confirm a find. He has no reason to identify the original as bob’s unless ed says its owner's name or shows the face.
