## 1. Recover the actual successful collaboration inside the hearing example

**Location:** `02.json:176` (with source support in the retained Writer interview, Q2, lines 15–17).

The chapter's only compact production success is compressed into “some missing receptions were defects; others were intentional ignorance.” This states a good rule, but hides the people, the changed judgment and the avoided work. Much of the chapter consequently reads as a tour of game-runtime contracts, followed by generic advice for a studio. The owner’s subject is the efficiency of an agent team making creative work; this is the place to show that subject directly.

The retained account contains a more interesting story: Writer initially treated all sixteen absent receipts as failures, Director/Level distinguished four legitimate nonreceipts, and Writer withdrew unnecessary audience edits while retaining twelve staging problems. Give that correction two or three sentences. Explain the narrow productive gain: the shared account let the team change its interpretation and avoid “repairing” intentional ignorance while preserving real obligations. This supplies a successful cross-role case and human fallibility without claiming quantified savings or superiority to one agent. If the later regression is included, distinguish its twelve required receipts/four nonreceipts from the earlier review decision rather than presenting it as already completed in the frozen review.

## 2. Shorten the generated-characters detour and remove the repeated scope explanation

**Location:** `02.json:140–158`; compare Chapter 1 block 7 (`The agents built and examined the game…`).

Chapter 1 already establishes that the production agents built a deterministic artifact and that interesting characters do not establish an efficient production team. Chapter 2 repeats that whole distinction after its useful production-memory argument, with a separate heading, figure and two paragraphs. It pauses the chapter’s progression from authoritative state to attributed knowledge to delivered information and pulls the book back toward game-AI taxonomy.

Either remove this prose detour from the main sequence or reduce it to the one new inference the shared-world argument needs: a stated intention and a completed commitment remain different records, as the party attendance demonstrates. The historic runtime/cost qualification is responsible, but the cost aside and final Loopforge-versus-generated-characters explanation need not be restated here. This is a narrative-scope recommendation, not an assessment of the figure.
