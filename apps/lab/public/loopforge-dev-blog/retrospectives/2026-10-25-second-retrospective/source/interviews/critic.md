## Q2 — Which decision of your own worked poorly? Give one concrete instance, its consequence, what you knew then, and what you would change. Separate your responsibility from dependencies outside your control.

I missed two local drawing defects while keeping five named storyboard repairs: a record plate still cut into Lou's head, and an ordinary-production inset retained practice observers and a clipped note. Visual caught them. My narrow KEEP was accurately scoped, but my inspection should have noticed those adjacent problems. The consequence was another two-image correction and review. I should reserve a short visual check around every changed region before sealing a finding, including heads, labels and nearby insets. I do not need a new whole-packet audit or an unlimited polish round. The miss was mine; rendering access and correction scheduling were dependencies, not excuses for it.

## Q3 — Describe one cross-role exchange that improved the game and one that created avoidable delay or rework. What made the difference? Name the work and evidence, not just the participants.

The useful exchange joined Level's adopted forward-push, held-stop and reset sequence to Visual's corrected boards and my returned-pixel check. The avoidable rework was a version mismatch around that same change: my frozen-source review retained a low/high suggestion while the adopted Level instruction was arriving. I then needed an explicit authority addendum to prevent the old recommendation guiding the correction. The difference was whether the exact source and the visible result travelled together. I should have surfaced the unsettled action authority as a dependency earlier; Producer still owns adoption and routing.

## Q4 — What did actual integrated footage or play reveal that source checks or isolated craft work did not? If you have not inspected moving evidence, say so and distinguish still-frame judgment from motion judgment.

I inspected actual screenshots and sampled decoded frames, not continuous playback or audio. The integrated views exposed failures that a correct script or attractive board cannot reveal: a speech panel hid the face it was meant to identify; the tutorial could show a clear button while cropping bob; an End of shift label appeared during admissions; a departing worker kept his arms sideways across several positions; and facade geometry blocked the exit shot. The frames also showed a real strength: familiar people returned in work and departure rather than disappearing after their introduction. I cannot turn those samples into claims about impact weight, grip, fluid transitions or sustained performance. I requested exact playback segments for those remaining questions.

## Q5 — The first retrospective called for one owned outcome, decisive evidence and a change-of-approach decision after a failed assumption. What concrete behavior in this cycle followed or missed that lesson? What should good work in your domain mean now?

The strongest follow-through was returning to exact corrected evidence and allowing a bounded pass. The final two storyboard corrections were checked on their own image hashes, closed, and left alone. My current review also saved its first-pass judgment before reading author explanations. The limitation is that the required evidence indexes already named some concerns; I should not describe that inspection as blinded. A missed lesson would be treating more source receipts as a substitute for seeing the game. Good critique now means one recognizable encounter, the exact observed effect, a correction or justified keep, and the smallest return evidence that could prove the decision wrong. Reaching the ending is useful evidence; it is not evidence that every intervening minute works.

## Q6 — Choose one improvement you would keep for the next cycle. Who owns it, when should it happen, and what observable result would show it helped? Include any tradeoff or disagreement with the current production approach.

I would keep a short independent review at the first complete in-engine encounter, before a large packet is polished. Producer owns scheduling it; Engine supplies the exact build and normal-control capture; Critic records a first impression and separates observable defects from preference. The next correction should return that same encounter with its surrounding transitions. Success would be fewer defects surviving until the full-day review, while already closed findings stay closed. The tradeoff is that an early check interrupts a craft's flow and may inspect rough work. I would accept that cost for the face, handoff and next-action moments, but disagree with making every small asset wait for a separate critic approval.

## Q7 — Where did your independent critique materially change the result, and where was its scope or timing inadequate? How do you distinguish a useful bounded KEEP from whole-game approval?

My critique materially changed the later-day pictures where missing recipients, unseen attempts and mixed production/practice staging became visible, ordered actions. My timing and scope were inadequate for the two adjacent drawing defects I missed, and the static passes could never answer the integrated game's camera, clock, locomotion or cadence questions. The present Day 1 findings are recommendations awaiting corrected evidence; I cannot claim they have already changed the game. A useful KEEP names its build or image, its question and its limits. It can mean that a record plate now clears a head. It does not silently become approval of mobile play, motion, performance or the whole game. The owner's approval of the demonstrated slice is separate and remains intact.
