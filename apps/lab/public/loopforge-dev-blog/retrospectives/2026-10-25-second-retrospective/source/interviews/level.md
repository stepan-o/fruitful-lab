# Second studio interview — Level Designer

## Q1

**What is the most useful result you personally contributed this cycle? Describe the player/team outcome, what you actually inspected, and the evidence that could contradict your assessment.**

I made the connected rooms more than a set of camera backgrounds. The navigation work tied station positions, furniture reservations and doorway routes to the same movement checks used by the scripted day and individual actions. It also corrected two narrow passages without enlarging the room. Engine integrated that work, and the captured day now reaches three completed outputs and twelve departures.

I inspected the route data and source checks at the time; in this review I inspected the actual line, checkpoint and ending stills and the downloaded final state. The clearest player result is the Conveyor's four control pads beside one continuous aisle: the production order is understandable and the workers have a route between controls.

That assessment would weaken if ordered footage shows a body passing through furniture, a worker sliding along the route, or a necessary control hidden during its action. My clearance checks use root discs. They do not prove the limbs, blades or hands fit. I have not watched the recordings as continuous motion.

## Q2

**Which decision of your own worked poorly? Give one concrete instance, its consequence, what you knew then, and what you would change. Separate your responsibility from dependencies outside your control.**

I let source geometry and camera projection become too large a part of the delivery. The Conveyor camera work accumulated several proposals before we had the actual player view. In the captured Wash gate, Bob's lower body is outside the picture; at Forge, almost all of him is below it. A correct control coordinate cannot help the player read an action that the screen crops away.

I knew the earlier camera results were numerical and that imported visibility remained unverified. I recorded that limit, but I should have made the full worker, pedestal, workpiece and supervisor composition the first shared deliverable. Browser access and the changing interface rectangle were outside my ownership. Prioritizing more spatial source work before that evidence was available was my decision. I would now settle the actual picture area with Engine and Director before refining a camera list.

## Q3

**Describe one cross-role exchange that improved the game and one that created avoidable delay or rework. What made the difference? Name the work and evidence, not just the participants.**

The Systems–Level reconciliation produced a useful counterexample: Bob could remain physically at Belt while Sam worked Forge, even though the standing assignment belonged to someone else. An assignment count was therefore not a reliable occupancy count. I supplied that particular state, Systems preserved the legitimate paired-control exceptions, and Engine changed the public count to actual non-departed occupants. Its saved tests cover Bob at Belt, visitors and departures. That exchange had one question, one failing example and a concrete correction.

The camera exchange was less efficient. We moved from a south Conveyor proposal to a wider lens and then a north proposal, comparing projection and mount constraints without an actual selected-feed image. Those calculations helped reject bad options, but they still left the operating view unreviewed. The avoidable work was treating successive source proposals as the next main milestone. A shared actual viewport and named work-state capture would have made the tradeoffs visible sooner.

## Q4

**What did actual integrated footage or play reveal that source checks or isolated craft work did not? If you have not inspected moving evidence, say so and distinguish still-frame judgment from motion judgment.**

I inspected nineteen supplied game stills, not moving playback. They showed that dialogue can cover a supervisor's face, that the picture changes height between speaking and silent states, and that a readable machine can coexist with a cropped operator. The Security arch is a strong landmark, but the selected views explain the clerks' work less clearly. The night illustration reads well; on the desktop capture the result sits below the first screen, while the narrow ending presents the image, count and restart together.

The final export records twelve actors outside. The departure still I inspected shows the inner Security doorway, so I cannot turn that record into a claim that I watched everyone cross the exterior threshold. I requested ordered extracts with both video offsets and the factory clock. Even those frames would not equal continuous motion or measured performance.

The owner has now approved the demonstrated R10 slice. These findings preserve that approval while identifying technical corrections and optional improvements; they do not reopen the taste decision.

## Q5

**The first retrospective called for one owned outcome, decisive evidence and a change-of-approach decision after a failed assumption. What concrete behavior in this cycle followed or missed that lesson? What should good work in your domain mean now?**

The later Wash reconciliation followed it. The proposed teaching scene depended on a lift-and-turn action, but the implemented station had a pedestal and a forward-push-and-stop clip. I selected a forward stroke, held stop and withdrawal/reset, then supplied eight exact turn replacements and two matching staging changes. Producer adopted them without expanding the room or inventing a mechanism. The physical contact and timing limits remained open.

The earlier camera work missed part of the lesson: a carefully pinned proposal was useful, but it was not decisive evidence about the player's view. Good level work now means a usable route, a readable local action, and an honest account of what the rules allow there. It should also say when to keep the layout. Moving a wall to compensate for a dialogue panel would have solved the wrong problem.

## Q6

**Choose one improvement you would keep for the next cycle. Who owns it, when should it happen, and what observable result would show it helped? Include any tradeoff or disagreement with the current production approach.**

I would keep one shared local-action capture before the layout is frozen. Producer schedules it, Engine supplies the actual integrated view, Level and Director settle coverage, and Animator checks the action at the real target. It should include a reading hold, the action and its result, using the ordinary interface dimensions.

The observable result is simple: the worker, relevant control and consequence remain readable without moving the actor for the camera, and the next route is understandable. If the same subject disappears when a dialogue panel arrives, the test has found a useful defect early.

The tradeoff is doing less decorative or numerical refinement before seeing the whole action. I prefer that to another round of precise camera proposals without pixels. It need not become an approval ritual for every small adjustment; one representative difficult action can expose the shared framing problem.

## Q7

**Which actual room, route or operating position best supports play, and where did a source spatial proof fail to establish what the player can see or hear? How should Systems–Level agreement reach runtime?**

The Conveyor's control-side aisle is the strongest part of the actual room. Four aligned pads explain the sequence, and the space behind them supports movement and supervision. I would keep it. The current view still needs to make the support roles and Forge output easier to identify.

The limits differ for sight and hearing. A clear root route did not keep Bob inside the tutorial picture. In the later scenario work, Kit and Lou's posts were about 13.3 metres apart; one six-metre local speech radius cannot reach both from any common point. We selected a real local meeting and return, but its travel and readiness costs still need scheduling. A drawn meeting does not grant free time or delivered knowledge.

Systems should specify the allowed action and its consequences; Level should supply the actual post, route, receiver and local hearing conditions; Engine should enforce the agreed case through the common action path. Test a valid case and a concrete rejection, then view that same event in the room. Preserve the current four-control exceptions for paired work. Eight future required duties, receiver capacity and safe handover need explicit rules and integration before the design page calls them implemented.
