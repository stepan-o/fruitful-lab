# Sanctuary Economics — literature and narrative revision

1 October 2026. This pass replaces the short chapter summaries with a connected
essay across the same 21 stable routes. Three dedicated research/editorial
agents worked on history, design theory and transactions; the integrated draft
received a separate cross-chapter audit and an authorial edit.

## The argument

Begin with a returning player: possessions can survive while the occasion for
using them moves on. Compare the invitation to return across six games, then
separate progress in a character, an account and a person. Commercial history
shows that payment and play were being tuned together in cabinets and text
worlds long before contemporary shops. Concord introduces population as part
of a multiplayer product; the Outer Wilds prototype shows a test changing a
specific design decision.

The middle follows what people want from a session and what reward systems
actually change. SDT, Yee, Koster, MDA and Juul supply different lenses, without
being collapsed into a player taxonomy. INDIKA is a close reading of reference
images. The loot, calendar and encounter models let the reader examine the
argument directly.

The transaction chapters trace access, identity, time and power through actual
examples: Erdtree's entry requirements, avatar representation, Warframe's
acquisition routes and Diablo III's auction-house closure. CAD pack arithmetic
leads into catalog access and claim requirements, then into comprehension of
the complete offer. The final chapter returns to funduck and the difference
between recording behavior and understanding a worthwhile evening.

## Literature added or closely revisited

The source catalog in `apps/lab/lib/sanctuary/content.ts` contains titles, dates,
direct URLs and scope notes. The pass adds 18 entries and revisits existing
primary references. Particularly useful additions:

- **Bartle, MUD Advanced Project Report:** hourly charging versus a known
  payment for an access period; historical observations/proposals, not proof
  that every proposed tariff shipped.
- **Atari, Gauntlet operator manual (1985), pp. 2–3 and 3–4:** health per coin,
  earnings, value and repeat visits. The difficulty-first recommendation gives
  the historical chapter a concrete commercial design decision.
- **Mobius, Demaking Outer Wilds (2015), Separating the Signal from the Noise
  and The Intentionality of Wandering (2016):** notes, misleading spatial cues
  and pathing revisions. These are three distinct developer accounts.
- **Activision, The Role of Skill in Matchmaking (2024):** multiple matchmaking
  constraints. It illustrates the mechanism separately from Concord; it does
  not diagnose why Concord closed.
- **Riot, Scalability and Load Testing for VALORANT (2020):** the connected
  service work underneath a continuing player relationship.
- **Bandai Namco, Erdtree entry guide (2024):** Radahn and Mohg prerequisites.
  Ownership and readiness can be satisfied in either order in our exhibit.
- **Yee and Bailenson, The Proteus Effect (2007):** assigned avatars in short
  VR experiments. The narrative does not claim measured armor-purchase effects.
- **Grinding Gear Games, Trade Manifesto (2017):** a historical design argument
  about trade and upgrade journeys, rather than a controlled outcome study.
- **Raghubir and Srivastava, Monopoly Money (2008):** consumer payment-form
  experiments. No spending coefficient is transferred to Diablo.
- **Hsee et al., Medium Maximization (2003):** the cited ice-cream pilot uses
  questionnaire choices, not observed completion of the proposed tasks.
- **Luguri and Strahilevitz, Shining a Light on Dark Patterns (2021):**
  service-enrollment experiments, with effects varying by presentation.
- **Ballou et al. (2025):** play records and surveys from 703 casually engaged
  US adults. Observational life-fit associations and inconclusive equivalence
  tests are preserved, without claiming that playtime has no effect.
- **Microsoft experiment-method research (2009 and 2016):** causal inference
  and threats to interpretation over longer periods.

Official publisher sources also anchor selected product structures, seasonal
transfer, the 2025 Expedition 33 update, INDIKA's premise, 2024 Loot Reborn,
Halo's 2022 premium/free distinction and DRG's April 2024 proposal.

## Editorial and evidence boundaries

Facts carry paragraph citations. Original analysis follows the specific
example, while hypothetical scenes and simplified models are identified when
introduced. Chapter evidence notes retain the population, date and method
limits. Unnecessary repeated disclaimers were removed from the body.

No invented playthrough, current Diablo drop rate, Concord budget/sales total,
causal shutdown explanation or undisclosed production allocation was added.
Publisher screenshots remain confined to the local research archive; public
prose describes references without asking readers to inspect hidden images.
The English editorial edition and six-language navigation retain their
existing scope.

Source-derived wording was reviewed across manuscript, bibliography, captions
and model instructions. Repeated Reliquary summaries were condensed to keep
room for its retained diagram. The catalog-access rules remain explicitly
historical (April 2025). Original arithmetic and analytical examples are kept
separate from publisher documentation. There are no extended quotations.

`Chapter.paragraphCitations` maps zero-based paragraph indices to source IDs.

that citations and headings resolve and that links use the matching source.
