**Exact proposed provider brief · LF-MUS-PILOT-MENU-05**

Original instrumental golden-era rolling drum and bass for a factory Main menu. 164 BPM, 4/4, about 128 fast bars. Curiosity with a pulse, like powering up an unfamiliar terminal while the line is already moving. Close dry drums, a short firm kick, springy bass, a distinct pulse lead and a rounder reed-like reply voice. Start on low D with A–G–F–E in four sixteenths, landing on C♯ on beat two and holding it. Bring the complete hook back at bars 13, 29, 45, 65, 81, 101 and 117. Give the reply a singable C♯ pickup to D, followed by F and E. Use two deliberate endings. One settles back on D. The other opens up to A and steps down through G and F to E. Let the reed phrase breathe before the next lead entrance. Give each phrase a breath. Play close electric keys in a middle register, with chord attacks on beat one or three that ring across several beats. Move an inner voice between Dm9 and Dm6/9, visit G minor and B-flat major ninth and use a brief A-dominant turnaround. Anchor the kick on beat one and the “and” of three, with snare on two and four. At bar 49 give the drummer sixteen bars to play a rim call against low toms, with six isolated closed-hat marks across the whole passage. Keep the kick and backbeat steady. Build to eight quick 32nd notes on snare and rim at the end of bar 56, then open the space up again. On the last two beats of bar 64, send a sextuplet run down the toms and across the snare, landing on a short cymbal bell as the melody returns. At bar 97 let the bass answer alone for four bars. Bring the closing groove around to the opening downbeat. Instrumental throughout, with a clear groove and space around the lead. No ambient wash, stock break recordings, end fanfare or fade.

## A commercially usable whole product

The owner's requirement is that the complete Loopforge stack can be sold. MUSIC DIRECTOR owns audio-chain clearance and records any new music tool/runtime dependencies; PRODUCER reconciles the whole-product inventory with ENGINE and other crafts. “Free-to-sell” means supported commercial distribution, not necessarily free production, exclusivity or freedom from every continuing license obligation.

Maintain a release register with one entry per source/output/derivative and dependency: identifier, source URL and author/provider, acquisition date, exact license/terms version, evidence location, permitted platforms and distribution, modification/embedding rights, attribution, restrictions, expiration or revenue conditions, derivative parent IDs, reviewer and clearance status. Do not silently replace unknown with approved.

For human recordings and samples, check composition and recording rights separately where applicable. For code, inspect the actual dependency license and bundled example media; source-code permission does not clear sample audio. Do not use reference masters, ripped film dialogue, unlicensed breakbeats or dataset material with missing provenance. Do not repackage provider music as a public general-purpose sample library merely because it can be embedded in our game. Keep editable masters and internal production assets distinct from shipped game exports.

The proposed release gate is concrete: every shipped audio asset and proposed music dependency has a resolved commercial-use entry, required notices are included, restricted assets are excluded or replaced, and the sold build can run without a buyer having an ElevenLabs account or a live generation call. Track obligations that survive release. A Music Director review covers its audio scope, not an unsupported certification of every existing game asset.
