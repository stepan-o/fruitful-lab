## Media Strategy

The visual system for this article should make the central idea instantly understandable:

```txt
Marigold meaning changes by where the flower is placed.
```

The images should not feel like botanical research cards. They should feel warm, clear, sensory, and useful: gold petals becoming a path, a doorway, a garland, a grave, a pattern, or a table.

Primary visual motifs:

- ruffled orange and yellow marigold heads;
- petals arranged as path, threshold, altar, grave, garland, table, and wallpaper;
- gold against botanical green, candlelight, deep indigo, warm paper, or off-white;
- visible placement: on the ground, around a doorway, in a garland, in a vase, on a wall;
- respectful cempasuchil / Dia de Muertos references that center memory and welcome, not Halloween spectacle;
- calendula and cempasuchil distinction when the visual teaches plant-context clarity;
- William Morris-inspired pattern rhythm without copying museum objects.

The first media batch should support the article as a pillar. It needs one broad meaning pin, one distinctive cempasuchil/remembrance visual, one plant/context clarification asset, and one or two high-save quote or design/wedding assets.

## Editorial Guardrails

- Do not flatten Dia de Muertos into a spooky orange aesthetic.
- Do not show cempasuchil as generic Halloween decor.
- Do not imply every marigold is the same plant or carries the same meaning.
- Do not create a casual plant-identification guide.
- Do not show eating, brewing, tinctures, oil, supplements, harvesting, foraging, dosage, or medical use.
- Do not present magical or spiritual meaning as factual metaphysical proof.
- Quote graphics must be clearly Bloom Whispers original wisdom lines, not folklore, history, science, or source quotes.
- Use the approved V6 thesis and final H1; do not reintroduce the superseded V5 line.
- Keep type large enough for mobile Pinterest reading.

## Designer Notes

- Palette: marigold orange, saffron yellow, botanical green, candle cream, deep indigo, warm paper, restrained red-orange.
- Avoid an all-orange monotone palette; the visuals need green, dark contrast, and quiet negative space.
- Use real marigold texture wherever possible: ruffled petals, dense flower heads, garland repetition, scattered petals.
- Keep cultural visuals respectful, non-costume, and non-Halloween.
- Keep the first few assets readable and obvious on a phone screen.
- Let the article's poetry come from placement and light, not from over-decorated typography.
