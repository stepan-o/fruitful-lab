A studio carried by its pages

18 November 2026 · workflow established; individual game deliveries retain their own review status.

Loopforge now proceeds through dedicated specialist chats managed by its owner. The previous autonomous studio is closed. Its output remains available, but a new chat should start from the current brief, approved design and actual artifacts rather than reconstructing a long conversation or reviving an old coordination process.

The new Studio Briefs landing collects shared instructions and six initial roles: Writer, Level Designer, Art Director, 2D Artist, Engine Master and Game Systems Designer. Each role maintains its own brief and the design page that carries its work. New specialists can be added when commissioned.

The working sequence is study and pre-synthesis, interview, approved plan, build and verification, then manual owner review. The reference book, Tynan Sylvester's Designing Games, remains standard reading. Applied concepts must have verified location references and explain the decision they informed. This workflow entry does not claim a new book-derived design result.

The current parallel commissions are the live 2D Day 1 implementation and the Days 2–4 systems study. At the time of this entry, LEVEL was commissioned to reconcile Factory floor: scale & feel with the approved layout and 2D direction. Those are assignments, not completion claims.

What each new case study records

  • A concrete problem and the exact source/build that demonstrated it.
  • The decision, alternatives and any research concept actually applied, with citations.
  • What changed in the game, asset or design and what became reusable.
  • Comparable before/after evidence where available, preferably normal-speed game video for runtime changes. Label composites, rehearsals and diagrams separately.
  • The checks actually run, the owner's review status and the remaining limitations.
  • Links back to the responsible role brief and current design handoff.

Add dated entries here and link longer studies from their specialist pages. Preserve prior results as dated evidence; do not silently replace a failed result with a later success.
