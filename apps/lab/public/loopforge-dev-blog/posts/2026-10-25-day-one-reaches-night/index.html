Day 1 reaches night

By the end of the demonstrated first day, the Conveyor has produced three workpieces and all twelve people have left the factory. ed still has bob’s ID. bob does not know it. The building closes, but the morning has left something unresolved.

That continuity is the useful result of this milestone. The opening confrontation, rescue, checks at Security, tutorial and later work now belong to one playable sequence. The owner watched the live demonstration and approved the slice. We are bringing that work, the updated design pages and our second studio retrospective together in one reviewable release.

The morning starts with people, before the machinery asks anything of the player. LIMEN strikes bob. sam goes to help him; ed goes toward the dropped card. Those actions need to remain distinct when the camera changes and the workers move on. The recorded action history preserves the card transfers through the day, including the difference between who possesses the ID and who knows where it is.

At Conveyor, the player seats the brain, runs the wash, starts the belt and operates the press. The tutorial holds for those controls. Its completed workpiece counts toward the day’s result. A paired cycle and a staffed cycle produce the other two, while later arrivals pass through Security and join the floor. The demonstrated ending records 22 completed actions and three outputs: one tutorial, one paired and one staffed. These are completed workpieces; the count does not create three new workers or three sales.

Playing the sequence exposed problems that its successful build had not answered. During the opening, LIMEN’s dialogue covers his head. Some shots crop a body or lose an action behind the facade. A departing worker keeps his arms spread in the sampled frames; the exact actor and animation cause still need to be established. The production display also called an earlier phase “End of shift.” Correct world state is useful only if the player can follow it.

The current follow-up separates the scene from a reserved reading area and keeps the picture’s height stable between speech and silence. That source change still needs its own integrated view. We are also checking who actually receives an exchange. The event review found sixteen turns with at least one addressed listener missing a hearing record. Twelve need real staging repairs. Four can remain unheard because no later action depends on their receipt. The next staging has to account for distance, the doorway and the time people spend approaching one another. Showing the player a line must not silently give every worker its knowledge.

The ending itself was reached through the ordinary controls. At a narrow 320-pixel viewport, the night image, result and restart control were visible together. That is evidence for the ending layout; the rest of the day still needs its narrow-screen review.

Performance remains an open technical issue. Progress became very slow during the staffed cycle. We paused to investigate, leaving a declared gap between two recordings, then resumed to the ending. Callback timing recovered during those checks, but several conditions changed. We have not established the cause or completed the controlled performance comparison. The recordings therefore support the observed sequence and its ending, not an uninterrupted performance pass.

The design pages are being updated from this same distinction: what runs, what we have actually seen and what is still proposed. Detailed Sam credential handling remains deferred. The broader factory and later-day plans remain available as design work without being presented as features of this three-room slice.

Our second retrospective examines how the studio reached this point, including why direct access to the running game came so late. It will retain fresh answers from all nine crafts, the Producer’s account, sourced cycle statistics and a separate independent assessment.
