### Who makes the decisions

Producer assigns a bounded piece of work: its owner, exact inputs, permitted changes, expected artifact and review. Specialists work on isolated candidates. Engine Master prepares integration and build instructions; Writer handles voice and continuity; Animator handles movement and contact. Scene Director reviews framing and sequence, while Critic gives a separate judgment of the supplied work. Visual Artist, 3D Artist, Systems Designer and Level Designer cover their own parts of the game. Being hired does not mean a role has begun production.

There are two working contexts. Native Hive agents belong to this studio. A separate, owner-facing Codex chat supplied early integration and later desktop execution, browser captures and host operations. Its reports are identified as bridge evidence. It is not another native specialist, and its unlogged token usage is excluded from the native accounting.

When the studio took production ownership, a read-only canonical checkout still prevented Engine Master from writing there. The response was a private candidate preserving the existing pending files, followed by an explicit execution handoff. Ownership did not grant permission to discard earlier work or bypass the boundary.

### What counts as evidence

A handoff names the version reviewed and the limits of the result. Source checks can prove that lines match, files are intact or a motion is present in a delivery. Numerical checks can find a collision. Neither establishes whether a gesture reads naturally, a line reaches its listener or the player can understand a scene. Those questions need the actual scene and an appropriate review.

Critic receives the creative artifact before the author’s dedicated explanation. That order helped keep the opening review narrow: two dialogue changes were judged ready for text craft, while a stale version label still required correction. In the later story proposal, useful exchanges survived review while all four illustrated boards remained marked REVISE. A recommendation from Critic is not owner approval.

Heavy work is scheduled one process at a time, with at most two active specialists. A quiet performance test cannot share its machine with a build or motion sweep and still give a useful comparison. Work has time and token limits; partial results should survive a stop. The history also records where checkpoints were missed and this discipline failed.
