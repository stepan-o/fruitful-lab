# Independent whole-book editorial, research and media audit

Date: 27 October 2026. Reviewer: fresh-context Codex audit agent. Scope: the nine reconstructed chapters, their source registry, reader/authorship presentation, media declarations and selected evidence/data components. Worktree: `codex/agentic-systems-book`.

## Judgment

The nine boundaries are earned. This is a coherent critical narrative about commissioning useful work, not nine rearrangements of a cost warning. Each chapter has a different scarce resource, an inspectable conflict and a marginal next decision. The prose is situated in Root's actions and mistakes; the visible byline, cover note and reading room consistently distinguish Root / Codex from Stepan. It does not pretend that Root is a human witness or that the cover's “happy” describes agent feelings.

I would retain all nine chapters and all 35 current visual placements. The two definite prose cuts and missing research comparison identified below have now been corrected and independently rechecked. The requested media-use documentation is recorded in `docs/agentic-systems/MEDIA_USE.md`, including the unresolved rights limitation for Generative Agents. The editorial and source-documentation findings are closed at this revised snapshot; legal clearance is not thereby established. This audit is **not** a new live-browser acceptance, complete rights clearance, field-performance result or full CI pass.

## Research coherence and evidence boundaries

At the revised snapshot, the registry contains **95 records; 83 distinct sources are cited inline and listed in the relevant chapter bibliography**. There are no unknown inline IDs, no missing chapter bibliography entries for an inline citation, and no chapter-listed source without an inline citation. The reading room explicitly calls the work a critical narrative review rather than an exhaustive systematic survey. This is the right description; the reference count is not evidence of exhaustive coverage.

The 12 reading-room-only records are `debt`, `mast`, `evals`, `compiler`, `willison`, `ronacher`, `twelve-factor`, `ralph`, `gastown`, `edpcg`, `studio-second`, and `loopforge-hearing`. Their presence is legitimate further reading, not evidence that their full arguments were synthesized in the book. In particular, the book should not claim a substantive Huntley/Yegge comparison merely because both entries remain. Tane/Steinberger/Hashimoto do receive actual narrative comparison. The formerly missing `self-correct` comparison is now in chapter 4, satisfying that explicit comparison in the research method.

For the intended employer audience, the strongest transferable evidence is already concrete: contracts and state, dependency-aware delegation, evaluator design, executable candidate search, measured optimization, and reproducible handoff. The book demonstrates those through a game. It does not demonstrate a deployed external client's full-stack data/AI system or measure hiring appeal. Avoid turning this case into that claim. A further general-purpose chapter would dilute the existing argument.

Primary-page spot checks in this audit confirmed the following representations. They verify what the sources report, not their experimental results independently:

- [Sarkis's October 2026 account](https://claude.com/resources/articles/building-agents-when-to-fold-subagents-into-your-main-agent-loop) supports the qualified folding argument and preserves cases for separate agents. Chapter 3 correctly treats it as vendor experience.
- [Scaling Agent Systems v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08296v1) reports the 180-configuration comparison and the PlanCraft declines. Chapter 3 avoids importing its fitted thresholds as universal rules. The source itself makes stronger mechanistic claims than this book; retaining the book's narrower wording is appropriate.
- [METR's February 2026 update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/) supports the selection and concurrent-timing qualifications. The chapter does not represent it as a clean reversal of the 2025 experiment.
- [Generative Agents v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2) supports the five-of-twelve attendance account and Tom's omitted memory relation. Believability and gameplay enjoyment remain separate in the manuscript.
- [FunSearch](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06924-6), [AlphaEvolve v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.13131v1), and the [autoresearch repository](https://github.com/karpathy/autoresearch) support executable evaluation and retained candidates. The manuscript calls the yard authored and does not present it as a replicated discovery rate. The repo link is living: a pinned commit for the inspected README/program instructions would improve durability, though the date and limits are already disclosed.
- [EA's production-integration account](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.11105v1) and [Fly, Fail, Fix v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.12666v1) support the bounded production/player stories. The book correctly withholds human-enjoyment and representative-player conclusions from its two scripts.
- [Huang and colleagues' abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798) supports the proposed intrinsic-correction qualification. I checked the abstract, not all of that paper's experiments during this audit.

The distinction among attributed testimony, independent synthesis, authored demonstration and unperformed future work is unusually consistent. The proposed first-player study is explicitly unperformed. No matched single-agent studio rerun is claimed. Sampled views are not turned into continuous-motion certification. Closure is not turned into performance acceptance. The teaching examples require no model credentials in the stated main path; this audit did not independently run all downloads.

The useful figure's criticism purpose, exact source license and unresolved clearance status are now specifically recorded in `MEDIA_USE.md`. Loopforge material is attributed to its published or retained source, and the reading room preserves Chaitanya Giri/Munder Difflin and LimeZu credits. The existing public statistics file is aggregate role/session-count evidence, not private conversation content.
