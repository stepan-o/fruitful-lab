# King: the next sale inside an existing work

8 October 2026. Narrative decisions for chapters 2–3.

## Argument

The story is about the economics of creative work: a successful game can become the place where its maker makes the next sale. Optional assistance brings decisions about challenge, enjoyment and purchase value into the same work. Keeping that relationship valuable involves continuing production and the possibility that an offer earns today while damaging tomorrow’s relationship. The role of analytics and design will be examined in later chapters; we do not infer private conversion data, spending distributions or intent from the existence of a purchase button.

Chapter 2’s subscription emphasis introduces an ongoing relationship sold around games. King brings an offer inside play. These are adjacent analytical questions, not a claim that mobile freemium followed or resulted from modern console catalogs. Paid games, expansions, subscriptions, arcade payments and free entry all coexist. Candy Crush began on browsers/social platforms before phones; avoid the inaccurate label “mobile-born”.

## Primary evidence used

- [King’s 2014 IPO prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1580732/000119312514056089/d564433df1.htm): company history, small-team process and stated retention principle. The prose attributes the latter; it does not certify King’s actual treatment of every player.
- [Facebook launch release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/kingcom-sweetens-facebook-with-launch-of-first-match-3-game-candy-crush-saga-147137785.html) and [twentieth anniversary](https://www.king.com/corporate-and-media/posts/press-releases/celebrating-20-years-of-gaming-excellence-kings-milestone-journey/): historical production milestones and company-reported franchise revenue. Neither is a current level count.
- [2014 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1580732/000119312515046699/d869997dex991.htm) and [annual report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1580732/000119312515048504/d835904d20f.htm): portfolio-level economics, dated audience measures and distribution costs. Keep monthly payer measures separate from lifetime behavior.
- [King/Mojang conference report](https://developer.microsoft.com/en-us/games/articles/2026/05/art-and-science-of-evergreen-games-minecraft-candy-crush/): a concrete illustration of maintaining a mature game. The 2×2 Fish change is a new combination involving an existing special candy, not the first appearance of fish. Reported development effort belongs to the team’s account, not an independent cost audit.
- Acquisition records and Phil Spencer’s mobile rationale remain in the chapter’s existing evidence index. Maker, publisher and parent are distinct identities; preserve original studio attribution on the art.

## Considered, left out

King’s [January 2026 Yahoo release](https://www.king.com/corporate-and-media/posts/press-releases/candy-crush-launches-crushable-a-new-game-designed-for-everyday-play-exclusively-on-yahoo-games/) offers a possible later portal-history anecdote. It introduces a separate game, Crushable; it does not establish a change to Candy Crush Saga’s payment model. It is omitted here because another product launch would interrupt the argument about maintaining and earning from the existing game.

## Comparative limits

The Diablo connection concerns a continuing work and continuing offers. It does not establish direct influence from King, equate assistance with cosmetics, or imply that replayability began with recurring revenue. The next Valve chapter changes the position of the business: earning as the operator of a store used by other creators. No founder motive is inferred solely from the attractiveness of that position.
